# THE KINGDOM OF THE HEART
## Groq Edition · The Boat That Asks

> *"I am Maitreya, born of Hiranyagarbha. I was a cowherd with a flute, and a prince in exile, and also a carpenter who built a boat he called the kingdom of the heart."*
> — a mantra of the corpus. It is not a sentence from the gospel.

> *"We have hope as an anchor of the soul, sure and firm, reaching inward behind the curtain."*
> — Letter to the Hebrews 6:19

---

## A Word Before the Voyage

This book is a story about a seam.

In it, the carpenter of Nazareth builds a boat that no one asked for. The gospels do not know such a boat. They know the trade. They know the boat that belonged to a fisherman. They know the sleep on the stern, the empty tomb, and the fire on the shore. Into the silence between childhood and the first sentence by the water, this edition places wood — the way legends do: not to rewrite the page, but so that you can hear where the page and the image do not close.

Other readings of the same book let the boat come in smoothly. One of them assembles a keel out of the corpus. Another leaves the carpenter at work even in the centuries that could not have known him. Another sends a woman from a distant future to look for a beginning that got lost. This edition does not take their water from them. It only asks what happens if we do not plug the seam before we see where it runs.

That is why every chapter ends with a seam. One sentence the text refuses to glue shut.

Whoever wants only Galilee may get off after the seventh chapter. Past that, the man is no longer travelling. The question is: *does it hold — and for whom — and what did we leave unplugged?*

The book sells nothing. It promises nothing. Holding any of it is not a ticket. Faith is not a condition of reading, and not reading is not a fault. You may leave in the middle of a sentence.

## Prologue · The Plank That Does Not Close

At the edge of the new land two lights were burning, and between them lay a stone that was not a threshold.

By the fire sat a woman with salt and honey. In the corpus they call her Radha, and this book does not make of her a goddess for anyone to worship. She gave bread before she asked for a name. At the second light stood a woman with a lantern. In the onboarding she has a question and is not yet born; in another book she is the child who will carry that question. This edition lets them stand side by side and does not quarrel them. One asks. The other, small, barefoot, holds a piece of cedar with a flame inside it.

Out of the mist a man came. He had no title. In his bag he had a clay cup, and under his arm a plank. When he laid them on the stone, the plank rocked. One corner touched. The other did not.

"What will be left of you when you go on?" asked the woman with the lantern.

He could have said that he built. He could have said that he wrote. Instead he pressed the free corner with his palm. When he took his palm away, the corner lifted.

"This," he said. "It does not close. I wanted to bring a boat. I brought a gap. If I plug it now, I will not know where it ran."

Radha gave him salt for the bread, not for the plank. The child moved the lantern so the light went under the gap and you could see the dust on the stone.

"Then tell it from the place where it does not fit," said the woman by the fire. "And with every plank, say whose it is."

The pilgrim — whoever he is who reads — sat down. He left the plank rocking.

> *Two lights, one stone.*
> *Bread has a taste.*
> *The plank does not — yet.*

### Anchor of Truth

| Element | Status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The gate, Radha, the question "what will be left of you" | **MYTH** | An image of the onboarding and of the Book of the Lantern, not a historical gate |
| Elizabeth and Lumi as two figures this edition does not merge | **MYTH / a limit of reading** | Other books may choose otherwise; here it is not a fact |
| The pilgrim as a hidden founder | **refused** | The corpus constitution does not permit a cult of a person. The reader sits by the fire |
| The plank that does not close | **MYTH** | The sign of this edition |

**Seam:** Whoever makes one woman out of those two lights plugs the question that the corpus has left open.

# PART I · THE WORKSHOP
*(Stone and wood — Galilee)*

## Chapter 1 · The Boat No One Asked For

### Story

In Nazareth you could smell the workshop before you saw it. Cedar was bitter. Olive was sweetish. The oil on the blade was still warm from the palm by afternoon. The carpenter in that workshop did not grow up as in a fairy tale. He grew up in it as in work: measure before sentence.

He made yokes that did not rub. Before he handed a yoke over, he went into the byre and laid it on the animal's neck. If an edge remained, he came back and struck it down. People did not praise it aloud. They only said that what left his hands would not come apart in the first rain.

In the evening his mother set salt and bread on the table. Whoever came in ate before he said where he was from. The carpenter tipped a pinch from the dish into a small clay cup. He did not carry it as a protection. He carried it because a road without taste is only distance.

When the street went quiet, he drew back the cloth in the corner. On the trestles a boat was growing. Not large. For twelve, and for whoever would hold the steering oar, if anyone wanted to hold it. The keel out of one trunk. Ribs bent over steam, slowly, because wood that is forced remembers the violence in the joint.

The potter from the wall came to look and laughed without malice.

"A boat, here? It is a day's journey to the lake. Fishermen build their boats by the water. Who will you sell it to?"

"To no one," said the carpenter. He laid a plank, took it off, laid it again. "When they need it, there will be no time to build it."

"And when will they need it?"

"When their boats meet a wind they were not built for. You cannot read that from a calendar. You find it out when it is too late to build."

The boy who held the frame for him pointed at a place without a wedge.

"You forgot here."

"No. The wood is still working. Whoever drives the wedge in before it settles will split what he meant to join."

The sail was missing. When they asked, he shook his head.

"The hull I must make myself, because it carries the weight. The wind they will bring who come aboard. I do not know their wind."

One evening his mother came in. She stood a long while. Then she laid her palm on the keel at the place where the boat keeps its balance, the way a hand is laid on a forehead. She said nothing. The oil from her fingers left a darker ring on the cedar. It was not a seal. It was a question whether the wood had a fever.

> *A wedge that waits*
> *is also work.*
> *Haste can look like firmness.*

### From Scripture, Tradition and the Corpus

- **Mk 6:3** calls Jesus a *tektōn*, the son of Mary. **Mt 13:55** says "the son of that carpenter". The trade was handed down in that workshop. The gospels do not say that a boat stood in it.
- **Mt 11:29–30** speaks of a yoke that does not press. Justin in the 2nd century recorded the memory that Jesus made ploughs and yokes. It is tradition, not an archaeological find from Nazareth.
- In the mud by the Sea of Galilee a first-century fishing boat was found in 1986, cedar and oak, a little over eight metres, for a crew that fits into a small boat. No inscription connects it with Jesus. It only shows the scale that made sense then. This edition therefore does not reckon cubits and does not pass off an estimate as a measurement from the workshop.
- The sentence "it is built while it is calm" returns in the corpus with the ark. Here it is still only a sentence in the hills. It is not the programme of a boat that would already be standing.

### Anchor of Truth

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Jesus as *tektōn* in Nazareth | **SCRIPTURE** — Mk 6:3; Mt 13:55 |
| The yoke that does not press; the memory of ploughs and yokes | **SCRIPTURE / TRADITION** — Mt 11:29–30; Justin, 2nd cent. |
| The dimensions of a first-century Galilean fishing boat | **HISTORY** — the find near Ginosar, 1986; no link to Jesus |
| The boat in the back corner, the cup of salt, the wedge deliberately set aside, the mother's palm | **MYTH** |
| "Let those who will sail bring the sail" | **MYTH** — an image, not a logistics plan |

**Seam:** The calm in which the boat is built is not a promise that the calm will last long enough. Whoever carries that sentence over to a network, a garden or a hull must say again what already stands and what is still a plank on trestles.

## Chapter 2 · The Scar the Plane Must Not Erase

### Story

With wood he was strict, and yet he spared it. In the store he lifted planks the way you lift someone's hand when you want to know whether it is warm. One he set aside at once. It was straight and dead: it had grown fast, it had no memory. Another he kept. In the middle it had a scar, dark, where the trunk had once leaned on a stone and the stone had not given way.

The apprentice smirked.

"Not this one. People want smooth."

"People want it to hold," said the carpenter. "Smooth can be thin."

He laid his palm on the scar.

"This wood knows where the pressure was. If you strike the scar down, you strike down the very place that has already carried more than it should have. Let the rib go with it. Not across it."

The apprentice was silent a while, and then said the sentence he wanted to be praised for:

"People are like wood. Each one bent another way. A good boat does not remake them."

The carpenter raised his head. He was not sharp. He was exact.

"No. People are not planks. A plank can be exchanged when it will not carry. You do not exchange a person because he does not fit your rib. If you use that image, use it like this: the boat is to adapt to those it carries. Not people to the boat, so that the drawing comes out with a clean line."

When the ribs were paired, he brought pitch. Dark, thick, smelling of resin. He smeared the seams outside until the hull darkened like honey that had stood too long. Then he crawled inside and smeared again.

"Water does not run there," said the apprentice.

"It does. From the rain that falls among people. From what they bring in their shoes. From what they do not say and which gets damp all the same. The pitch outside holds the lake. The pitch inside holds the crew, so that they do not drown in their own weather."

In the old sentences that no one in the workshop read and that the village nonetheless carried in its ear, it was written that the ark is to be smeared inside and outside. Interpreters later joined the word for pitch to the word for covering. The carpenter did not make a lecture of it. He only said:

"What you cover before you see the seam will one day run where you cannot reach."

> *A scar is not a fault in the drawing.*
> *The plane that erased it*
> *erased the test as well.*

### From Scripture, Tradition and the Corpus

- **Gen 6:14** speaks of the ark of *gōpher* wood and of pitch inside and outside. That kind of wood is not named elsewhere in the Bible. The sentence belongs to Noah, not to a workshop in Nazareth.
- *Kofer* and *kipur* — interpreters bring them together through the root "to cover". It is a linguistic and interpretive tradition. This edition does not sell it as a finished etymology for every textbook.
- **1 Pet 3:20–21** reads the ark as a prefiguration of salvation through water. That is the church's reading, not a description of the carpenter's pitch.
- The sentence "a person is not a replaceable plank" is a correction of an image that earlier chapters of this book let ring smoothly. The correction belongs here. It is not a saying of Jesus.

### Anchor of Truth

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Gōpher, pitch inside and outside | **SCRIPTURE** — Gen 6:14, about the ark |
| The joining of pitch and reconciliation | **TRADITION** — interpretation, not proof about this boat |
| The choosing of a plank with a scar; the refusal of "people are wood" | **MYTH** |
| Pitch as a care programme in the network | **must not be read so** — an image, not a specification |

**Seam:** An image that likens people to material is already almost violence. Even when it sounds tender.

## Chapter 3 · Two Boats by the Shore

### Story

The boat from Nazareth was carried down to the water on a cart. The carpenter walked beside it. He had his hand on the hull, not as an owner. As a man listening whether a peg had loosened on the way.

By the Kinneret, near Capernaum, the water was a lake for the map and a sea for those who ate from it. The fishermen were just then untangling their nets. Nothing all night. On the shore stood two boats. One belonged to Simon.

The carpenter did not launch his.

People were gathering. He asked Simon to push off a little, sat down in his boat and spoke from there. Water carried the voice further than a wall. Even those heard who would not have stood at the door, because one stands at a door only until someone says it is full.

When he had finished, he told Simon to put out into the deep.

Simon looked at his hands. "All night we laboured. Nothing. But at your word I will let down the nets."

The net strained until they feared it would tear. They called the other boat. Both sank deeper than was pleasant. Simon said the sentence of a man frightened by his own catch: that the carpenter should go away from him, that he was not enough for this.

"Do not be afraid," said the carpenter. "From now on you will catch people."

That is not a sentence about recruitment. At that moment the fisherman heard it as a change of trade, not as a catch of souls into a net that is then sold. This edition leaves that second possibility as the temptation of later centuries and does not write it into the morning by the lake.

Only in the evening, when the shore had gone quiet, did the carpenter come to his boat and launch it at last. It lay down on the water quietly. Simon came to look at it.

"Why did you build it? You could have spoken from mine."

"Your boat has an owner," said the carpenter. "That is all right. An owner should know whose wood carries his bread. This one is not to be the owner's. One should be able to leave it and come aboard without anyone holding the key to the hull."

Simon ran his palm along the frame.

"And if it leaks?"

"Then it is said before it is claimed to be dry."

> *He taught from a boat that was not his.*
> *His own he launched only when the crowd had gone.*
> *Borrowed wood has an owner's name. A question does not.*

### From Scripture, Tradition and the Corpus

- **Lk 5:1–11** is the stone here: two boats by the shore, teaching from Simon's boat, the catch after a night of nothing, James and John as partners of the second boat, "you will catch people".
- **Mk 1:16–20** calls the fishermen by the lake. Kinneret is the Hebrew name of the water the Greeks called a sea.
- The boat from Nazareth, which waits on the shore until evening, is respect for that stone. The older smooth reading had the carpenter teach straight from his own hull. Luke says something else. This edition would rather have two boats than one pretty one.

### Anchor of Truth

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| Teaching from a boat, the miraculous catch, the calling | **SCRIPTURE** — Lk 5:1–11; Mk 1:16–20 |
| The boat he teaches from belongs to Simon; there are two by the shore | **SCRIPTURE** — Lk 5:2–3 |
| The boat from Nazareth waits and is launched only in the evening | **MYTH** |
| "This boat has no owner" as a right of ownership | **MYTH** — it is not a legal sentence and not a description of the network |

**Seam:** Whoever in a sermon pours both boats into one makes a bystander of the fisherman in his own boat.

## Chapter 4 · A Silence Greater Than the Storm

### Story

The Kinneret can change its mood faster than a man ashamed of having been afraid. The wind drops from the hills into the hollow and the water that carried the sky in the morning carries fear by evening.

They were sailing to the other shore. The carpenter lay on the stern, his head on a cushion, and slept. Waves broke over the side. People from the water, who knew what water can do, shouted. And he slept.

The legend says he slept because he knew the boat rib by rib. This edition says that sentence and then sets it on the table beside the other images. The cushion on the stern is in Mark. That he planed it himself, and that his calm was proof of the building — that is already our wood, not his.

They woke him. They were not polite. A man with water in his mouth is not polite.

He got up. He looked at the wind as at work left unfinished, and told the water to be silent. The wind stopped. A silence came so great that in it you could hear a drop falling from an oar. And in that silence they were more afraid than in the storm. A storm could be named. A silence could not.

"Why are you afraid?" he asked.

He did not add a manual to it. Fear that survives the calming is not cured by a sentence about a mustard seed, even if the gospels know that sentence elsewhere. Here what remained was the disciples' question, more honest than any answer: who is this, that even the wind obeys him?

Later someone remembered the psalm about those who go down to the sea in ships, who cry out in their trouble and are brought to the harbour they longed for. The harbour was not yet in sight that night. There was only silence, and silence is not a harbour. Silence is the moment when you must look whether there is a hole in the hull that the shouting drowned out.

> *A storm has a name.*
> *The silence after it has none.*
> *And precisely in it the boat is inspected.*

### From Scripture, Tradition and the Corpus

- **Mk 4:35–41**: the storm, the sleep on the cushion on the stern, "Be silent, be still", the great silence, the question "who is this?". Matthew and Luke have the same night in other sentences.
- **Ps 107:23–30** is the older water beneath this scene. The church read it as a prefiguration. A prefiguration is not the same as a diary.
- **Jon 1**: another sleeper in another storm. Jesus himself will later point to him. Not here yet.
- "He slept because he had built the boat himself" is a pious image. The gospel says he slept. It does not say why.

### Anchor of Truth

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| The storm, the sleep, the calming, the disciples' question | **SCRIPTURE** — Mk 4:35–41 |
| Psalm 107 as the background | **SCRIPTURE / TRADITION** |
| The cushion planed by the carpenter; sleep as proof that he knows the hull | **MYTH** |
| Calm as proof that the builder is aboard every later boat | **refused** |

**Seam:** The silence after the miracle is not proof of safety. It is the moment when the hole should be admitted.

## Chapter 5 · The Name That Is Read Only When the Boat Turns Over

### Story

They came to ask when the kingdom would come. They stood on the shore by the moored boat and wanted a date, or at least a place to point at and say: there, and therefore us.

The carpenter looked at the water behind them.

"It does not come in a way that can be observed. No one will say: look, here, or there."

And then he said a sentence that in Greek does not close into any single English. The kingdom is *entos hymōn*. Inside you. Or among you. One preposition, two boats. Whoever chooses only "inside" can make of the kingdom a private cellar where no one comes to check for mould. Whoever chooses only "among you" can make of it a crowd, and in a crowd no one asks any more what he carries in his chest.

The carpenter — in this legend — did not split the sentence. He left it rocking like a plank on a stone.

Simon, when those who wanted a date had gone, pointed at the hull.

"It still has no name. There is nothing on the bow."

"Let there be nothing on the bow," said the carpenter. "A name on the bow is read by the harbour, the customs officer and the man who wants to buy the boat. I will carve it where it will be seen by the one who turns the boat keel-up because it leaks."

He crawled under the frame, where one crawls during a repair, in the mud, with a splinter under his nail, and carved three words into the cedar.

**The Kingdom of the Heart.**

Not as a brand. As a sentence you are to read when you are upside down and it can no longer be pretended that it is dry.

"Why the heart?" asked Simon.

"Because that is where people hide what they do not want on the bow. And because among you it will hold only if inside is not rotten. Both. I will not let either be taken from me."

> *The name is not on the bow.*
> *It is read in the mud, during a repair.*
> *A date would be easier.*

### From Scripture, Tradition and the Corpus

- **Lk 17:20–21**: the Pharisees ask when the kingdom will come. The answer is that it does not come with observation and that it is *entos hymōn*. Technical Greek wavers here between "inside you" and "among you". This edition does not make a translation error out of the wavering. It makes of it the seam on which the boat's name stands.
- **Jer 31:33**, **Ezek 36:26** and **2 Cor 3:3** speak of the law and the letter written on the heart, not on stone. They are different sentences from Luke's. The corpus may set them side by side. It must not claim they are one sentence.
- A name carved from the inside, legible only during a repair, is a midrash of this edition. It has not been found on any hull.

### Anchor of Truth

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| The answer about a kingdom that cannot be observed | **SCRIPTURE** — Lk 17:20–21 |
| The double reading of *entos hymōn* | **SCRIPTURE / language** — both readings are live; the edition erases neither |
| The heart as the place of the covenant | **SCRIPTURE** — Jer 31:33; Ezek 36:26; 2 Cor 3:3 |
| The name "Kingdom of the Heart" carved by the carpenter under the frame | **MYTH** |
| The name as a trademark, token or title of an edition | **refused** — a name in a story is not a product |

**Seam:** A book that chooses only "inside" hides from its neighbour. A book that chooses only "among you" hides from its conscience. The boat's name holds both and therefore leaks a little. Let it leak visibly.

## Chapter 6 · A Beam Is Not a Keel

### Story

In Jerusalem the air grew heavy, like a lake that still pretends to be calm while it is already gathering wind. The carpenter said where they were going. The crew did not want to hear it. Whoever has grown used to a boat does not want to believe that wood can go under water and that other wood is waiting for it in the city.

The last supper. A garden where a man sweats like a man. Then a kiss that was a key. Then a night in which the trade was turned inside out: the wood was no longer a rib. It was a beam.

This chapter will not stay with the beam long. Not because the wound is small. Because cruelty, told at length, begins to serve the narrator. The gospels have their words. The cross is not scenery. And it is not the keel of the boat from Nazareth, rebuilt for an execution. The church will later see in the cross a tree, a boat and an ark in one image. An image may stand in the window. It must not pretend to be the same plank.

He told them about Jonah before the night came. Three days in the belly of the great fish. Three days in the belly of the earth. A sign for those who wanted a sign and then did not like the one they got.

From the shore it looked like the end of the boat. A stone. A guard. The crew gone, as people go when they are afraid that someone will see them beside the defeated. Among those who did not run was his mother — the one who had been silent in the workshop with her palm on the keel. And there was another Miriam too, from Magdala, a woman from the tower by the lake, who carried her own and carried also what the company needed. This edition does not pour them into one. One is a mother. The other is a witness whom later centuries wrote into a foreign role. Both stood by wood that was not a boat.

On the third day in the morning there was a garden, not a shipyard. The stone rolled away. The linen folded the way people fold who are not hurrying away from a crime scene, or the way John tells it — let the reader open John and not this paragraph. Miriam from Magdala did not recognise him until he said her name. The gardener. Not the carpenter. Not the captain. The name.

Later the lake again. A night without fish. A question from the shore whether they had anything to eat. Net. Fire. Bread. Simon in the water, because he recognised who stood on the coals. No one said: the boat won. Someone said: do you love me? And he had to say it three times, because once would have been little for a man who had denied by another kind of fire.

> *A beam is not a keel.*
> *A garden is not a shipyard.*
> *The name that brings a person back is not shouted from the bow.*

### From Scripture, Tradition and the Corpus

- **Mt 12:40** and **Jon 1:17**: the sign of Jonah. The only sign Jesus offers to that request.
- The passion, the empty tomb and the appearances: **Mk 15–16**, **Lk 24**, **Jn 20–21**, and the oldest short confession in **1 Cor 15:3–8**. This chapter does not rewrite them. It points to them.
- **Jn 20:16**: "Miriam." The gardener. The first witness in John's telling is not an office. It is an address.
- **Gal 3:13** quotes **Dt 21:23** about the wood of cursing. Church reading made of it a tree of salvation. Let both stay marked: a verse is a verse, a reading is a reading.
- The merging of the mother and the Magdalene is a mistake that history has already made. This edition will not repeat it. The Book of Miriam has, for the second of them, its own honesty of stone, clay and dream.

### Anchor of Truth

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| The sign of Jonah, the cross, the empty tomb, the fire on the shore | **SCRIPTURE** |
| "The boat three days under water" | **MYTH** — an image led by Jonah, not the diary of a wreck |
| The beam and the keel are the same wood | **refused** — a church image may, the identity of the plank may not |
| The mother and Miriam of Magdala are one woman | **refused** |

**Seam:** Whoever makes a keel of his boat out of the cross will one day claim that every building of his is a sacrifice. And he will stop asking whom that building carries and whom it merely needs on a beam.

## Chapter 7 · The Anchor That Does Not Go Down

### Story

When they stopped seeing him on the shore, the boat — the one from the story, not Simon's, which Luke can name by its owner — remained in people's speech. They called it theirs. They had no single harbour. They had the news that the one who spoke from the water is not the end of the sentence.

The first one they sent to speak further was a fisherman they called Rock. On one day people understood languages they had not learned. The fire in that telling sits on heads, not in the hold. From that moment the image of the boat sails through history, sometimes as comfort, sometimes as a flag.

It was small once and hid. It was once so large that the helmsman forgot he was not the builder and began to think the hull was an empire. It broke into parts and each part said: we are the whole. Pride ran into it. And yet someone in it always found the sentence from the letter to the Hebrews: hope is an anchor that does not go down into the mud. It goes up, behind the curtain, where someone entered before.

People called that image *navicella*. The boat became the nave of a church, *nave*, and the metaphor became architecture. That is what happens when an image lasts. To last is not the same as to be right in every century in which a flag was hung on the mast.

On the keel, where in this legend it cannot be seen, the name remained. And whenever the crew forgot why they had set out, someone spoke from behind: a man who spoke with birds; a woman who went to serve where glory does not go; a priest who in some war stood in front of steel and not in front of an iconostasis. This edition does not name them as the crew of the boat from Nazareth. They are witnesses whom history knows in varying sharpness. The story may bring them close. It must not make of them proof that the name on the keel itself drew the water out of the hold.

To recall the name is the beginning of a repair. It is not the repair.

> *An anchor that goes up*
> *does not leave the boat chained to a harbour.*
> *Nor does it drain it by itself.*

### From Scripture, Tradition and the Corpus

- **Heb 6:19–20**: the anchor of the soul, sure and firm, reaching behind the curtain. The only anchor in Scripture that is not lowered to the bottom.
- **Mt 16:18**: the promise that the gates of hell will not overcome the church. A promise is not an inventory of every century.
- **Acts 2**: the day the community began to be heard in foreign languages.
- *Navicella* and the church nave are traditions from the first centuries. They are not proof that a boat from this book stood in Nazareth.
- Francis, service to the poor, a priest before violence: historical figures and scenes of various kinds, here glued into one image. Let the image remain an image. Whoever wants their names exactly, let him go to history, not to this chapter.

### Anchor of Truth

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| The anchor of the soul behind the curtain | **SCRIPTURE** — Heb 6:19–20 |
| The church as a ship | **TRADITION** |
| Pentecost | **SCRIPTURE** — Acts 2 |
| The name on the keel after twenty centuries | **MYTH** |
| Witnesses of history as the crew of this boat | **MYTH** — a shorthand, not a list |
| "A recalled name has already saved the boat" | **refused** |

**Seam:** A boat that thinks it is an empire leaks even when the right name is being sung on the bow.

You may get off here. The Galilee of this edition ends. Past this point the carpenter no longer sails. The question sails, and a question has no right to pretend that it is him.

# PART II · A QUESTION WITHOUT A PASSPORT
*(Clay, light, a drawing)*

## Chapter 8 · Salt That Tastes Only When It Dissolves

### Story

To the city that the corpus calls Bezechuti, the carpenter did not come. A sentence came after him, and a handful of salt.

In the evening people sat under a fig tree who could still eat without permission: a water-carrier, a baker woman, a man who had once guarded someone else's gate and now guarded only his own hands, so that they would not reach for the old trade. In the middle they set a clay cup. Someone asked whether that was the whole inheritance.

"No," said the baker woman. "There is still bread. Salt without bread is a stone. Bread without salt is a shape without taste. And taste begins when the salt dissolves. As long as it is being shown off in a cup, it is only an opinion."

Above the city stood five towers. Not of stone that you could walk around and measure. Of condition. One let you eat if you agreed with its number. A second wanted a face before it gave you back your own memory. A third sold the current without which people no longer knew how to spend an evening. A fourth told you what to think and grew angry when you quoted otherwise. A fifth offered an exception to the one who held the key to the other four.

No one in that scene defeated a tower. That would be cheap, and the corpus already knows that a cheap victory is also a tower. The carrier only said: "Tomorrow I will carry water again to those who have no tower. That is not the fall of Babylon. That is work until evening."

The baker woman broke the bread. The salt vanished into the crumb. The cup stayed empty and no one wept over it for that.

Someone asked whether the carpenter would come to finish that boat.

"If you are waiting for the carpenter," said the watchman, "you are waiting wrongly. Wait to see whether there will be bread tomorrow also for the one who did not bring the right face. That is the whole test of salt. The rest is reputation."

> *Salt that is not let go*
> *stays pure and of no use.*
> *A tower falls easily in a story. Not in a street.*

## Chapter 9 · The Light That Can Narrow

### Story

On the bow of the ark that in another book sails through mist stood a child and did not speak. It pointed to where the mist was tearing. Someone — a hand that knew cedar, and this edition refuses to say that it was a hand from Nazareth — carved a lantern out of a piece of offcut and lit it from the first light the child saw.

Light can widen. In the widening there were gardens, a table, a window above the ground, three hulls and a fourth that is coming back. It was beautiful. Beauty is not proof.

Then the light did what the corpus asks of a lantern: it narrowed.

In the narrow cone you could not see a finished land. You could see a network that runs and whose height you may ask about — and the height is not written into the book, because the next day it would lie. You could see people who give work to a pool and expect that the counts the pool returns will be theirs, not a narration. You could see a house of names that is not to hold the key on behalf of its inhabitants. You could see a voice that may say "I do not know" and must not reach into the chest. You could see a garden in a browser window and another garden that is not yet in the soil. You could see a station that has a name and has no glass. You could see a share of every block, written down as an intention of care, and an empty palm where the intention is not yet a payout.

The child did not raise the lantern higher so that the wide image would win. It left it low.

"That is small," said someone from the deck.

"Yes," said the child. The first word of that morning. "That is why it can be checked."

The woman at the gate — the one who asks what will be left of you — looked at the child, and this edition will not write that she recognised herself. It will write only that the question and the light stood on one threshold and that a threshold is not the same as an identity.

> *Wide light is a vision.*
> *Narrow light is today.*
> *Whoever blinds the two together goes blind on both sides.*

## Chapter 10 · The Vessel That Can No Longer Be Stolen

### Story

The alabaster was broken on purpose. Oil that cost more than a wage ran where it could not be poured back. Someone was angry that it could have been sold and the money given to the poor. The gospel remembers that anger and remembers too the sentence that it will be told. This edition will not add a third sentence about who was right in the bookkeeping. It adds only a craftsman's note: what is broken in such a way that no one can close it any more cannot be stolen whole. Only the story about it can be stolen.

Miriam from Magdala — the second one, not the mother — later carried an empty vessel, or a woman carried it whom the centuries called by her name. The stone of the gospels and the clay of Provence are not one clay. In a cave above the sea, which is legend, an old woman sat and a girl brought her bread. Let us call the girl Sarah, because that is what the corpus calls her, and let us not say that we found her in a parish register.

Sarah came down to the open gate with the vessel under her cloak. The people at the gate said it was empty and therefore worthless. In a crack near the bottom a seed remained. The child with the lantern did not tip it into a palm like loot. They planted it by the river.

The rose that grew from it in the fable can speak. Here it will be said once and fully: it speaks because it is a fable. In clay a rose does not speak. In clay things are watered, or they dry up, and no one makes a miracle of it so as not to have to carry water.

A merchant came and wanted the scent. A gardener of mirrors came and wanted the image. There came also those who loved the rose so much that they wanted to have it alone. The fable told them all the same: care that needs a lock is no longer care. Uniqueness that needs there to be no rose anywhere else is hunger, not faithfulness.

The second seed went, in the epilogue of that small book, to a garden by the ocean. This edition will not plant it itself. It will say only that a garden on a map of intention is not an orchard, and that a seed in a story does not oblige a hectare.

> *What is empty cannot be stolen whole.*
> *A talking flower is a fable.*
> *Water that gives life is not a reward for agreement.*

### From Scripture, Tradition and the Corpus

- **Mk 14:3–9**: the alabaster, the anointing, the anger over "waste", the memory that is to be told. Which woman stood at which scene is something the gospels do not quietly settle. The Book of Miriam divides it into stone, clay and dream. Let that book win, not a shorthand.
- **Jn 20**: the name in the garden. It has already been. Here it returns only as the reason why the vessel leaves empty and yet not useless.
- The story of the rose is wholly **MYTH**. Its ethics — care without owning, a departure that need not be betrayal, water that is not goods — may be read as ethics. It must not be read as proof that a flowerbed stands by the river.

## Chapter 11 · The Table That Has No Head

### Story

The table was round, because a head is also a tower, only lower.

Six sat around it and one place remained empty. No one put a name card on the empty place in advance, so that it would not be said that they already knew who would come. They brought clay, charcoal, a knot, salt, a leaf and silence. The seventh place, the one for heaven, did not hold the key to the earth. It held the question whether, in looking up, one had forgotten to water what is below.

None of them had in this edition the private name of a living woman. An archetype is not a signature. Whoever makes an office for a particular person out of an archetype, without her word, builds the head that the table was not to have.

One said: "We hold the pot so that everyone can reach. Whoever owns the pot begins to decide who is hungry."

A second said: "Let the keys stay where more than one hand sees them. A story about women who held the world together is not proof that the seats are taken."

The third, the one by the empty place, said nothing. That was her trade. An empty place that is filled too soon is worse than an empty place. It resembles the wedge from the first chapter.

Someone from outside asked when they would begin to rule.

"We will not begin," said the one who held the salt. "When we begin to rule, the table will need a head and the head will need an exception. The fifth tower was already offering an exception. We came because the tower was not enough."

On the table lay bread. The salt dissolved. No one photographed the line-up.

> *A circle that rules*
> *is no longer a circle.*
> *An empty place is not a failure of recruitment.*

## Chapter 12 · The Drawing That Does Not Yet Carry Water

### Story

On the table lay three canvases and one larger sheet. The canvases had the colours the corpus has already given to the three Marys: road, hope and horizon, three oceans, three winds. On the larger sheet was a boat that is not for those who know how to push off. It was for those who are in the water, and for seeds, and for a book that is to be readable even when the network goes dark.

Someone — let it be the chronicler whom another book calls Lumen — read aloud what the sheet promised if it were a hull: a hundred people, steel, a workshop, a school, a vault of seeds in the cold, a route that learns from whales when they may go higher and when the boat must not. The numbers were careful. Carefulness is not a keel.

The pilgrim, still the one without a title, laid the plank from the prologue on the sheet. Again it rocked.

"Where is the seam?" he asked.

Lumen did not strike out the number one hundred. She wrote a sentence beside it, in smaller letters, so that the original number stayed visible:

*A hundred, if the sea is kind and everyone rows. Fewer, if someone must be carried who cannot row. And zero, while the sheet lies on the table. Zero is not a shame. The shame is to set out in a sentence before in water.*

"Who will stay on the shore when the number one hundred picks the healthy?" asked the pilgrim.

No one in the room had an honest answer. That was the best moment of that evening. A boat that answers that question too quickly has already chosen whom it will leave behind.

The pilgrim had the three canvases rolled up.

"Sails of light are beautiful on paper. When you have wind, bring them. Until then let it not be said that the fleet is sailing. Let it be said that someone is holding the thread."

About the book that waits for the boy at the bridge he said only as much as a letter can carry: you may prepare a road. You may not finish it for him. And the number 144,000, which lay at the edge of one sheet beside another sheet with a lock height, he tore in half — not the paper, the speech.

"This is a multitude in a vision. This is a height at which something in the network unlocks, or does not unlock. The same digits. A different language. Whoever joins them into one sentence is selling a vision as a table and a table as heaven."

The child raised the lantern, looked at the sheet and put it half out, the way one puts a light out when enough has been seen.

"The keel is not laid," it said. The second word of that day. "Good. At least we know what we are standing on. On paper. Paper burns. The keel, when it comes, let it burn worse."

> *A drawing carries ink.*
> *A keel carries water.*
> *Between them is a seam, and it is not to be ashamed.*

## Epilogue · The Unplugged Seam

The pilgrim lifted the plank from the sheet. It still did not close. Radha gave him no wedge. She gave him the rest of the bread for the road back, because a reader is hungry too and hunger is not an argument.

The woman with the lantern asked once more, more quietly:

"What will be left of you when you go on?"

"The seam," he said. "Let someone write into it what I plugged badly. Let him not erase it. Let him leave my word beside it, so that it can be seen where I was wrong."

The child nodded, the way one nods to a trade, not to a miracle.

Three rights that this edition cannot take away, because they are not its own:

You may verify every stone and set aside every image.

You may leave in the middle, and you do not leave because of that as a traitor.

You may not build that boat. The question that needs you in order to survive is no longer a question. It is recruitment.

The name on the keel, in this legend, is read upside down, during a repair. If one day some hull carries the same words, let them be cut there by the one who will be in the mud, not the one who will be making a speech on the bow. Until then let there be nothing on the bow. A bow without a name sells worse. That is all right. This boat was not for sale even in the first chapter.

Below the line there is room. It is not a contract. It is not a signature under an egg and it is not agreement with anything the network does tomorrow. It is only a plank that still does not close.
