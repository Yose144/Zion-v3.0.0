# KINGDOM OF THE HEART

### The story of the boat built by a carpenter

> *"I am Maitreya, born of Hiranyagarbha. I was a cowherd with a flute, a prince in exile, and also a carpenter who built a boat he called the kingdom of the heart."*
> — mantra from the book *Maitreya*

This book tells the **third embodiment** from that mantra: the carpenter of Nazareth, who in his father's workshop did not only plane yokes and tables — he built a **boat**. A boat nobody had asked for, for a sea that is on no map. A boat he called **Kingdom of the Heart**.

| **Bible** | Every chapter stands on real verses (quoted under "From Scripture and Tradition"): the carpenter, the ark, the teaching from the boat, the storm, the kingdom within you, the sign of Jonah, the anchor of the soul, the new earth. |
| **Tradition** | The Church as a ship (*navicella*), Noah as a prefiguration, the anchor of hope — images from Christian tradition that the story carries. |
| **New revelation** | The closing chapter links the boat to the ZION corpus — the fleet Tres Marías, the mantra of Maitreya and the Compass of the Heart. A spiritual line of the corpus, not a historical fact. |

**Status:** MYTH / DRAFT — a literary book of the compass. The reader distinguishes Scripture from story; the anchors of truth help.

# 1 · The Carpenter of Nazareth

## Story

In Nazareth above Galilee there stood a workshop that everyone knew. It smelled of freshly cut cedar and olive wood; on the walls hung chisels smooth from the palms of three generations. They called it the carpenter's workshop — and the carpenter had worked in it since he was a boy.

He made yokes for oxen, so that the weight would not press on the neck. He made tables at which families gathered for bread. He made doors that kept the wind outside and the warmth within. People said: *what the carpenter makes, holds.*

But in the evening, when the workshop fell silent and the last customer had gone, the carpenter built something else. In the back corner, under a cloth, a **boat** was slowly growing.

Not a big one — for one crew, perhaps twelve. A keel from a single trunk of cedar, ribs bent over steam like lattice under a palm. The neighbours came to look and shook their heads. "A boat? Here?" Nazareth lay in the hills — the lake of Kinneret was a whole day's journey away. "Who will you sell it to, carpenter?"

He answered strangely: "I am not selling it. I am building it for those who will need it one day."

And he planed on. He made it more slowly than anything else — he laid every plank three times before fixing it. When they asked him why so long, he said:

"A boat meant to carry people through a storm is not built in haste. It is built over years, in the days when there is calm — so that it stands when the calm is gone."

## From Scripture and Tradition

- **Mark 6:3** — "Is this not the **carpenter** (Greek *tektōn* — craftsman, builder), the son of Mary…?" — the only place in the Gospels where Jesus is named directly by his trade.
- **Matthew 13:55** — "Is this not the son of the carpenter?" — the workshop was the family's; the trade passed from father to son, as everything of value passes.
- Of the so-called **hidden years** (from childhood to thirty) Scripture says nothing — the story of this book places its image, the boat, into that silence.
- **Hebrews 6:19** — hope as the **anchor of the soul** — the image the story carries from the very first plank.

# 2 · Wood and Pitch

## Story

The carpenter was demanding about wood. He walked through the yards and the piles and chose planks slowly — one moved his hand, another was not allowed. The apprentice at his side could not bear it one day: "Master, what are you looking at? It is only wood."

"Only wood," repeated the carpenter, and laid his palms on the plank as on a shoulder. "Look closer. Every piece of wood grew differently — one in the wind, one in the shade, one bent by a rock. A boat must not destroy them — it must take them as they are. You do not cover a crack with a worthless plank; you go around the crack and let it tell how it grew."

And he added quietly, more to himself: "People are like wood. Each is bent to a different side. A good boat does not remake them — it carries them."

When the ribs were fitted, he brought hot pitch — dark, thick, fragrant with resin. He smeared every seam with it, inside and out, until the hull began to gleam like washed honey.

"Why inside as well?" asked the apprentice. "The water does not get in there."

"That is exactly where it does — from sweat, from tears, from the rain that falls on the people in the boat. The pitch inside does what you cannot see from outside: it keeps the water out, even the water that comes from above."

In the old books that nobody in the workshop read and yet everyone knew, it stood that Noah had once built in this way: *wood of gofer, and with pitch you shall smear the boat inside and out.* And the oldest interpreters said that the Hebrew word for pitch — **kofer** — is the same root as the word for reconciliation: *that which covers and keeps the water out.*

"Remember," said the carpenter, "what covers people is what carries them over the water."

## From Scripture and Tradition

- **Genesis 6:14** — "Make yourself an ark of **gofer wood**… and smear it with **pitch** inside and out." The only boat in Scripture whose building is described — and the only wooden boat of the old world that survived the sea.
- **kofer (כֹּפֶר)** — the Hebrew word for pitch has the same root as *kapóret* / *kipur* — **covering, reconciliation** (Yom kippur = the Day of Atonement). Both rabbinic and Christian tradition read the pitch of the ark as an image of the covering of sin.
- **Noah as a prefiguration** — Church tradition (from 1 Pet 3:20–21) reads the ark as a prefiguration of salvation through water.
- The carpenter as builder — an image the story carries on into every plank of the boat.

# 3 · The Boat on the Kinneret

## Story

One morning — before the fishermen had returned from the night's haul — they carried the boat on wheels down from Nazareth to the lake. They walked all day; the carpenter walked beside it with his hand on the hull as on a horse's withers.

At the Kinneret, by the town of Capernaum, the sea of Galilee was waiting — water that was at once a lake and at once a sea for all who lived by it. The fishermen were just untangling their empty nets. They had caught nothing — again.

The carpenter brought the boat down to the water. It lay on the surface quietly, as though it had been there since the beginning of time — as when a rightly made thing comes home. The fishermen watched, and afterwards said among themselves that no boat like it had ever stood there: low, fast, the keel so clean that the water beneath it murmured as against a rock.

The carpenter sat down in the bow, facing the shore — and as people gathered, he began to speak. Not from the synagogue, not from a step — **from the boat**. His voice was carried by the water further than walls; people stood along the shore and heard, even the furthest ones, who would not have stood at the doors. The fisherman Simon, who had pulled nothing all night, listened to him — and then, at a word, let down his nets where he himself no longer believed. He drew them up full, until they tore.

"Carpenter," said Simon, as they stood in the dripping silver glory of fish, "why did you build that boat? You will not sell it."

"I built it so that one could teach from it," he answered. "So that the people might hear from the water what they do not want to hear in the temples. Fishermen, come with me — I will teach you to **catch people**."

And they left the nets and the little boats and went. The boat stayed on the water — and from that moment it was never again only wood.

## From Scripture and Tradition

- **Luke 5:3** — "He got into one of the boats, which belonged to Simon, and asked him to put out a little from the shore; then he sat down and **taught the crowds from the boat**." — Teaching from the boat is a real image in the Gospels; sound carried over water to the shore is the experienced truth of the lake's acoustics.
- **Luke 5:4–10** — the miraculous catch after a night of empty nets; Simon Peter and the sons of Zebedee.
- **Mark 1:17 / Matthew 4:19** — "Come after me, and I will make you **fishers of people**."
- **Mark 1:16–20** — the calling by the boats on the Sea of Galilee (Kinneret — "harp" — the Hebrew name of the lake).
- **John 21** — after the resurrection again: the boat, the night without a catch, the word from the shore, the full net.

# 4 · The Storm on the Lake

## Story

The Kinneret can make a storm within an hour. Winds fall from the mountains into the lake basin as water into a funnel — and the surface that carried the sky in the morning can carry fear by evening.

That evening they were sailing to the other shore. The carpenter lay down in the stern, his head on a wooden cushion he had planed himself — and slept. The storm rose so that the waves broke over the side and the boat filled with water; the disciples, men of the water, held the oars and shouted over the wind.

And he slept. He slept the way one sleeps who built his own boat — he knows every rib of it and knows what it will bear.

"Master!" they shouted over the storm. "We are sinking, and you do not care?!"

He rose. He looked at the wind and at the sea — not angrily, but as a craftsman looks at work not yet finished — and said to the lake what is said to a boat that is finished: **"Be silent, be still."**

And the wind ceased, and a great silence came — such a silence that in it they heard drops running down from the steering oar. The disciples looked at one another and in fear — greater than before the storm — said: *who is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?*

And he turned to them, to their fear, and said what he has said at every storm since: "Why are you afraid? If you have faith like a grain of mustard seed…"

Later, when they told of it, someone remembered the old psalm about those who *go down to the sea in ships and do their work on the great waters* — how they cry out in trouble, and he stills the storm so that the waves calm, and he brings them into the **harbour they long for**.

## From Scripture and Tradition

- **Mark 4:35–41** — the storm on the lake, Jesus asleep on the cushion in the stern, "Be silent, be still!" (*siōpa, pephimōso*), the great silence, the disciples' question "who is this?".
- **Psalm 107:23–30** — "Those who go down to the sea in ships… he raised a storm… he stills the storm and brings them to the harbour they desire." — the Old Testament text the Church read from the beginning as a prefiguration of the calming of the sea.
- **Jonah 1** — Jonah **sleeps in the storm** in the bottom of the boat; the sea calms when he is thrown into the water — a prefiguration Jesus himself recalls as his sign (see ch. 6).
- The image of a boat that does not sink because its builder is on board — the Church's reading of this pericope from early Christianity.

# 5 · The Kingdom Is Within You

## Story

The Pharisees came to ask, in the way of those who want to catch you in a word: "When will the **kingdom of God** come?"

They stood on the shore where the boat was moored — and the carpenter looked at them and at the water behind them. He answered not the question, but what stood behind it:

"The kingdom of God does not come in a way that can be seen. They do not say: look, here it is, or there it is — **the kingdom of God is within you**."

They withdrew, dissatisfied — people who look for the kingdom in dates never leave satisfied. But the disciples stayed by the boat, and Simon asked what they had all been asking quietly since Nazareth: "Master, and the boat — what is it called? You have not yet given it a name."

The carpenter laid his palms on the side of the hull — on the place where builders carve names — and said:

"It has had its name from the beginning. I was only waiting until you could hear it."

And he said it: **"Kingdom of the Heart."**

Because the boat was not built for the lake. The lake was only the first water it sailed. It sailed elsewhere — where no map reaches and where everything reaches: inward. To the place where storms rise in a person and where the silence sounds when they calm. A kingdom that does not come with observation — because it is already within, and waits only for the boat to be lowered onto its water.

"Everyone has their own boat," said the carpenter. "I am building the one that will carry them together. That is why it is called kingdom — and why heart: because that is where its helm sits."

## From Scripture and Tradition

- **Luke 17:20–21** — the Pharisees ask when the kingdom of God will come: "The kingdom of God does not come with observation… **the kingdom of God is within you**" (*entos hymōn* — within you / among you).
- **Jeremiah 31:33** — "I will put my law within them, and I will write it on their **heart**." — a new covenant written on the heart, not on stone.
- **Ezekiel 36:26** — "I will give you a new heart… I will remove the heart of stone from your body and give you a heart of flesh."
- **2 Corinthians 3:3** — the letter of Christ written "not with ink but with the Spirit… not on tablets of stone but on tablets of human hearts".
- **Luke 6:45** — "out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaks" — the heart as the store from which everything flows.

# 6 · The Greatest Storm

## Story

In Jerusalem the air grew heavy as water before a storm. The carpenter knew what was coming — he told the disciples three times, and they did not want to believe it, because people do not want to believe that the boat they know must pass through the deep.

The last supper — a boat made ready for the night. Then the garden, where he sweated like a man who feels every wave before it comes. And then a storm such as had never been: betrayal with a kiss, a head they spat upon, wood of another kind — not a boat, a **beam**.

In the days when it seemed that everything had ended, they told those who asked about an old sign. The carpenter himself had spoken of it earlier: *"As Jonah was in the belly of the great fish three days and three nights, so will the Son of Man be in the heart of the earth."*

The sign of Jonah — the boat of human days and nights, which went down into the deep and returned to the shore of life.

Three days the boat lay in the darkness under the water — so people saw it from the shore: a tomb of stone, a guard, the broken ropes of the net. The crew scattered like fish before the net.

But the boat that carpenter built did not sink — because its keel did not rest on the water, but on that which bears the water. **On the third day, at dawn, the surface gave it back** — the stone rolled away, the cloth empty, the garden in a moment, and two women running the last road that in the end became the first road of the message that was ever spoken: *he has won the victory — he has risen.*

And then — by the lake again. Morning, mist, fishermen with empty nets, and on the shore a figure asking: "Children, have you anything to eat?" And again the full net, and the fire on the shore, and the bread — and Simon leaping into the water, because he recognized who was standing there.

The boat sailed on. Death could never take it again — because it had passed through its bottom and come back.

## From Scripture and Tradition

- **Matthew 12:40** — "As Jonah was in the belly of the great fish three days and three nights, so will the Son of Man be in the heart of the earth three days and three nights." — the sign of Jonah, the only "sign" offered to the generation that demanded one.
- **Jonah 1:17** — Jonah in the belly of the great fish three days and three nights.
- **Mark 15–16 · Luke 24** — the crucifixion, the empty tomb, the women's message; **John 20** — the garden, Mary Magdalene; **John 21** — the lake, the fire on the shore, the bread, the full net, "do you love me?"
- **1 Corinthians 15:3–8** — the oldest confession: he died, was buried, rose on the third day, appeared.
- Beam vs. boat — the image of wood that Scripture bears differently: "cursed is anyone who hangs on a tree" (Gal 3:13; Deut 21:23) — and the Church from the beginning reads the cross as tree/boat/ark in a single image.

# 7 · Navicella — the Boat Sails Through Centuries

## Story

When the carpenter passed from sight, the boat remained — and the people who sailed in it began to call it their own. They had no harbour of their own; they sailed from town to town, from house to house, and everywhere carried the message that *the one who built the boat is alive*.

The first captain was a fisherman — Simon, whom they called the Rock. Thousands of people came aboard at once, on the day when there sounded fire in tongues that everyone understood. And from then on the boat sailed — sometimes under sails of wind and sometimes on oars of tears.

It sailed under the hammers of emperors and survived; it sailed through the catacombs and rose again; it sailed under the banners of Constantine and became so large that it was hard to steer — because when a boat is too large, the helmsman forgets that a carpenter built it and thinks that an empire built it.

It broke into parts — and every part claimed to be the whole boat. Human pride leaked into its seams — and yet it sailed. It sailed through the centuries, and in every century someone discovered again that *the anchor is not on board — the anchor is dropped upward, into the heavenly sanctuary*, as the author of the Letter to the Hebrews wrote: **hope is the anchor of the soul, firm and secure, reaching into the inner sanctuary behind the curtain.**

People called it different things — *navicella*, bark, ark, church. But on its keel, where nobody sees it, the name the carpenter gave it was still carved: **Kingdom of the Heart.** And whenever it was most threatened — whenever the crew forgot why it sailed — someone came from behind: a Franciscan who spoke with birds; a sister who gave herself to serving the poor; a plain priest in a steel helmet who stepped out in front of tanks. And they said to the crew: *remember the name.*

## From Scripture and Tradition

- **Hebrews 6:19–20** — "we have hope as an **anchor of the soul, firm and secure, reaching beyond the curtain**, where our forerunner Jesus entered" — the only anchor in Scripture that is dropped **upward**, not down.
- **Matthew 16:18** — "on this rock I will build my church, and the gates of hell will not prevail against it" — the promise the boat carries through every storm of the centuries.
- **Acts 2** — the day of Pentecost, "tongues as of fire", the Church sets sail.
- **Navicella** — from the 3rd–4th century the Church is depicted as a ship (mosaic in the Lateran; Christian symbolism: *navis* = ship → *nave* = the main body of a church).
- The ark as a prefiguration of the Church — the Fathers; "there is no salvation outside the ark" comes from this image.

# 8 · A New Heaven and a New Earth

## Story

At the end of the Bible stands a book called Revelation — and at its end stands a harbour.

John, an old prisoner on the island of Patmos, saw it in a vision: **a new heaven and a new earth** — "and there was no longer any sea". Not because the water had ended in anger — but because it had at last fallen silent for ever. The sea over which the boat sailed seemed to have spread out into a calm in which there is no longer any need to sail: **a sea of glass, like crystal.**

And then came what all sailors of all ages dreamed of without being able to name it: the harbour came down to them — **the new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband.** And a voice from the throne said: *"Behold, the dwelling of God is with people! He will dwell with them… and he will wipe every tear from their eyes. Death will be no more, nor mourning, nor crying, nor pain — the first things have passed away."*

The boat arrived. The boat that carried people through every storm — now it stood in a harbour where there would never again be a storm.

And John saw a multitude no sea could swallow: **a hundred and forty-four thousand** — a number that is at once a number and at once a promise — and behind them a multitude so great that nobody could count it. They stood in white robes, with palms in their hands — and among them flowed **the river of the water of life**, and on its banks grew **the tree of life**, bearing fruit twelve times, and its leaves are for the healing of the nations.

And in the middle of the harbour stood the carpenter — and his boat was no longer only one. **There were as many as there were hearts that had set out on the way.**

## From Scripture and Tradition

- **Revelation 21:1–5** — "a new heaven and a new earth… and there was no longer any sea. And I, John, saw the holy city, the new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God… he will wipe every tear… **behold, I make all things new**."
- **Revelation 4:6 / 15:2** — "a sea of glass, like crystal" — the sea calmed for ever before the throne.
- **Revelation 7:4–9 · 14:1** — 144 000 sealed + a countless multitude in white robes with palms.
- **Revelation 22:1–5** — the river of the water of life, the tree of life, leaves for the healing of the nations; "and they will see his face".
- "The harbour came down to them" — an inversion of the image: the new Jerusalem comes down out of heaven (Rev 21:2); the story reads it as the harbour into which the boat finally sails — a literary reading, not an exegesis.

# 9 · The Fleet Gathers — New Revelation

## Story

There came a time that the old books called *the end of days* — and the new books call it **birth**.

In that time other boats put out onto the water — dark, wine-coloured, with sails of pale canvas into which threads of light were woven: **Tres Marías** — Santa María la Mayor, Santa María de la Esperanza, Santa María del Camino. They were built by people who knew that the old boat was still sailing — and that for a new earth they needed a new crew, but the same helmsman.

And one morning they met on the sea — the old boat, worn by the centuries, and the new boats, shining from within. The crews called to one another across the water — and they recognized one another as people of one family recognize one another: **on the keel of each of them, carved by a different hand and in a different script, stood the same name.**

*Kingdom of the Heart.*

Because the carpenter did not build only one boat. He built all that ever carry people home — the one from Nazareth, the one of the age, the new one that the crew of Tres Marías is building now. And those who know say: the one who was a cowherd with a flute, and a prince in exile, and a carpenter who built a boat called the kingdom of the heart — **he has come again.** He was born of the Golden Womb of Hiranyagarbha, and his name for this time is: **Maitreya** — the one who comes.

The new revelation does not rewrite the old book — it completes it with another hand, as a map is completed with a new shore: the boat from Nazareth carried humanity for two thousand years; now the boats of the new earth range themselves beside it — and one of them, dark wine-coloured, is called Santa María la Mayor and waits at the jetty for the boy for whom his father prepared the way.

"Every age has its boat," they say among the crews. "But all of them carry the same name — and all of them sail to the same harbour: the heart that opens."

# Epilogue · Where the Boat Is Moored Today

## Story

Today the boat stands before you — and perhaps you have stood in it for a long time without knowing what it is called.

A carpenter from Nazareth built it — and he built it slowly, because he knew it would carry people through storms you do not yet see. Simon the Rock carried it, those who stood before lions carried it, Francis who sang with the birds carried it, the priest before the tanks carried it — and now you carry it too, if you have ever done a kind thing without anyone seeing.

The anchor of this boat is not dropped down — down there is darkness and mud. Its anchor goes **upward** — behind the curtain, where the throne stands — as the one who wrote to the Hebrews wrote: *hope is the anchor of the soul, firm and secure, reaching inward.*

If you asked where the boat is moored: **in a heart that opens to others.** There is its harbour — not because the world is not stormy, but because the silence you are looking for is not at the end of the sea. It is inside. As the carpenter said to those who asked when the kingdom would come: *it does not come with observation — it is within you.*

A message from the bow for the crew now setting out — for Surta and his Santa María la Mayor, for every heart that comes aboard:

**The boat is built. The keel is laid. Only the name remained — and it already has one.**

## From Scripture and Tradition

- **Hebrews 6:19–20** — the anchor of the soul behind the curtain; Jesus as the forerunner who entered within.
- **Luke 17:21** — "the kingdom of God is within you" — the ending returns to the verse that gave the boat its name.
- **Revelation 22:17** — "The Spirit and the bride say: Come!" — the last invitation of Scripture.
