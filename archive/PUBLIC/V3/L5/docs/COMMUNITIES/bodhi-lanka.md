Under this tree, the community gathers. Not to worship, but to remember. Not to command, but to listen. The Bodhi tree is the axis — the living bridge between root and crown, between the human and the divine, between the story of Rama and Sita and the silence of awakening.

> *"In Bodhi Lanka there is no temple and no throne. There is a tree. And under the tree, love is not a feeling — it is the ether through which all things move."*

This is an **Akasha node** — the fifth element, the all-permeating principle. Where the other four nodes hold earth, fire, water, and air, Bodhi Lanka holds the space in which all four can exist. Its practice is **Bhakti** (devotion) — not as sentiment, but as the recognition that love is the substrate of all governance, all agriculture, all technology, all community. Three roots anchor this node:

It was alive when the Roman Empire fell, when the printing press was invented, when the internet was born. It is the oldest living planted tree on Earth. | Aspect | Status | Details |
|--------|--------|---------|
| **Legal entity** | 🔵 Researching | Sri Lankan NGO (under Voluntary Social Service Organizations Act No. 31 of 1980) vs company limited by guarantee vs cooperative society |

| **Zoning** | 🔵 Researching | Must permit: agriculture, educational activities, eco-tourism, Ayurvedic wellness, temporary structures |
| **Building permits** | 🔵 Not yet required | Phase 1 uses temporary/eco-cabin structures; permanent pavilions in Phase 2 |
| **Tax residency** | 🔵 TBD | Sri Lankan tax optimization for non-profit agricultural/educational entities; BOI (Board of Investment) incentives possible |

### Recommended legal path

1. **Phase 0–1:** Voluntary Social Service Organization (NGO) — registered under Act No. 31 of 1980 with the NGO Secretariat. Lowest barrier for non-profit community work, eligible for international grants, can receive foreign donations. Requires a Sri Lankan board majority.
2. **Phase 2+:** Company Limited by Guarantee (under Companies Act No. 7 of 2007) — better for commercial activities (retreats, farm products, Ayurvedic wellness), can enter contracts, own assets. No share capital; members guarantee a nominal amount.
3. **Alternative:** Cooperative Society (under Cooperative Societies Act) — member-owned, profit-sharing, agricultural focus. Strong tradition in Sri Lanka (farmer cooperatives, tea smallholder cooperatives).
4. **Hybrid:** NGO (operations, education, grants) + Company Limited by Guarantee (commercial arm, retreats, products) — separates non-profit and commercial activities, common structure in Sri Lanka.

### Land strategy

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| **Long-term lease (30+ years)** | Legal for foreigners, lower initial investment, common in Sri Lanka | Lease restrictions on permanent structures, landlord dependency |
| **Local partnership (Sri Lankan entity)** | Can own freehold through majority-local company, deep integration | Requires trusted local partner, governance complexity, potential for dispute |
| **Co-ownership (cooperative)** | Shared risk, community equity, Sri Lankan cooperative tradition | Complex governance, exit difficulties, regulatory requirements |
| **Municipal / temple concession** | Very low cost, cultural legitimacy, sangha relationship | Uncertain availability, political/religious sensitivity, restrictions on use |

**Recommended:** Long-term lease with Sri Lankan partner entity — secure the land through a registered local company or NGO with Sri Lankan majority board, prove the model, then transition to cooperative ownership. Sri Lanka's foreign ownership restrictions make direct freehold impractical without local partnership.

**Cultural triangle context:**
- Anuradhapura region is a UNESCO World Heritage sacred area — building restrictions are stricter near archaeological and religious sites
- Hill country (Kandy, Matale, Kegalle) offers fertile land, cooler climate, and proximity to Ayurvedic traditions
- South coast offers tourism infrastructure but less cultural/spiritual anchoring
- **Recommendation:** Anuradhapura region for spiritual anchoring, or hill country for climate and Ayurvedic partnerships — site TBD (see §8)

| **Efficiency measures** | Solar LED, hand tools | DC microgrid for lighting, solar water heater, passive cooling design | Solar air conditioning (DC), biogas backup, rammed earth thermal mass |

**Tropical monsoon climate reality:**
- **Tropical sun is strong** — Sri Lanka receives 4–5.5 kWh/m²/day of solar irradiance year-round, significantly higher than European nodes
- **Two monsoon seasons:** Yala (May–September, southwest monsoon) and Maha (October–February, northeast monsoon) — solar production drops 30–40% during heavy monsoon periods
- **Critical design factor:** Battery sizing must account for 3–5 consecutive overcast days during monsoon; humidity protection for all electronics
- **Recommendation:** Grid connection as fallback for monsoon (unlike off-grid Pacific nodes) — minimal use, but essential for reliability during extended overcast periods
- **Humidity:** All solar equipment must be tropical-rated (IP65+ enclosures, conformal-coated electronics, ventilated battery housing)

**Technology stack:**
- Panels: Longi / Jinko / Tier-1 tropical-rated (anti-reflective coating for high humidity)
- Batteries: Pylontech / BYD LiFePO4 (tropical-rated, ventilated enclosure, 5–15 year warranty)
- Inverter: Victron MultiPlus-II (hybrid, scalable, proven in tropical off-grid)
- Monitoring: Victron VRM + custom ZION telemetry (future)
- **Cooling:** Passive design (cross-ventilation, shaded roofs), solar-powered fans, no AC in Phase 1 (natural ventilation sufficient at 25–32 °C with proper design)

### 3.2 Water

| Parameter | Current | Phase 1 Target | Phase 2 Target |
|-----------|---------|----------------|----------------|
| **Source** | Municipal / delivered | Rainwater harvesting (primary) + well | Rainwater + well + greywater reuse |
| **Collection area** | N/A | 200 m² roof → ~200 m³/year (1,000 mm effective) | 400 m² roof + paved → ~600 m³/year |

| **Treatment** | Municipal | Sand filter + UV | Multi-stage: sediment → carbon → UV → remineralization |
| **Greywater** | Septic | Constructed wetland (reed beds, vetiver) | Full closed-loop: wetland → irrigation |
| **Blackwater** | Septic | Compost toilets | Biodigester → biogas + fertilizer |

**Sri Lankan water context:**
- Rainfall: 1,000–4,000 mm/year depending on region (Anuradhapura: ~1,200 mm dry zone; hill country: up to 4,000 mm wet zone)
- **Monsoon harvesting is essential** — the two monsoon seasons deliver the majority of annual rainfall in concentrated bursts; cisterns must be oversized to capture monsoon surplus for dry season
- **Well water** is common in the dry zone (Anuradhapura) — quality varies, often needs filtration for iron/hardness
- **Vetiver grass** (*Vetiveria zizanioides*) — native to Sri Lanka, proven in constructed wetlands for greywater treatment; also used for erosion control on monsoon-affected slopes
- **Dry season risk (June–September in Anuradhapura):** Rainwater cistern + well backup essential; irrigation priority for food forest during dry months
- **Flood risk during monsoon:** All water infrastructure must be elevated or sealed against flooding; drainage channels essential

## 6. Governance

### 6.1 Off-chain: Sociocratic Circles + Sangha Consultation

```
General Circle (all Guardians + long-stay members)
    ├── Operations Circle (daily farm, guests, maintenance)
    │       ├── Agriculture Sub-circle (food forest, Ayurvedic garden, rice)
    │       ├── Hospitality Sub-circle
    │       └── Infrastructure Sub-circle (solar, water, monsoon preparedness)
    ├── Finance Circle (budget, accounting, ZION node, grants)
    ├── Community Circle (conflict, wellness, culture, admissions)
    ├── Practice Circle (Bhakti sessions, meditation, retreats, Ayurvedic programs)
    └── Knowledge Circle (documentation, education, seed library, Akashic archive, Dharma)
```

**Decision-making:** Consent-based (not consensus — "no reasoned objection" threshold). Double-linking: each sub-circle elects a representative to parent circle.

**The Bodhi tree as the circle center:**
- All major community decisions are made **under the Bodhi tree** — the living center of the site
- No electronic devices in General Circle meetings held under the tree (paper notes only)
- The tree is a reminder that governance is rooted (in the earth) and reaches upward (toward the sky) — it is both grounded and aspiring
- **Monsoon exception:** When rain prevents outdoor circle meetings under the tree, Pavilion Prema (Love) serves as indoor governance hall
- **Sangha consultation:** For matters involving cultural, spiritual, or community relations with local Buddhist communities, the circle consults with a respected member of the local sangha (Buddhist monastic community). The sangha does not govern — it advises. The circle decides. This mirrors the traditional Sri Lankan relationship between lay community and sangha: mutual respect, distinct roles.

## 15. Cultural Sensitivity Framework

Bodhi Lanka draws inspiration from the Ramayana, Buddhist tradition, and Sri Lankan indigenous culture. This requires the most careful framing of any L5 node, because Sri Lanka is a multi-religious society (Buddhist, Hindu, Muslim, Christian) with a complex history where sacred narratives intersect with ethnic identity and political conflict.

| Element | Appropriate Use | Inappropriate Use |
|---------|----------------|-------------------|
| **The Bodhi tree** | Universal symbol of awakening, living connection to the Sri Maha Bodhi, the oldest planted tree on Earth — a symbol of continuity, patience, and rooted wisdom | Claim to Buddhist religious authority, commodification of a sacred symbol, "Bodhi branding" for commercial purposes |
| **Rama and Sita (Ramayana)** | Universal teaching about love as devotion through adversity, the journey of exile and return, the bridge (*setu*) as connection | Appropriation of Hindu epic for commercial retreat product, taking sides in Sri Lankan ethnic narratives, claiming the Ramayana as "our story" |
| **Sri Maha Bodhi (Anuradhapura)** | Inspiration for the central Bodhi tree, recognition of 2,300 years of living continuity, cultural anchoring | Claim that our Bodhi tree is "the" Bodhi tree, tourism that exploits the sacred site, implying religious authority |
| **Bhakti (devotion)** | Universal principle — love as the substrate of all action, devotion as practice not sentiment | Appropriation of specific Bhakti traditions (Gaudiya, Vaishnava) without context, "Bhakti branding" |
| **Dharma (practice)** | Universal principle — the path walked, not the destination reached; practice over belief | Claim to Buddhist doctrinal authority, "Dharma certification", gamification of spiritual progress |
| **Ayurveda / Hela Wedakama** | Partnership with Sri Lankan practitioners, honoring 3,000-year tradition, food as medicine | Appropriation without practitioner involvement, "Ayurvedic wellness" as luxury product without cultural context |
| **Lotus** | Universal symbol — blooms in mud, not of the mud; awakening from suffering, not in spite of it | Religious kitsch, overuse as decorative motif without meaning |
| **Setu (Rama's bridge)** | Metaphor for connection — ZION as bridge between communities, not a wall | Claim to the physical Ram Setu (Adam's Bridge) site, political positioning on the bridge's existence |
| **Sangha consultation** | Respectful relationship with Buddhist monastic community — advice, not authority; mutual respect, distinct roles | Implying sangha endorsement of ZION or the project, using monk imagery for marketing, blurring governance and religion |

**Guiding principle (from Execution Charter):**

> *"No monetized 'sacred skins', no gamification of spiritual authority."*

Bodhi Lanka must not be sold as a "Buddhist retreat center" or a "Ramayana experience." It is a **community rooted in love as governance**, drawing inspiration from Sri Lankan and South Asian contemplative traditions. The Bodhi tree, the Ramayana, and Ayurveda are **inspiration and context**, not authority or product. The project honors these traditions by practicing them authentically — not by packaging them for sale.

**Multi-religious sensitivity:**
- Sri Lanka is home to Buddhist (70%), Hindu (13%), Muslim (10%), and Christian (7%) communities
- The Ramayana is sacred to Hindus and revered by many Buddhists — but its narrative intersects with Sri Lankan ethnic history in complex ways (Ravana, the demon king of Lanka, is a villain in the Ramayana but is revered by some Sri Lankan traditions)
- The Bodhi tree is sacred to Buddhists worldwide — planting a Bodhi tree is an act of reverence, not decoration
- **The project must be explicitly inclusive** — not a "Buddhist community" but a community that draws from Buddhist wisdom, open to all who practice compassion regardless of religion
- **Cultural review panel:** Include representatives from Buddhist, Hindu, and local Sri Lankan perspectives from Phase 0
- **Local community engagement:** The project cannot succeed without the trust and support of the local Sri Lankan community — this is built through years of relationship, not through legal structures or marketing
