# THE ROSE STORY — Epilogue: the Genesis Garden
## The seed from the alabaster vessel reaches the Nabão valley — and the story touches the clay without passing itself off as evidence

That other water had the taste of a spring — cold, calcareous, as though it flowed straight from the earth.

It happened before her return to the river, when she was carrying in the alabaster vessel the seed given to her by the Rose.

Lumi felt it before she saw it — the air changed, everything smelled of damp soil and beneath the soil of rosemary. She called it "the other water", because the river by the gate had stayed far away and the Nabão carried it differently — more slowly, more quietly, like water that has time.

The Genesis Garden did not surprise her. No domes, no gold, no shrine. A canvas tent, a barrel of water, a circle of stones one could sit on — and by the first olive tree a woman with calluses on her palms, kneeling in a bed and weeding.

"Is this the Genesis Garden?" asked Lumi.

"This is the Genesis Garden," said the woman without raising her head. "My name is Ana. And you are carrying something heavy — I can tell from your walk."

Lumi smiled. She set the vessel down beside her — the same one that had once arrived empty and in whose crack the first seed had waited. That one had already grown into the Rose by the river. The seed Lumi carried now had been given to her by the Rose at parting: one ripe seed that had fallen by itself, as seeds can when no one holds them.

"What are you carrying?" asked Ana.

"Not a relic," said Lumi. "A possibility."

"A possibility of what?"

"Of a rose. Whether it may have a place here."

Ana wiped her hands on her trousers and looked Lumi in the eyes for the first time. Then she looked at the vessel, at the crack, at the seed.

"We grow food here," she said. "Tomatoes, figs, olives. Everything here justifies its water — a barrel is not a river. When it drops in summer, what feeds people is watered first. Do you understand?"

"I understand," said Lumi. "And precisely therefore a rose belongs here. Not everything alive must justify its existence by yield — but nothing alive may drink at the expense of the people or the beds that feed them. The rose will get only water the garden can spare. If there is no such water, the seed will not go into the ground yet."

"Care without privilege," said Ana — and it was not a question.

"Care without privilege," said Lumi.

They chose the place together — carefully, the way places are chosen when you know the plant will have thorns. Not into the centre of the circle, where things are laid for everyone to look at. Not under the post with carved signs, as though a seed needed a label in order to grow. At the edge of the bed, near the place where the water ran down from the barrel — close to the moisture, but aside from the footpath along which people walk barefoot for water and for tomatoes.

"Thorns do not belong under bare feet," said Ana. "A boundary is planted so that no one trips over it."

Ana brought a handful of soil in her palms — from the same earth to which she had once said *here*. Lumi took out the seed with both hands, the way one takes out things that are not yours.

"And now comes the hard part," said Ana. "People bring stories here — and soon someone will learn what you planted. They will come and say: let us name it. Let us give it a label. Let us call it *Miriam's rose* — and it will become a place people go to as to a relic."

"And you don't want that?"

"I don't," said Ana. "We planted a seed, Lumi. Not a woman, not a dynasty, not a little avatar, not a secret lineage. When it grows, it will be a rose — and that will be enough for it. Things that get a label stop growing and begin to represent."

Lumi nodded — and she knew that this said the same as the Rose herself had once said when choosing a name: a way of address, not a cage; a name, not a rank.

The seed went into the earth — into ordinary soil, into an ordinary late morning. No voice from above, no rainbow, no flower in honour. Ana watered it with a small cup of water the garden could spare — only after the beds of tomatoes.

"And now?" asked Lumi, when only turned earth remained before them.

"And now you will come back," said Ana. "Not in a week — seeds do not need visits. In a month. And if it does not grow, I will write in the work notebook that it did not grow — and no one will make of it a legend about a miracle that did not happen. A garden that holds the truth holds failure too."

Lumi sat for a while on the circle of stones and looked at the bare soil. There was nothing in the vessel any more — and for the first time it seemed lighter precisely because something had gone from it into the earth where it belonged.

They did not plant evidence. They planted a question — and the evidence was yet to grow.

## The boundary of the epilogue

- **The whole scene is MYTH.** Lumi, Ana, the second seed and the conversation by the first olive tree are a literary fable — none of it happened.
- **Repository fact:** in the repository there exist the project documents of the Genesis Garden ([`../BodhiGaia/03-Zahrada-Genesis.md`](../BodhiGaia/03-Zahrada-Genesis.md)), the metadata of the OASIS world `GENESIS_GARDEN` (`apps/OasisWeb/src/domain/config/worlds.ts`) and the follow-up chapter [`../BodhiGaia/12-Ruze-v-Zahrade-Genesis.md`](../BodhiGaia/12-Ruze-v-Zahrade-Genesis.md). Their existence is verifiable; their content tells a story.
- **The physical rose in Portugal: UNSUBSTANTIATED.** No rose in the Genesis Garden has been documented as planted — not even by this text.
- **The pilot: HORIZON / NOT STARTED.** The proposal for the physical planting is the document [`../BodhiGaia/pilots/GENESIS-ROSE-001.md`](../BodhiGaia/pilots/GENESIS-ROSE-001.md) — not started, without a confirmed place, consent or finances.
