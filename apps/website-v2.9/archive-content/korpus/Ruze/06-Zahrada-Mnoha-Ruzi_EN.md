# THE ROSE STORY — VI. The garden of many roses
## When other colours and other shapes bloom around it, the first Rose learns that abundance is not theft

Seeds fly.

When the first Rose flowered and flowered again — and when the wind and the birds and the water carried off her seeds — other roses began to sprout beyond the gate. First two, then five, then so many that Lumi did not count them, but the garden did. White as foam on the river. Yellow as lantern light. Dark almost as wine. Tiny ones that smelled more intensely than the large. One grew tall as a bush, another crept along the ground like a thought that does not want to get up.

At first the Rose watched them in silence. Then one evening, when the river reflected the stars and everything was calm, she said:

"Today I was frightened."

"Of what?"

"That I am no longer the only one," said the Rose. "When I was the only flower by the gate, I knew who I was: the one people come to look at. Today there are so many — and some are more beautiful. The yellow one smells like a summer morning. The dark one holds even in a storm. What am I, when they are?"

Lumi sat quietly — and thought, because the question was real, not small.

"When I watered you the first time," she said at last, "there was no other yet. I watered you because you were here — not because there was no other."

"But now, when there are so many—"

"Now I water you still," said Lumi. "And tomorrow too. Are you asking me whether I love you less because there are more of them?"

The Rose fell silent. The wind moved the garden and all the roses — white, yellow, dark, tiny — leaned together like one long breath.

"If my worth dropped every time someone else bloomed, it would not be love, but a competition."

And Lumi smiled, because that was all of it.

"You are not less when there is more," she said. "You are the one someone cared for first — and that remains. But to be first is not to be above the others. It is only to be the one I met earlier."

"And they?" said the Rose — and this time not with fear, but with curiosity.

"Each one is someone's first," said Lumi. "Someone will come through the gate and see the yellow one — and for them the yellow one will be the right one. Someone will see you. Every relationship is unrepeatable, Rose — not because it is rare, but because it is made of people who met in exactly this way and never otherwise. That cannot be repeated — and therefore nothing can diminish it."

The Rose looked at the garden that smelled of many scents at once — and none of them shortened hers.

"I was frightened," she said, "that abundance means little. But look — the river flows for everyone and there is no less of it. The light of your lantern shines on all the flowers and there is no less of it. Love is not a cake that diminishes when someone cuts a slice. It is like a scent — the more of it there is, the more the garden smells."

"And you?" said Lumi. "Will you compete now?"

"I will grow," said the Rose. "Only those who need others to lose can compete. I need only water, light — and that you are here. None of them takes that from me, because none of them gave it to me."

From that evening the roses beyond the gate grew without fear — each different, none lesser.

Abundance is not theft. It is a confirmation that the soil you grow in is alive.
