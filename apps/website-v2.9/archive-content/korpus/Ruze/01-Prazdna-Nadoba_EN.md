# THE ROSE STORY — I. The empty vessel
## Sára descends to the gate, hands over what cannot be stolen — and the single seed that remained

The Rose began in a vessel that everyone said was empty.

Sára carried it under her cloak from the cave above the sea all the way to the open gate. The way was long — and the vessel seemed heavier precisely because there was nothing in it. If it had been full, one would carry it as a burden. Empty, it is carried as a question.

By the gate ran a river, and by the river sat a child. Beside it stood a lantern, although it was day, and it looked at the water as though the river were telling it a slow story.

"Are you the child with the lantern?" asked Sára. "An old woman told me about you. She said you met by a river — and that you asked her whether she would go with you."

The child turned. "I am Lumi," she said. "And you are carrying something heavy."

"A vessel," said Sára, and set it down on the grass between them. It was of alabaster, old, with seams like scars — and at the bottom a crack where the stone had broken long ago and been joined again. "Everyone says it is empty. Miriam gave it to me and said: you carry it onward."

Lumi stood up. She was small — smaller than Sára — and barefoot, and when she touched the vessel she did it carefully, as though touching someone's sleep.

"Why carry an empty vessel?" she asked.

"Because it was once full of oil," said Sára. "All of it poured out and nothing remained. But the vessel stayed. Miriam said a full vessel can be stolen, but an emptied one cannot. What is gone cannot be taken."

Lumi tilted the vessel against the light. And there — in the crack at the bottom, there where the stone had once broken — sat a single seed. Small, brown, ordinary. It could have been there since yesterday or since long ago; seeds do not say how long they have been waiting.

"It is not a relic," said Sára quickly, because she saw how Lumi's gaze was changing. "Nothing sacred. Miriam planted roses the whole way — this is only a seed that remained. The last of an ordinary handful."

"That is the best kind of seed," said Lumi.

They planted it by the river, a little way from the gate, there where the first sun of morning falls. Sára loosened the soil with her fingers, Lumi carried water in her palms — slowly, several times there and back, because palms are a poor vessel and water runs between the fingers like time.

"It doesn't matter," said Lumi when on the third attempt only a few drops were left. "Watering can be learned again and again. We must only not forget why we come back."

When the seed was in the earth, Sára stood up and brushed off her knees.

"Won't you wait until it grows?" asked Lumi.

"No," said Sára. "My task was to bring the story. With you it can grow." And then she added more quietly, as though it were the only thing she had truly understood on the way: "An empty vessel is not a lack, Lumi. It is the only honest place where something new can be laid."

She left through the open gate. She did not look back, because a story we hand on must not be held by the sleeve.

And Lumi stayed by the river and looked at the patch of loosened soil that did not look like the beginning of anything — and yet it was.

The seed beneath the earth did not know it was the last. It thought it was the first — and it was right.
