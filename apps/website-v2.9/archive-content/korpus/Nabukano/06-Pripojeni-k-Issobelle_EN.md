# 6 · Connection to Issobella

## The Story

Up close it was not a city. It was a **wheel**.

The ring turned slowly, the way a mill wheel turns, only without the mill: two hundred metres across, two revolutions a minute, and on its inner side a corridor that rises before you and behind you and never ends. And inside, when you look out of the window of the dock, you do not see a station. You see the landscape returning.

*"Land!"* said the child, because words from the water come by themselves.

*"Here one does not land,"* said the navigator. *"Landing is the word for when the earth catches you. Here one **connects**. You must hit the rotation — if you touched it going the other way, the wheel would throw you. You connect into the hub beneath the centre, where nothing turns, and then a lift carries you up into the ring. On the way the wheel spins you up. Not all at once. Slowly, so that the body has time to believe it."*

*"And how much will I weigh there?"*

*"A third. As on Mars. The ring is a hundred metres from the centre and almost two revolutions a minute — enough that you do not float away, little enough that it does not pull at your head. And when you need more, you go to the small wheel in the middle: twenty minutes a day of full Earth, like going to a sauna."*

*"And if I skip it?"*

The navigator did not smile. *"Then you will come home with bones like a swallow's. The body here forgets that it is meant to be a body. That is why there is a schedule on the wall that is not a duty roster — it is a roster of the body."*

They were silent for a while and the child looked out of the window of the dock at the Earth. It was below and it was large. And on its blue edge, there where the sea touches the horizon, a tiny light could be seen moving over the surface so slowly that it could be mistaken for a wave.

*"That is the ark,"* said the child.

*"That is the ark."*

*"And can they see us?"*

*"They can. From below we are only a light to them too — and at night they see several of us at once: one red, one white, one gold, and one lantern."*

The child thought for a moment about how above and below people look at one another and both see only a little light — and then asked the thing that had troubled the whole crew from the moment the fleet came into being:

*"And who will actually throw us up here?"*

The navigator laughed, because it was the only question that could be answered without a poem.

*"Others, today. Today a place in orbit is bought like a plane ticket — rockets fly often, the price is falling, and if the fleet were to begin tomorrow, someone who does it better than we do would carry it. And that is good: a carpenter did not dig clay for bricks either when he could buy it from those who fire it all their lives."*

*"Then why do we build it at all?"*

*"Because buying a place is not the same as having a station."* He pointed at the ring. *"Someone will carry you up and someone will carry you down again. But no one will lend you a home. What you see here is not a rocket. It is **a place to which one can return** — and a place like that cannot be bought for a single use."*

In the evening, when they ate in the ring, the child sat by the wall where pictures of old stations hung: a small tin one with a single window, a larger one with great wings, a long one put together by two hostile countries and outlasting their quarrel. And beneath them, newly hung, hung an empty frame.

*"What will be there?"*

*"That house,"* said the navigator. *"When someone builds it, then its picture. Until then we leave it empty — because an empty frame is more honest than a drawing that pretends to be a photograph."*

## What it means

**The connection to Issobella is the moment when the fleet stops asking where it is going.** Until then it is transport. From that moment it is a node: a place where one can eat, sleep, repair, learn and return.

And two things this chapter says aloud:

1. **Others carry things up today — and that is not weakness.** Commercial transport to orbit is a real service; the first leg of the journey therefore need not be our own rocket. The fleet is not buying glory, it is buying a place.
2. **A place in orbit is not a home.** You buy a flight, not a place to return to. That is why the fleet does not begin with a rocket but with a **dock** — and a dock is a covenant, not hardware.

## From Scripture and tradition

- **Acts 17:26–28** — *"in him we live and move and have our being"*; a place that moves is not a contradiction in the corpus but an old image of relationship.
- **Corpus** — ring ~200 m across (r = 100 m), ~1.9–2.1 revolutions per minute, target **0.38 g** (Martian weight), short doses of 1 g in a small centrifuge ~20 min a day, spin-up from rest over 24 hours ([`canon/l6/Umela_Gravitace.md`](../../l6/Umela_Gravitace.md), [Issobella 03](../Issobella/03-Kolo-Ktere-Drzi-Zem.md)).
- **Corpus** — *"the ring is two hundred metres across and turns twice a minute… enough that you do not float away, little enough that it does not pull at your head"*; *"whoever neglects it comes home with bones like a swallow's"* ([Lumi 09](../Lumi/09-Strom-nad-Zemi.md)).
- **Corpus** — the gallery of old stations (Salyut, Skylab, Mir, ISS) as a heritage from which we learn what to copy and what never ([Issobella 05](../Issobella/05-Dedictvi-Stanic.md)).
- **Today's reality** — launching cargo and people to orbit is a commercial service (Falcon 9, Electron; Starship in its development years; suborbital tourism New Shepard / Virgin Galactic). Prices are falling, but **transport ≠ infrastructure**: a purchased flight does not create a place to which one can return.
- **History** — docking in orbit (Salyut, Mir, ISS) is always about one gesture: fuel, air, attitude and people in a single moment.
