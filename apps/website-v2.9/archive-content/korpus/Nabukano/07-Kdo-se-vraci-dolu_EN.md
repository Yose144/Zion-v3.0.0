# 7 · Who Comes Back Down

## The Story

On the dock hung a board and on the board names. It was not a crew list — it was a **list of returns**: who, when, through which window and with whom is coming back down. The names were written twice: once up here, once down in LUMI. When one entry changed and the other did not, it meant an error — and an error in a list of names is the most expensive thing there can be in a place like that.

*"Why twice?"* asked the child.

*"Because above and below must know the same thing,"* said the navigator. *"If only one place knew it, then that first place would not be a record. It would be a supposition."*

The child studied the board for a long time and then noticed that some names had a small dot beside them and others did not.

*"What does the dot mean?"*

*"That the person is staying one window longer. Not because they have to — because they want to. And because there is a place for them on the board until they return."*

*"And who comes back?"*

*"The one who has a reason,"* said the navigator, and it was plain that this was not a stock phrase but a rule with a few people behind it who had no reason and stayed above for one window, two, five — and then were afraid to return, because no one would be waiting for them at the gate below.

Then he sat down and said the part that no one on board wants to say aloud.

*"You get up by buying a ticket. That is the easy part today — you pay and someone carries you up. You get down only by someone waiting for you. And that cannot be bought. That is why the board is read aloud here every week: not so that we know who is where, but so that we remember that every name has someone at the other end holding it."*

*"And when no one is waiting for someone?"*

The navigator pointed to an empty row at the end of the board — the only place that had never been filled.

*"Then the waiting happens here. That is why it is empty. It is not a free place for the one who arrives. It is a place for the one who stays, so that there is someone to wait."*

In the evening they sang in the ring. Not out of piety — out of maintenance. In a closed world time without celebration dissolves like salt in water, and so the week was counted aloud, even though there was no Sunday there. And when the singing was done, the child asked the last thing he wanted to know that day:

*"And will I come back?"*

The navigator looked at him and said neither yes nor no.

*"You are neither here nor below,"* he said. *"You are the light that goes with the ship and with the lift, because it belongs to both ends. That is why you are not counted on the board. You cannot be entered as a return — you are entered as the reason why others return."*

## What it means

**The return is a harder engineering problem than the ascent** — and in this book it is also the main sentence about people. Up you are carried by a purchased place. Down you are carried by **someone who waits**. That is why the fleet does not measure success by the number of launches but by the number of names that have the same entry at both ends.

And the second consequence: the board is not administration, it is **liturgy**. It is read aloud so that the return does not become a statistic.

## From Scripture and tradition

- **Luke 15:20** — the father waits on the road; the return is not merely transport, it is a relationship that someone holds at the other end.
- **Heb 11:13–16** — people on the way who had a homeland only in promise; the corpus reads it as a rule of crews: whoever sets out must have somewhere to return to.
- **Corpus** — the roster of the body, cabins, *"what you think is yours; what you say is the crew's; what you do is the ship's"*, counting the week without a Sunday ([Lumi 09](../Lumi/09-Strom-nad-Zemi.md)).
- **Corpus** — human factors in a closed environment: isolation, confinement, sleep, conflict and its resolution ([Issobella 04](../Issobella/04-Posadka.md), [`canon/l6/Lidske_Faktory.md`](../../l6/Lidske_Faktory.md)).
- **Reality** — the physiology of weightlessness and partial gravity: loss of bone mass and muscle, the need for countermeasures; on long stays the return is always part of the mission, not its end.
