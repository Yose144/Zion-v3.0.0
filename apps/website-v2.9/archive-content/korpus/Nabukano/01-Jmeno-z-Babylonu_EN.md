# 1 · A Name from Babylon

## The Story

In the workshop above Galilee a boy who carried the wood shavings once asked: *"Why do ships bear the names of women and of saints? And why will the new one bear the name of a king?"*

The carpenter did not answer at once. He pointed to the keel, where the name had already been carved that every ship that had ever carried people home bore — *Kingdom of the Heart* — and then he took a chisel and carved a second beside it.

**Nebuchadnezzar.**

*"A name on a keel is not a title,"* he said. *"It is a confession. A ship is not named after the one who was strong. It is named after the one who was visited — or after the one who was humbled."*

And he told of a king who had everything. He had a city with walls of which it was said that no soldier could climb them; he had gardens he had built so that his wife from Media would not feel a stranger on the plain; he had a court in which people bowed until he entered. And yet twice a dream visited him that gave him nothing: once a statue of gold, silver, bronze, iron and clay, which a stone broke — a stone that tore itself loose from the mountain. The second time a tree — tall, growing to the sky, with leaves for all and fruit for everyone, and beneath it beasts at rest. And a voice said: *cut down that tree.*

The king asked his wise men and they were silent, because they knew the answer was about him. The man who answered him had come from a foreign land without an invitation: the tree is you. You will not be cut down, but you will be humbled, until you learn that what decides above is not the one who sits on the throne.

And then what happens to everyone who thinks their city is eternal happened. The king went up on the roof of the palace, said to himself: *all this I have built myself*, and before he reached the nearest step he was gone. For seven years he ate grass, his hair grew like eagle feathers and his nails like claws — and no one killed him, because in that land it occurred to no one that the king could be gone.

After seven years he *lifted his eyes to heaven*, and that was his whole repentance: not an apology, not a sacrifice, not a vow. Only that he stopped looking at himself.

*"And that is why the name,"* said the carpenter. *"Not because he was great. Because he learned to look up only after everything had been taken from him. The ship that carries him is not a praise of power. It is a note in the margin: this too happens. This one too comes back."*

The boy asked: *"And why do you put it there? You have never seen a king."*

*"I have,"* said the carpenter. *"I have seen the moment when people wanted to be above without going up with anyone. They built a tower so that the name would make itself. And only confusion was made."* He swept the shavings into his palm. *"The heavenly ship is not meant to carry anyone above people. It is meant to carry them up and back down again — to people. The difference is whether you look from a throne or from a deck."*

When the keel had dried, the crew tried to say the name aloud and could not manage it. It was long, official, from another language and another land. So they shortened it, as is done with every name that is too large: **Nabukano.**

*"Nebuchadnezzar is a king,"* they say between decks. *"Nabukano is a ship."*

## What it means

The name of this fleet is not admiration. It is a **reminder of direction**: one may go up only when the path leads back down. The king who looked at his city from a height fell ill with that very height. The ship that bears his name is meant to be its opposite — a place where no one stands above anyone, because there is no palace roof there, only a dock and a lantern.

And a second thing the book says outright: **a name alone is not enough.** Babylon had gardens, and no one who had been carried there was allowed to stay in them. Therefore the fleet does not bear only the name of the one who learned to lift his eyes — it also bears the name on the keel that is the same for all four: *Kingdom of the Heart*.

## From Scripture and tradition

- **Dan 2** — the dream of the statue of metals, broken by a stone "cut out of the mountain without hands".
- **Dan 4:10–37** — the tree growing to the sky, the voice that commands it be cut down, "seven times", and the king's eyes lifted to heaven; **4:37** — *"those who walk in pride can be brought low."*
- **2 Kings 25** — the same king burned the temple in Jerusalem and carried its inhabitants away; his name on the keel of a ship that carries people home is also his counterweight.
- **Gen 11:1–9** — the tower that wanted to be on high without people: an image of a path that goes up and does not return.
- **Tradition** — the hanging gardens as one of the seven wonders of the world; **history** speaks of them cautiously: in Babylon archaeology has found no trace of them, and some scholars place them in Nineveh under Sennacherib (the hypothesis of Stephanie Dalley). The book keeps only what is humanly attested: the desire to make a landscape in which someone from a foreign land would feel at home.
- **Corpus** — *"On the keel of every ship that ever carried people home stood the same name"* ([Kingdom of the Heart, ch. 9](../KralovstviSrdce/09-Flotila-se-Schazi.md)).
