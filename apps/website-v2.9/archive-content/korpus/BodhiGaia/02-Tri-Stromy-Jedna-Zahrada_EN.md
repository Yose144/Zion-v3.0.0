# BODHI GAIA — Chapter 2: Three trees, one garden
## Root, trunk and crown — the L5 Pentagram as architecture, not decoration

> *"A tree does not grow in one place at once. First a root in the dark, then a trunk that withstands the wind, and only at the end a crown that bears fruit. Whoever wants a crown without a root gets a flower in a vase."*

---

## The Story

From the seed Sita planted below the house of Lumi, not one tree grew.

When the pilgrims returned from the west after a year — from the bridges of WARP and from the golden womb of Hiranyagarbha — they found three saplings on the slope. They grew from one root, but each in a different direction: one towards the sea, one towards the mountains, and one towards the water that ran down the slope into the bay.

Sita was nowhere. In her place sat by the saplings **Hanuman** — the Servant of Life, the one who in Salt of This Earth built a bridge and in the First World of Oasis gave tithes to the poor. He brought water in two buckets and watered all three alike.

*"Why three?"* asked the carpenter.

*"Because the land is not one,"* answered Hanuman. *"Down there by the sea the soil is sandy, salty and windy. Whoever wants to live there must be able to work clay and read waves. Up in the mountains the soil is made of volcano ash — warm, fertile, but silent; whoever wants to live there must be able to keep silent. And there in the bay the soil belongs to no one for half the year, because the sea takes it; whoever wants to live there must be able to sail without a map."*

*"So three gardens."*

*"Three **places**. One garden. Root, trunk, crown. Whoever comes for the first time comes to the sea — there is the gate, work, food and people who will teach them to hold a hoe. Whoever needs more than a hoe will go to the mountains — there is the silence from which people return different. And whoever already knows who they are will go to the water — there they learn how to steer a canoe in which no one is captain."*

The carpenter looked around at the three saplings. They were small. None of them reached even to his waist.

*"And when will there be a tree?"*

Hanuman set the buckets on the ground. *"When you stop asking that question and start carrying water."*

That winter the pilgrims understood for the first time what Sita's sentence about witnesses had meant. The sapling by the sea survived because people came to water it every day — it was closest to the house. The sapling in the mountains survived because it rained there. The sapling by the water survived only barely: twice the water took it and twice someone planted it again a little higher.

In spring the carpenter carved three small strokes into the beam above the door, beside the seed. Root, trunk, crown. Earth, fire, water.

## What it means

**L5 Free World is not one project. It is a network of physical communities that share protocols but differ in place, role and energy.** The original trio of three trees grew into a pentagram of five nodes — the Heart (Bohemia) and Akasha (Lanka) were added. The five nodes form the **L5 Pentagram**:

| Node | Element | Tree archetype | Role in the network | Place | Energy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Genesis Garden** | Earth | **Root** | Base Camp — the entrance gate, farm, work, pilgrim route | Sabacheira, Tomar, Portugal | Movement, albergue, morning light |
| **Dharma Temple** | Fire | **Trunk** | Sanctuary — silence, practice, education, depth | La Palma, Canary Islands | The calm of a volcano, night, stars |
| **Te Pīko Ora** | Water | **Crown** | Fulfilment — abundance, integration, marine permaculture | Raiatea, French Polynesia | Flow, eternal noon |
| **Golden Republic Bohemia** | Air | **Heart** | Governance lab — a circle of decision, Czech wisdom (salt, bridge, the Golden Bull, Přemysl the Ploughman, Libuše, Charles IV), the Golden Republic protocol | Bohemia, Czech Republic | A bridge, a bridge between tradition and experiment |
| **Bodhi Lanka** | Akasha / Ether | **Ether** | Love — Rama-Sita, Sri Maha Bodhi (the oldest living tree, 288 BCE), the Rama Setu bridge, the Bhakti protocol, Ayurveda | Sri Lanka | Love, bhakti, the oldest root |

Why precisely these five:

1. **The root must be first and nearest.** The Genesis Garden is in the EU, reachable by car and plane, with a mild climate and a low legal barrier (the Portuguese `Associação`). Whoever is to enter L5 for the first time needs a place where one can arrive for a week, sleep in a tent, dig a bed and leave. That is why Genesis is the *Base Camp*.
2. **The trunk gives shape.** Dharma Temple is not a farm with meditation added. It is the place where **silence becomes a protocol**: a 21-day silent retreat for every keeper before taking on a role, no electronics in the Dharma Circle, 48 hours of reflection before every major decision. A shape that holds when the wind comes.
3. **The crown comes last — and must not be promised earlier.** Te Pīko Ora is the furthest, legally the most complicated (foreigners in Polynesia cannot directly own land) and culturally the most sensitive (sacred Raiatea, the marae Taputapuātea). Therefore it is marked **Vision / Preparation** and its phases begin last (2027–2028). Whoever sold it as a finished paradise would be lying.
4. **The heart joins.** Golden Republic Bohemia is a governance lab — a circle of decision where Czech wisdom (salt, bridge, the Golden Bull, Přemysl the Ploughman, Libuše, Charles IV) meets experiment. The Golden Republic protocol tests how one decides when no one is above another. Air is the element that carries a voice — and a voice is what a community needs so as not to stiffen.
5. **Ether is the oldest root.** Bodhi Lanka is the place where the tree beneath which the Buddha awoke stands to this day — Sri Maha Bodhi, planted in 288 BCE, the oldest living tree of documented origin. Rama-Sita and the Rama Setu bridge are the memory of love as a bridge. The Bhakti protocol and Ayurveda bring what no other node has: a continuity of care thousands of years old. Akasha is the element that holds all the others — the space in which trees grow.

### One root, five places — shared code

All five nodes implement **the same basic protocols** (in detail in [chapter 6](./06-Protokoly-Zeme.md)): a Guardian Node with a 90/10 split, a seed library, a Medical Table, a LoRa/Meshtastic mesh, a sociocratic DAO, conscious admission of members and a vow of care. That is "one root": whoever learns governance in Genesis understands governance on La Palma. Whoever exchanges seeds in Portugal can exchange them on Raiatea. And whoever learns the circle of decision in Bohemia understands the circle in Lanka.

### The same phases for every node

| Phase | Name | Goal | Typical length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Seed** | Legal basis, first keepers, access to land | 3–12 months |
| 1 | **Roots** | Energy, water, basic food, shelter | 6–18 months |
| 2 | **Community** | Permanent residents, governance, ZION node | 12–24 months |
| 3 | **Network** | Connection with the other L5 nodes, shared protocols | 18–36 months |
| 4 | **Radiance** | Retreats, education, international guests | 24–60 months |

> **Hanuman's sentence "when you start carrying water"** is in technical language M5 from the Execution Charter: a phase is not advanced by a declaration but by evidence — a contract for the land, a photograph with provenance and consent, a budget, an on-chain reference and an independent report.

### A mystical map (only as an image)

In [`docs/Zohar/01-SEFIROT-VRSTVY.md`](../../Zohar/01-SEFIROT-VRSTVY.md) L5 corresponds to the sefirah **Yesod** — *Foundation*, the generative connection between vision (Keter) and manifestation (Malkhut). "Without Yesod, ZION would remain in the cloud. With Yesod it becomes the land under your feet." The vow of care of this sefirah reads: *"I vow to care for this land as I would care for my own body."* It is an image, not a rule of consensus.
