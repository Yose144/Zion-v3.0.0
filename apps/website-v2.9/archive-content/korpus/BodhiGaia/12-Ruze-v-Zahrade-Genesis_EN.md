## Intersection · L5 — what happens when a literary seed enters a physical project and refuses to pretend it has already grown

> *"Soil does not accept symbols. It accepts only what someone comes back to care for."*

## The Story

On the morning after the child with the lantern went off beyond the horizon — by the same path she had come — Ana rose before dawn, as every day, and went first for water and then to look at the beds.

At the edge of the bed, near the place where water ran down from the barrel, only fresh earth remained. Nothing more. No trace of a miracle, no shoot, only loosened soil at whose edge remained the prints of small palms and knees.

Ana bent down and tidied the edge of the bed — not for beauty, but so that the wind would not scatter what lay there beneath the ground.

Later, when work was being divided on the circle of stones, a guest came to her — a person who had come for a week with a hoe and a telephone full of pictures. He had heard overnight how things go in the Garden.

"They say you planted something special yesterday," he said. "Something like a sacred rose. That a girl brought a relic — what was her name — that priestess."

"The child with the lantern," said Ana.

"So it is true? Is it that sacred rose?"

Ana looked at the bare earth and then at the guest — for a long time, the way you look at someone who has time.

"We planted a seed," she said. "An ordinary one, small, brown. A child brought it in a vessel that looked empty the whole way. We are not planting a shrine — we are planting a possibility that will have to defend itself every day there is drought."

"But is it a special seed? From a special garden?"

"It is a seed from a garden where someone learned to ask before touching," said Ana. "I will say no more — and not because it would be a secret, but because it is not evidence. A seed is a promise, not a certificate. What grows from it we will know only when it grows — or does not grow."

"And if it is a rose — will it be that woman's rose? Miriam's?"

Ana shook her head. "It will be a rose. Not a woman, not a dynasty, not a brand. I refused the label — and I will refuse it again, even if you bring it. Things we give names instead of care die beneath labels."

The guest wanted to ask more — but Ana was already standing and taking the jug, because the tomato beds needed water sooner than questions.

"If it grows, we will have a rose," said Ana. "If it does not grow, we will have the truth. Either is more than a legend passed off as evidence."

The guest went that day to weed the next bed — and when in the evening he asked whether "that rose" would have its own world in the lens, Ana told him that the mirror shows only who comes back here to care. A picture of a bed is an image; a bed is work.

In the evening, when the work was done and the circle of stones had emptied, Ana took the work book — the same one in which she recorded the counts of seedlings, the water records and the price of timber — and opened the page with the date.

She did not write down a miracle. She did not write down a rite. She did not write down a name.

She wrote only two things: *seed planted — edge of the bed by the barrel* and *check in a month*. And beneath that, like everything else in the book, in small letters, without glory:

*water only after the meal.*

The Garden did not need to know that the seed was exceptional. It needed someone to water it — and to come for the answer, not for the story.

## What it means

This chapter stands at the **intersection** — the place where two books meet that have nothing to explain to each other, and yet need each other:

1. **The Rose × Bodhi Gaia.** *The Story of the Rose* is a book of relationship — care without ownership, boundaries, freedom. *Bodhi Gaia* is a book of soil — evidence, water, work, time. Their intersection poses a single question: what happens when an image enters the ground? The answer: only what someone comes back to care for.
2. **A story is not a physical act.** The epilogue and this scene are MYTH. To plant a seed in a story takes a sentence; in the ground it takes consent, a season and hands. The text plants nothing — and the book nowhere claims it does.
3. **OASIS is a mirror, and its Rose lore must carry MYTH.** The world `GENESIS_GARDEN` may tell of the rose — but it must say of itself that it is literature, not evidence of a plant or of infrastructure on site.
4. **Real care is return and evidence, not a photograph of one day.** A physical pilot is not judged by the day of planting but by survival checks — and even then it remains a small, honest proof, not a symbol.

## The proposal of the pilot GENESIS-ROSE-001

Literature planted the seed. Physically no one has yet — and if that ever happens, it must stand as a **documented event, not as a legend**. That is why the proposal of a micro-pilot exists: [`pilots/GENESIS-ROSE-001.md`](./pilots/GENESIS-ROSE-001.md): a decision framework with four gates (consent and place → botany and safety → care → finance and claims), a record packet, Day 0 evidence and checks on Day 30 / 90 / 365.

**State: HORIZON / NOT STARTED.** The proposal lists no land, no permit, no purchase of a plant, no planting, no survival and no impact. It is not an M5 exit gate, marketing, a ceremony or fundraising — it is the discipline of how a small physical act should be carried out and documented, if it were ever to occur.

## The boundary of this chapter

- **No sacred status.** The rose is not a relic, the plant receives no spiritual authority, and no shrine is built in the garden.
- **No identity of Mary/Miriam.** The plant is not a person, a bloodline, a reincarnation, an avatar or a continuation of `18_MERIAM_ROSE.md`.
- **No claim on the fund.** No L5/DAO spend is approved, proposed or implied; no token, NFT or reward.
- **No claim of physical impact.** Until a Day 0 packet exists, the planting has not happened; survival is documented by D30/D90/D365 checks, not by a story; and even documented persistence alone does not fulfil the M5 exit gate.
