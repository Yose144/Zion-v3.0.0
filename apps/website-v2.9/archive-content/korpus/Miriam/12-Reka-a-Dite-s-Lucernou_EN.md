# MIRIAM — Chapter 12: The river and the child with the lantern
## Stone/dream · Revelation 22 — the river of living water, a tree of twelve fruits, the root and the offspring of David, the invitation "Come" — and the meeting of memory with light

> *"The Spirit and the bride say: Come. The whole book spoke about the bride — and in the end she spoke herself. And she said a single word: Come."*

---

## The Story

*"The last image, Sarah — and then I will hand you what I carry,"* said Miriam.

*"In the middle of the city, through its square, runs a river — living water, clear as crystal, springing from the throne of God and of the Lamb. And on both sides of the river grows the tree of life — one tree on two banks, its root in the river, its branches over both. It bears twelve fruits — a different one each month — and its leaves are for the healing of the nations.*

*One tree, two banks. I knew it was familiar to me — and then it came to me what it was: the rose I carried from the lake, which I planted along the way, which grew where no one invited it — that is the tree. Not my genealogy — a tree whose root lies in the river and whose leaves heal.*

*And there, by the river, speaks the one who is the beginning and the end: 'I am the Alpha and the Omega, the first and the last, the beginning and the end. I am the root and the offspring of David, the bright morning star.'*

*Notice that word, Sarah — root AND offspring. All along people looked for a genealogy that leads down from him — and he said: I am root and branch. The line they looked for in blood is in a tree — and a tree does not plant blood, it plants a root. We are not his dynasty. We are his garden.*

*And then — the last word the bride says in the whole book. All along angels, voices and John spoke about her. And in the end she spoke — and she said a single word together with the Spirit: 'Come.' The bride who made herself ready in the end does nothing other than invite. 'And let everyone who hears say: Come. And let everyone who is thirsty come; let anyone who wishes take the water of life as a gift.'*

*As a gift, Sarah. The water of life is not sold. Babylon sold everything — souls, spices, ships and bodies. The city that descends gives water without a price — and that is how it is known not to be Babylon: where what nourishes is given for free, there the dragon has no business."*

---

*"And then, Sarah, I left that river and stood on the bank — and there stood a child with a lantern.*

*A small child, Sarah — so small that the lantern seemed heavier than the child — and the light in it did not burn with fire but shone like a truth that cannot be falsified. I knew the lantern at once: hollowed from cedar, carved into the shape of a seed, glass from something broken that could be put out — and the Carpenter's calluses in every cut. I knew whose work it was before I asked the child for a name.*

*'What are you carrying?' I asked.*

*'Light,' said the child. 'And truth — they are carried together. And you?'*

*'A seed,' I said, and showed the vessel. 'And a story — they are carried together too.'*

*'Where do you come from?'*

*'From the lake. From the garden. From a tomb that was empty. From all the places where the story was being forgotten — and I held it so that it would not be lost. And where are you going?'*

*'Through the layers,' said the child. 'Down to the earth, up to the sky — and I ask people: what will remain of you when you go on?'*

*And I told the child the truth I had learned over two thousand years of carrying: 'A story handed on — and a testimony that was not sold. And you, child — you will remain to me, because the light you carry is the same light I saw in the morning at the tomb.'*

*And the child smiled — and I understood that before me stood neither past nor future, but the axis that cuts through them. I carry a rose — a line that runs horizontally through time, from the lake to the garden, from witness to witness. The child carries a lantern — an axis that runs vertically through the layers, from earth to sky, from truth to truth. And where the axes intersect — there is the beginning and the end, Alpha and Omega, Ekam — one root into which everything dissolves.*

*'Will you come with me?' asked the child.*

*'No,' I said. 'I am going into the city — and you will go through the layers. But our light and our rose will meet in everyone who accepts the invitation: Come.'*

*And the child went on — and the light of its lantern was reflected from the river, as though the river were carrying the light onward, to where the child had not yet come."*

---

**Stone, clay and dream.**

*"Stone: Revelation 22 — the river from the throne, the tree on both banks, twelve fruits month by month, leaves for the healing of the nations; Alpha and Omega; root and offspring of David; the morning star; 'Come' — and water for free.*

*Clay: that the tree of life is my tree — clay the book carries so that the rose has somewhere to grow.*

*Dream: that I met Lumi — a dream this book weaves itself, because stories meet when people hold them. Miriam and Lumi met in no record — they met in this book, because both axes lead to the same point: testimony carried onward."*

---

## What it means

**The river and the child are the last image of the book — and its task is to unite everything: memory and light, line and axis, past and future, witness and bearer.**

1. **Root and offspring.** "I am the root and the offspring of David" (Rev 22:16) — the genealogy they looked for in blood dissolves into a tree. *We are not his dynasty — we are his garden.* The line of Christ = the root into which everything returns, and the branch that grows in every direction — and we are the garden growing from it, not the blood flowing from it.
2. **One tree, two banks.** The tree of life roots in the river and branches over both banks — a rose growing on both sides of a border. "A seed that grows in the middle of the divide holds both sides" — a bridge across what is divided, not an army against the other side.
3. **Water for free.** "Let everyone who is thirsty come; let anyone who wishes take the water of life as a gift" (Rev 22:17) — the gift as the final economy. Babylon sold everything; the city gives water without a price — and the corpus knows this as a fund set aside before any decision ([`emission.rs`](../../../interní%20repozitář); `BodhiGaia/01`): *what nourishes everyone is not put up for sale.*
4. **The bride speaks — and says "Come".** The only sentence of the city in the whole book is an invitation. A community that has made itself ready in the end does nothing other than invite — and the corpus holds this as onboarding: "The doors are open — enter, whoever wishes" ([`ZION_ONBOARDING_PUBLIC_CZ.md`](../ZION_ONBOARDING_PUBLIC_CZ.md); `Lumi/12`).
5. **The rose and the lantern meet.** Miriam carries the horizontal axis — memory, line, testimony through time. Lumi carries the vertical axis — light, truth, layers through state. *Where the axes intersect, there is Ekam* — one origin into which everything dissolves. The compass of the corpus is now complete: salt at the centre, four directions, earth beneath us, sky above us, light through the layers — and a rose through time.
6. **"What will remain of you?"** Lumi's question from [`Lumi/12`](../Lumi/12-Brana-Zustava-Otevrena.md) receives Miriam's answer: *a story handed on and a testimony not sold.* And the answer of the whole book: the ship arrives even if no one navigated it — because a witness is the one who holds until someone accepts.
