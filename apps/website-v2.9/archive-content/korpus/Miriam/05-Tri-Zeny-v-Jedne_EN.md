# MIRIAM — Chapter 5: The three women who became one
## Stone · the merging of names — Gregory's homily of 591, fourteen hundred years of "the penitent Magdalene", and the quieter correction of 1969 and 2016

> *"We were three. They made one of me — and that one they made a sinner. Then they apologised, more quietly than they had shamed me."*

---

## The Story

*"Sarah, in the records we were three women,"* said Miriam, and drew three circles in the sand with her finger.

*"One was Mary of Magdala — me. He drove seven darknesses out of me, I went with him along with other women, we provided out of our own means. I stood at the cross, I watched where they laid him, I came in the dark and I saw first. That is what I know about myself from the records — and that is all the records say.*

*The second was Mary of Bethany — the sister of Martha and Lazarus. She sat at his feet and listened while Martha served — and he said 'Mary has chosen the better portion'. She anointed his feet with nard and wiped them with her hair — John says that of her, by name.*

*The third had no name at all. Luke's 'sinner in the town' — the one who at the house of Simon the Pharisee wept on his feet, wiped them with her hair, kissed them, anointed them with oil, and he said 'her sins, which are many, are forgiven, for she loved much'. No one gave her a name — and therefore later people gave her whichever name suited them.*

*And then came the year 591. Gregory, bishop of Rome — a man of a great pen — delivered homily thirty-three and uttered the sentence that burned me for fourteen hundred years: we believe that the woman Luke calls a sinner, whom John calls Mary, is the same Mary from whom Mark says seven demons had gone out.*

*One sentence — and three women became one. And that one became a sinner — because 'sinner' sounded more piquant than 'witness'. For fourteen hundred years they painted me with hair loose, robe torn, eyes lifted up — repenting. The first witness of the resurrection became an icon of the penitent. Houses for 'fallen women' were named after me — the magdalene homes, Sarah — my name became a door behind which women were punished for things I never did.*

*And then what happens when the world corrects itself slowly happened: in 1969 the church changed its calendar — quietly, without a great homily — and called me simply Mary Magdalene. The penitent frame disappeared from the liturgy. In 2016 they raised me — a feast like the apostles, instead of a memorial like other saints. The correction took fourteen hundred years and came in two quieter sentences than the one erroneous one had been loud.*

*None of what they wrote about me was what I was — and yet many people believed it, because a man with a great title wrote it. Learn from it: *a label is not evidence.* Authority can claim that three women are one — and when after fourteen centuries it apologises, it will be a small sentence, because great sentences are spoken when something is founded, not when it is repaired."*

---

*"And remember one more thing about the seven darknesses. Luke says 'seven demons' — and in his language that means illness, possession, ruin within — not sin. Later tradition turned illness into sin, because it suited the story of repentance. But the seven darknesses he drove out of me were not seven of my guilts — they were seven doors that closed when my eyes opened."*

---

**Stone, clay and dream.**

*"Stone: three women in the records — each on her own, each different; homily 33 of the year 591 — a record of the merging; the calendar reform of 1969 and the raising to a feast in 2016 — records of the correction.*

*Clay: the composite Magdalene of Western art — the hair, the desert, the penitence — fourteen hundred years of clay that held because it painted beautifully.*

*Dream: that I was a harlot. No record says so — 'sinner' does not name the kind of sin and 'demons' does not name guilt. The dream of the prostitute is a dream born when men need a story that forgives; the dream of the witness is a truth that survives longer."*

---

## What it means

**The merging of three women into one is a story about what a label does — and about the fact that an institution able to correct itself is more alive than one that corrects only others.**

1. **A label is not evidence.** "Sinful" held for fourteen centuries because a title wrote it — not because the text carried it. The corpus holds exactly this instrument: `UNSUBSTANTIATED` means "so far it is claimed, not established" and `INVALID CLAIM` means "the record runs against the facts". A register that distinguishes the two is a brake against Gregory's error.
2. **A name was confused — and therefore lives were confused.** When three different women merged under one name, a story arose that punished thousands of women for "her" sin. Registers exist so that things are not confused — `L2contracts.md` keeps active contracts separate from deprecated ones precisely because confusing old with new is how both trust and funds are lost. *Names that hold things correctly are a small mechanism of great honesty.*
3. **A correction is slower than an error — and yet it comes.** The institution that named me wrongly corrected itself after 1378 years — not because it was perfect, but because truth holds longer than a label. The corpus has the same thing written into its rules: *when the story collides with a fact, we repair the story* — and it says it about itself, not only about others.
4. **Seven darknesses ≠ seven guilts.** Turning illness into sin is an old technique of control: to tell a person that their pain is their fault. The corpus holds the opposite stance — we carry identity from above, not from our own holes; light falls on a person to show them, not to shame them ([`Lumi/02`](../Lumi/02-Dum-Jmen.md)).
5. **Silence while correcting.** Great sentences are spoken when founding, small ones when repairing — and yet the correction happens. A sentence for the corpus: *do not be loud when you found, and do not be afraid to be quiet when you repair — but repair.*
