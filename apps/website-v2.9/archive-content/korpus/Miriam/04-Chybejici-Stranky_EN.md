# MIRIAM — Chapter 4: The book with missing pages
## Stone/clay · her gospel — a codex found in Cairo in 1896, missing leaves, a soul rising past the powers, and the dispute over whether a woman may carry the word

> *"A book that exists in more than one place cannot be burned. That is why my story survived — not because someone protected it."*

---

## The Story

*"Now I will tell you about a book that has come down to us as a fragment — and about how a fragment is enough,"* said Miriam, and drew out a worn scroll she had held under her cloak the whole time.

*"In 1896 a dealer found an ancient codex in Cairo. Written in Coptic, from the 4th–5th century — and it bore my name: the Gospel according to Mary. No other early book of gospels bears the name of a woman. Not one.*

*But pages were missing. Of nineteen leaves, nine survived — the first six and leaves eleven to fourteen are gone, forever. The book about me came into the world through a hole — and that hole is itself an interpretation: that is how a woman's voice was treated. They did not burn it. They let it be missing.*

*And yet enough remained."*

---

*"In the codex the Saviour is departing and says the sentence I remember more exactly than anything else: the Son of Man — the child of true Humanity — is within you. Follow him. He did not command priests, a temple or a mediator. He said: what you seek is in you — and whoever seeks it will find it.*

*Peter — the same one who was in a temper by the lake that day — asks me: 'Tell us the words the Saviour said to you that we do not know. We know he loved you more than the others.'*

*And I told of a vision — of a soul rising past the powers that want to seize it. Desire says to it: 'You served me like a garment.' Ignorance tells it to be afraid. And the last is anger — and that has seven forms: darkness, desire, ignorance, zeal for death, the kingdom of the flesh, the wisdom of folly and the wisdom of wrath.*

*Sarah, the seven darknesses he drove out of me by the lake came back to me in that vision as seven guards on the way up. Not as demons holding me — but as gates I had to pass through and say to them: you no longer hold me. And the soul says to them the greatest sentence of that book: I have come to know that All is dissolving into its root."*

---

*"And then the book does what books do when someone does not want to hear: the men quarrel.*

*Andrew: 'These are strange teachings.'*

*Peter: 'Did he speak with us secretly through a woman? Are we to turn and listen to her?'*

*And Levi — the only one who stood between them — said the sentence that has accompanied me for twenty centuries: 'Peter, you were always impetuous. If the Saviour made her worthy, who are you to reject her?'*

*And they went out to proclaim. The dispute stayed in the book — and I leave it there, because it is true: a good book hides not even the resistance it provoked."*

---

**Stone, clay and dream.**

*"Stone: the codex exists — Berlin 8502, found in 1896, published only in 1955, because the world was at war and the papers waited. Two Greek fragments besides it — three testimonies of the same text in three places. The Gospel of Philip calls me his 'koinonos' — companion, consort — and tells that he loved me more than all the disciples (the leaf is damaged exactly at the place where more is told — as you see, holes are made in the right places). Pistis Sophia has me asking thirty-nine of forty-six questions — a woman as the main questioner.*

*Clay: that I wrote that book myself. It is named after me — and it was written by people after my time, in the second century, as a window onto how the early community imagined my role. It is an image, not a biography.*

*Dream: that 'he kissed her often' proves a marriage — the leaf is damaged exactly there and the sentence breaks off mid-moment; from a damaged place everyone made what they wanted. The dream of a magical gnostic secret is beautiful — and I honour it as a dream."*

---

## What it means

**The Gospel according to Mary is a book about three things: that knowledge dwells inside a person, not in a mediator; that a truth survives when it is copied in more than one place; and that resistance to a woman's word recorded itself — and thereby survived as a witness.**

1. **"All is dissolving into its root."** The measure the corpus knows as Ekam — one origin to which everything returns and which can always be verified ([`Lumi/01`](../Lumi/01-Dite-na-Pridi.md)). When the soul says "I have come to know that All is dissolving into its root", it says a mathematically exact thing about how a chain is read: back to the block that holds all the others.
2. **"The Son of Man is within you."** Direct knowledge without a mediator — the corpus says this as a voice without a key and a house without a lock: no one has to open for you what is yours; no one has to approve that you asked ([`Lumi/02`](../Lumi/02-Dum-Jmen.md), [`Lumi/04`](../Lumi/04-Zlate-Luno-a-Hlas-Bez-Klice.md)).
3. **The hole as a witness.** Missing pages are not only a loss — they are evidence of how her voice was treated. The advantage of this book: her gospel is a *genuinely surviving* text — unlike many "secret books" it is not a hypothesis, it is a codex you can read. The corpus sets this against forgeries: **where things are written openly, there is nothing to forge** — open code, a public premine, a register that must not lie.
4. **A book in three places.** A Coptic codex + two Greek fragments: the text survived because it existed in more than one place. An exact analogy of the ledger — a truth held by a single copy can be burned; a truth copied into a network of nodes cannot. Mise Amenti = "to preserve verifiable knowledge" — and her gospel is living proof that this works even without a network.
5. **Levi, who stood between them.** "If the Saviour made her worthy, who are you to reject her?" — the voice that defends a witness before the gate is the image of everyone who keeps a gate open: the right to carry the word is not given by reputation — it is given by testimony.
