> *"Whoever does not read the stories of the ships that sank will build a ship that sinks differently."*

## The Story

In the camp of the printers of truth stood a tent they called the **Hall of Names**. On the walls hung no pictures of glory — there hung sheets with stories. Each sheet bore the name of a station that flew, or at least tried to.

The first sheet bore the name **Salyut**. *"The year 1971,"* read Korma. *"The first station in the world. Small, modest — and yet it invented something we still use today: an interior divided according to life, not according to technology. Work, food, hygiene, free time, sleep. It was designed by an architect named Galina Balashova — a woman who understood that a machine without a home is only a cage."*

On the sheet there was one more sentence, which he read quietly: *"On board Salyut grew the first space garden — OAZA. And when the first crew was returning home, a valve opened prematurely and all three died. A technical trifle. Three lives. Write it on your table wherever you build: banality kills as reliably as catastrophe."*

The second sheet bore the name **Skylab**. *"An American laboratory that burned up in the atmosphere in 1979. The lesson: even a station dies. Nothing you build above the Earth stays there forever — and a plan for how a thing ends is part of the plan for how it begins."*

The third sheet bore two names at once: **Power Tower** and **Dual Keel**. *"Great designs that were never built. Power Tower — a giant cross where the habitat lay far from the centre of mass and the experiments suffered from vibration. Dual Keel — a double keel, the most stable design ever drawn. Too expensive. The lesson: perfection that no one can afford is not perfection."*

The fourth sheet bore the name **Freedom**. *"The station that was not born. A budget sliced by sixty per cent, a crew reduced from eight to four, a promised launch postponed indefinitely. The lesson: a station does not die of physics — it dies of budgets and pride."*

And the fifth sheet bore the name **ISS**. *"The year 1998. The Russian module Zarya and the American module Unity joined in orbit — two worlds that had previously measured each other with weapons. Since then people have lived there continuously — for more than twenty-five years. The lesson: a station built over decades is not built by one state. It is built by agreement."*

*"And where is the sheet for Issobella?"* asked a newcomer.

Korma pointed to an empty place on the wall. *"Only what has happened gets written there. For now there hangs only a question: what of these stories will we repeat — and what will we do differently for the first time?"*

## What it means

**The history of stations is the one library Issobella must not skip.** The research bundle `L6data/Histori.md` is the most extensive L6 document — and that is no accident. L6 does not refactor physics; it studies **mistakes**.

The five sheets of the hall of names correspond to five lessons that history actually offers:

1. **Salyut (1971–):** the interior is the architecture of life, not the casing of a machine. Balashova's zones — work, food, hygiene, free time, sleep — are a model to this day. And Soyuz 11 reminds us: a banal technical trifle killed three people.
2. **Skylab (1979):** a station has an end. The deorbit/termination plan is part of the design from the first sketch.
3. **Power Tower / Dual Keel (1984–85):** microgravity stability is a physical, not a political question — a habitat far from the centre of mass = bad science. And the most perfect design that is not built is still only paper.
4. **Freedom (1987–91):** "very small for too much, far too late" (Buzz Aldrin). Budget slicing changed the design faster than the engineers could draw.
5. **ISS (1998–):** the result is not the genius of one, but the agreement of many — including former rivals. And the 1988 agreement says aloud what should hold for L6 as well: the station is used **for peaceful purposes**.

From this library the book derives the L6 motto: **we do not go faster, cheaper and better — we go slower, more truthfully and together.**
