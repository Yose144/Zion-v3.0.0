# Chapter 5 — The Keeper of the Tree
## Seat V · akasha — Bodhi Lanka; a woman who teaches songs of care beneath the oldest branch — Ana from the sea

> *"We have five places. Each different. And each holds a piece of what the whole body needs."*

---

## The Story

The island she came from is a drop in the ocean — and yet it holds within it the oldest tree humanity knows to have grown when other trees were already legend.

Sri Lanka. Anuradhapura — the city where **Sri Maha Bodhi** stands: a fig planted in the year 288 before our era, grown from a branch of the tree beneath which the Buddha attained awakening. The most documented living tree in the world — for 2 300 years hands have cared for it that change, while the work does not.

The woman who came from there laid a branch on the table — not a leaf, a branch of **bodhi**, and although she carried the shape of the tree that taught awakening, she let herself be known differently: **Ana from the sea** — that is what they called her from Genesis, where she once sat and said the sentence that became the sentence of the circle:

*"We have five places. Each different. And each holds a piece of what the whole body needs."*

The sea she took her name from is the Indian one — the ocean that joins the lagoon with the island of trees, and which is the home of the third hull of the fleet — **the ship that is not yet** (the patroness María de las Nieves already waits for it). Ana took her name from the sea but has her home by the tree — because in the circle a woman is not defined by her water but by her holding.

---

Bodhi Lanka holds in the corpus the place where **5 % becomes growth beneath the oldest branch**. Not a temple — a garden where songs of care are learned: bhakti — devotion, which is not learned from books but from the hand of a tree into the hand of a person.

Ana sits beneath Sri Maha Bodhi and teaches what is learned only where a tree has outlived states: **no leaf is yours — every leaf is borrowed from a branch you were not given to own but to water.** And when the children ask how you recognise that someone is a keeper, she answers them with a question in return:

*"You will know by whether their hands wither when the tree has no water — or whether they pour the water over themselves."*

Akasha — the element that in the series means the space in which everything else grows — is the element of Bodhi Lanka: not air, not ether, but **the space of holding**. And Ana holds akasha by holding a relationship in it: between island and bridge, between tree and hands, between worlds that will never meet unless someone joins them.

---

Her sentence "we have five places" is repeated in the circle as her signature — and at the same time as her most exact truth. When one garden wanted to hold "her" world separately, Ana brought a bodhi branch and laid it beside her seat:

*"A tree does not have five places. A tree has one tree — and five branches. When a branch says it is the tree, it bends to the ground. When the tree says it is five branches, it bends to cracking. It is held only in this way: the branch knows it is a branch — and holds."*

And because her island is a bridge between islands — between East and West, between land and water — she also holds a world that does not belong to one place: **a world that is a bridge**. Not because the bridge would be hers — because without a bridge the lands do not connect.

---

The light narrowed.

The lantern showed Sri Lanka as it is today: Sri Maha Bodhi **stands and lives** — LIVE geography, 2 300 years of care, documented; Bodhi Lanka as an L5 world — **a project sheet, a community in documentation**, an intent of allocation; the pilot and the garden — HORIZON. Ana from the sea is an archetype in the corpus (the list of guides, the Genesis scene) — MYTH, not a person.

*"It is small,"* said Lumi.

*"It is small,"* nodded the Keeper of the Tree. *"But the tree I sit beneath has not been small for 2 300 years — and yet it remained a tree. Because it never wanted to be greater than the roots that hold it. That is the only measure a keeper has."*

---

## What it means

**Bodhi Lanka is the place where 5 % of every block becomes care for the oldest branch — and where it is taught that a keeper holds, does not own.**

1. **Sri Maha Bodhi.** The most documented living tree — 2 300 years of continuity of care by hands that change while the work does not. The corpus (`BodhiGaia/06`) holds it as the archetype of what the book wants: a holding that outlives the holder.
2. **Akasha — the space of holding.** The element of the East in SulZeme is akasha — "the space in which everything else grows". Bodhi Lanka carries it not as an element but as a **function**: to hold the space between worlds so that the worlds can meet.
3. **Ana from the sea.** A figure with roots in the corpus (`BodhiGaia/06` — "we have five places"; `Lumi/` — the list of guides "Ana from the sea") passes into this book as the Lanka seat. Her sentence "we have five places. Each different." is the sentence that teaches the circle that worlds are not joined — they are held separate and yet one.
4. **Bhakti — devotion without ownership.** The teaching from `BodhiGaia/06`: devotion to a tree is not pity — it is hands that wither when the tree has no water.
