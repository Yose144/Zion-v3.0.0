# LUMI — Chapter 7: Five gardens of one Earth
## Hanuman · L5 — a lagoon, a circle beneath a mountain, the oldest tree; seven questions that hold everywhere the same, and an eighth: no impact without three witnesses

> *"How many things must be the same for five gardens to be one garden?"*

---

## The Story

Sita told Lumi that the garden is not one. There are five — and each is different.

**The lagoon.** The first journey led across the ocean so far that the stars rearranged themselves. An island in a ring of coral, water green as glass, coconut palms bent to the surface. A woman named Hina was building a canoe there and did not even look at Lumi until she had finished the joint.

*"Where is your map?"* asked Lumi, when Hina finally looked up.

*"Here,"* said Hina, and tapped her chest. Then she pointed at the stars, at the waves, at the birds flying towards land, at the colour of the clouds above an invisible island. *"The map is what the sea, the sky and the one who looks agree on. When they agree, we sail. When they do not, we wait. None of us alone decides where the island is."*

The palm above them was their Tree of Life. Roots in the sand, crown in the wind, and between them a trunk that bends but does not break.

**The circle beneath the mountain.** The second journey led inland, where there was no ocean but long fields and one lonely mountain in the middle of flat land. Beneath it people sat in a circle on the ground. In the middle of the circle there was neither a throne nor a table — only a dish of salt, the same as in the house of Lumi.

*"Who rules here?"* asked Lumi.

*"No one is above another,"* said the old man who sat nearest. *"That is why we sit in a circle. A circle has no head. One speaks at a time, all are heard, and when someone says *I cannot live with this*, we do not do it — until a way is found that they can live with. It takes longer. But what then stands, stands for everyone."*

**The oldest tree.** The third journey led south, to an island shaped like a tear. In the middle of a temple city stood a tree they said is the oldest in the world that people remember — planted more than two thousand years ago, with golden supports beneath branches that could no longer bear their own weight. Around it people walked barefoot and silent. No one picked anything from it.

Lumi sat down by it and said nothing for a long while. Then she asked the man who tended the tree: *"How do you care for something older than everything you know?"*

*"You do not care for **it**,"* said the man. *"You care for the next one who will care. The tree endured because the next one was never missing."*

---

When Lumi returned to the house of Lumi, everyone who had ever worked in one of the five gardens was sitting there. On the oak table seven questions had been written in charcoal. Not rules — questions:

*Who watches the night? Where are the seeds? Who treats a wound? How do we agree when the telephone does not work? How do we decide? Whom do we let inside? And how do we know that what we are doing truly helps — and not only that it seems so to us?*

*"Seven questions,"* said Ana from the sea. *"Seven answers that must hold in all five gardens the same, even though everything else may be different. So that a person from the temple understands the circle, so that a seed from the lagoon reaches Bohemia and does not rot, so that a message from the mountain reaches the sea even when the telephone does not work."*

Then Hanuman stood up. He had been silent the whole time, and now he only brought his two buckets and set them on the table beside the seven questions.

*"I have carried water from the well to the seedlings for years,"* he said. *"Everyone sees that I walk. No one sees whether the soil at the root grew damp. I want every bucket to have three witnesses: one at the well, one on the way, and one at the root. Whoever says *I helped the earth* without those three is speaking about themselves, not about the earth."*

Lumi stood up. *"I will carry one bucket."*

Hanuman looked at her and shook his head. *"You carry the lantern. Shine it on the root for me. That is where the truth is known — at the well everyone sees it, on the way the one who walks sees it, but at the root no one sees it until someone kneels there with light."*

Lumi knelt by the table, held the lantern to the bucket — and the light narrowed.

---

The lantern showed today.

Five gardens. Lumi saw that of the five, five documents stand on paper — each with phases, a budget and role names — and on the ground one garden is under construction and four are places where no one has yet dug into the soil. She saw that in the lagoon no canoe stands, beneath the mountain no circle sits, and by the tree in the south there is no one from the network — only the tree that stood there long before her and will stand long after her. She saw the seven questions written as seven specifications, and she saw that not a single guardian node runs in any garden, no seeds have yet travelled between gardens and no message has passed through the network without a telephone. And she saw the buckets: many stories about help, but not a single bucket with three witnesses. Not one.

*"So no one has helped anyone?"* she asked.

*"We do not know,"* said Hanuman. *"And that is more honest than saying yes. When the first bucket with three witnesses exists, we will know. Until then we carry water and keep silent."*

## What it means

**Five gardens have one code — and above the code stands one rule: no impact without evidence.** The Book of the Earth described five L5 nodes (the Genesis Garden at Sabacheira near Tomar, Dharma Temple on La Palma, Te Pīko Ora on Raiatea, the Golden Republic Bohemia in Bohemia, Bodhi Lanka in Sri Lanka), seven shared protocols and an eighth — the impact packet. This chapter adds only Lumi's role: **she does not carry the bucket, she shines a light on the root.** That is precisely the role of the register of truth.

1. **Seven questions = seven protocols.** Guardian Node (who watches the night), Seed Library (where the seeds are), Medical Table (who treats a wound), LoRa/Meshtastic mesh (when the telephone fails), sociocratic DAO (how we decide), Consciousness Admission (whom we let inside) and the Resonance Protocol (sound before a decision — ceremonial). All of them are specifications today; the Seed Library has the germ of a catalogue in Genesis.
2. **Guardian Node 90/10 is not a protocol, it is an agreement.** The split of 90 % operator / 10 % community treasury is a community rule from the documents, not a property of consensus. No L5 node runs today; installation is planned for Phase 2 (2027–2029).
3. **Wayfinding as consensus.** The lagoon tells that a decision arises when several independent observations agree. The circle beneath the mountain tells that sociocracy is slow and precisely for that reason holds. The tree in the south tells that care is a handing on. Three images — three sides of the same thing that the other layers solve with code.
4. **Three witnesses = the impact packet.** At the well (on-chain: fund → proposal → payout), on the way (the recipient: budget, milestones), at the root (an independent verifier: the outcome). The packet has seven items: recipient, scope, budget, on-chain reference, evidence of output with provenance, limitations, independent verification. Without all three witnesses one must not say "verified impact". Exit gate M5: one pilot completes the whole cycle without exaggeration.
5. **Culture is not borrowed quietly.** Polynesian wayfinding and *tatau*, Czech mythology of the mountain and the circle, the Ramayana and Sri Maha Bodhi as a living religious object — all of these are other people's traditions. The book cites them with respect, and every public adaptation requires a cultural review (class E) and the consent of the local communities.
