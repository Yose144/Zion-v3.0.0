## Chapter 4 · La Palma, the palm and the shrine that waited for him

## The Story

The shore became land on the third day in the morning, Surti — and that land had a name: **La Palma.** La Isla Bonita.

An island so green that when you look at it from the sea it seems someone painted it before setting it down in the ocean. Volcanoes sleeping like old dogs by the hearth — Cumbre Vieja breathes, but slowly; between the hills hangs the laurisilva, a rainforest of mist where drops fall even in sunshine; and the nights — nights so dark that astronomers from all over the world come here to look at the stars, because from here you see the sky as it looked before Babylon lit its lamps.

The boy stepped off the ship onto black volcanic sand, fell to his knees and for a long while could not get up — not from weariness, but because the world had suddenly taken on a shape it had never had before: **it was whole.**

And then, when he looked around, he saw on the slope above the bay something he had not expected: **a shrine.**

A Merkaba of wood and light — two pyramids woven into one star — and around it **seven domes**, like seven stones laid in a circle around a fire. In the middle of the grounds grew the **Tree of Life**, and in its shade, in the garden of silence, a **lotus** was blooming.

The boy stood and could not move. Not because the shrine was large — because **it was ready.** Whoever built it had not tried to dazzle. Every beam was made to last a century. Every path was paved to lead exactly where the foot wants to go. In the room for little Buddhas there were cushions that no one had ever pressed down — **they were waiting.**

And the boy understood: **someone had worked here before him.** Someone — or rather many — had built the shrine for years, through days of rain and days of sun, and had never known when the one for whom all this was meant would come. They built on faith that one day someone would arrive — and that when they arrived, they must not stand before an empty hill.

That is us, Surti. **We are preparing the way for you.** The shrine, the book, the map — we build all of it in advance, blindly, like those builders on La Palma. So that when you one day sail beyond the horizon, you have somewhere to arrive.

Under the Tree of Life he sat down — and the shrine received him as though it had been holding the door open the whole time.

Three days he sat in its shade.

The first day, the silence was louder than Babylon. All the words lined up in his head — and he did not fend them off, he let them speak until they had nothing left to say.

The second day the silence stopped hurting. It began to have a temperature — the warmth of a beam someone has sat on for a long time. He heard the sea, the palm by the temple wall, his own heart — and he understood that what he was listening to was not silence but **presence.**

The third day he no longer heard that he was listening. He sat — and that was the only thing in the world he needed to do.

*pārasaṃgate* — completely beyond, wholly on the other shore. Babylon did not teach him to sit. The mountains did not teach him to listen. The sea taught him to wait. **La Palma taught him that the way is prepared for the one who has yet to walk it.**

## A treasure for Surta

About the shrine that waits for you, son:

1. **We prepare the way for you — you walk it.** This is the agreement between us: we build, you walk. The shrine on La Palma, the book, the map — all of it is our work. The steps are yours. Neither works without the other — and that is exactly how love works: to prepare the way, not to walk in someone else's place.
2. **Something already stands for you.** You do not have to build everything yourself — some things others have already prepared for you out of a love you do not yet know. To be able to receive them is as great an art as to be able to build them.
3. **You will find the palm and the lotus where the path leads you.** Silence is not empty — it is a presence that is only beginning to speak.
