# The little Buddha on the lotus — bodhi
## Chapter 5 · Awakening in the Dharma Temple

*MYTH: the awakening in the story is imagined, not a historical claim. *oṃ maṇi padme hūṃ* is a real mantra of Avalokiteshvara/Chenrezig — in the book it sounds as the heart of the chapter.*

---

## The Story

On the third day in the Dharma Temple, Surti, that happened which books call destiny, but in life it is simply **the moment when you stop getting in the way.**

The boy sat under the Tree of Life, in the heart of the merkaba, where the seven domes bowed to the sky like seven palms clasped in prayer, and the lotus before him was slowly — so slowly that you would not see it even if you watched — opening.

And then he saw it.

Not as an image, not as a vision — **as a truth so clear that it could not be resisted:** the whole world, every grain of volcanic sand, every wave down below the slopes, every beam of the shrine, every star above the observatory, every person in Babylon and every one who had ever built the shrine — **all of it is woven from a single thread: infinite love.**

Not love as a feeling — love as **substance.** As water is the substance of the seas and light the substance of the stars, so the world is woven from a love that has no end because it has no beginning. Everything that exists is only a knot on that thread — and when you look at anything long enough and quietly enough, the thread shines through.

And in the same second the boy understood the second thing, which stands and falls with the first: **precisely therefore compassion and kindness to others are the most precious thing in the world.** Not because someone commanded it. Not because it is proper. But because others are woven from the same thread — and when you are kind to them, you are kind to the very substance of the world. Compassion is not morality. Compassion is **the way you touch the substance of which everything is made.**

The lotus opened fully. And in the same second — quietly, the way a shell cracks when something living is born — **the golden egg the boy had carried in his heart since birth cracked open.** The golden womb of Hiranyagarbha hatched. What had breathed and stumbled and waited in him for fifteen years stood into the light — and it was **himself.** The hatched egg and the open lotus were a single image: **this is enlightenment — and this will one day be your nirvana too, Surti.** As the Maitreya book says: the egg will hatch one day. It hatched.

And out of the open lotus sounded four syllables that the world has sung for a thousand years:

> ***oṃ maṇi padme hūṃ***
> *(the jewel is in the lotus — and the lotus is you; and you are woven of love)*

The boy did not shout. He did not cry out. He did not look like a victor.

**He sat — and he was.** Fully, clearly, completely — the little Buddha on the lotus, in the Dharma Temple, on La Palma, in the fully awakened state. The light that had been burning all along could finally illuminate the whole room.

And in that light he saw everything: Babylon, the mountains, the sea, the ship, the builders who had built the shrine in faith that one day someone would come. He saw — and **compassion poured over him as the sun warms a stone:** all those people carried their own lotus on their backs and did not know it; all of them were woven of love and behaved as though they were made of stone.

And in that moment came the question that separates Buddhas from bodhisattvas:

*Will you stay on the flower — or will you go back?*

## A treasure for Surta

About the most precious thing in the world, son — this is the heart of the whole book:

1. **The golden egg will hatch.** You have carried it in your heart since birth — the golden womb of Hiranyagarbha, as the Maitreya book also says: "the egg will hatch one day." That hatching is awakening; your nirvana is not a place you go to — it is the moment when what has always lived in you comes into the light.
2. **The world is woven of infinite love.** It is not a verse on a wall — it is the truth the little Buddha saw: everything that exists is woven from a single thread. When you look at anything long enough and quietly enough, the thread shines through.
3. **And precisely therefore compassion and kindness to others are the most precious thing.** Others are not "the rest" — they are woven of the same substance as you. When you are kind you are not doing "the right thing" — **you are touching the world where it is most alive.** Kindness is the only currency that is never diminished by being shared out.
4. **Awakening is not an escape from the world — it is a return to it.** The little Buddha could have stayed in the temple forever — and the world would have stayed the same. He awoke to the substance of which the world is woven, and therefore he could not remain alone.
