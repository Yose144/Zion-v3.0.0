# So be it — svāhā
## Chapter 6 · The bodhisattva's vow and the return with a seed

*MYTH: the vow and the return are story — not a real person's vow, not an event. The bodhisattva is an archetype that returns for others; not a role anyone holds.*

---

## The Story

The boy on the lotus, Surti, could have stayed sitting forever.

No one would have blamed him. He had awakened — that was all the path had promised. He could have sat in the Dharma Temple until the world drank itself, and the volcano would have whispered his name instead of the children. It was the happiest moment of his life — and that is exactly why he left it.

Because in it there was no end — there was a **vow.**

The boy stood up on the lotus — and the lotus did not collapse, it held him. He stood up — and looked back across the sea, across the mountains, across Babylon, and saw all the people who walked the same paths as he had, and did not know they carried the lotus inside. He saw the mother in the doorway. He saw the little children asking "and what is behind all of it" and no one answers them.

And he said — not with his voice, but with his whole heart, so that the ocean heard it and the waves stopped for a second:

> *"I will not enter nirvana alone. I will come back for all who are still standing in the doorway."*

That was the bodhisattva's vow — **svāhā, so be it.**

Tears sprang from him — not from sorrow, Surti, but from the size of what he had said. And those tears, as they fell on the lotus leaves, were not ordinary: each carried in it the light of one star that somewhere above Babylon someone had not seen, because they were looking down at a screen.

Then the boy did something no one expected: he bent down to the palm by the temple wall, picked a coconut — not for himself, but as a seed — and put it in his backpack next to the stone he had carried since the first gate.

"I will come back," he said a second time, already softly, "and I will plant it in the middle of Babylon, so that a palm grows there, under which children sit."

And he went back — **not because the awakening had ended, but because it was beginning.**

## A treasure for Surta

About the vow and the return, son:

1. **The bodhisattva is the one who returns.** It is not a title, it is not a medal — it is an attitude: "I received a gift, and therefore I pass it on." That is how you live, not by holding a treasure and waiting for the door to close behind you.
2. **Nirvana without return is half.** True awakening is recognized by whether, after it, you care whether others have woken too. If not — you were sitting on a flower, not on a lotus.
3. **Carry the seed home.** A coconut is not a treasure — a coconut is a **vow.** To bring back something that can grow even where you began is the most honest thing you can do.
