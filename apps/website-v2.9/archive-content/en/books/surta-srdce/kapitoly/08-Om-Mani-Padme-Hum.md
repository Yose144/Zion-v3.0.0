## The Story

The boy was returning — and the way back was different from the way there.

The mountains he had walked years before, barefoot and alone, now carried confident steps. The sea that had once taught him to wait now carried him home, because the direction had turned: **before, he was looking for a shore — now the ship was carried by the shore.** And Babylon, which he had left as a boy with a question, received him back — not a different city, but a different man.

He came down from the sea, down from the mountains, and before the gate of Babylon — there where the lamp used to stand, the one people return under when they are afraid to go on — he stopped.

He took the coconut out of his backpack, the seed he had carried from the palm. He looked for a place where it could grow. He found a gap in the paving, a small crack that Babylon did not want, because there was nothing to sell in it — only soil, where the rain runs down, and roots that were already waiting.

He planted the seed in the crack. He watered it from the bottle he had from the journey. And he said — quietly, like a vow spoken only to yourself and to the earth:

> *"Now I will water you — and then you will water me."*

And then he turned and went home — to the mother who stood in the doorway, as she had stood the whole way, only he could not see her, because he was walking.

## A treasure for Surta

Now comes the most important paragraph in the whole book, son — read it every morning until you remember where the seed grows:

**Six syllables — six ways to live:**

- ***oṃ*** — be humble. The world does not need your greatness; it needs your presence.
- ***ma*** — love deeds, not promises. Measured by action, not by words.
- ***ṇi*** — silence is a speech you learn all your life. Listen more than you speak.
- ***pad*** — the lotus grows out of mud. Mud is not the end — it is the soil.
- ***me*** — the jewel has been in you from the beginning; never guard whether you have it, but whether you give it away.
- ***hūṃ*** — so be it. The vow is work; the work is the vow.

It is not a prayer — **it is an instruction for life.** Sing it when you are afraid; speak it when you do not know; live it when you do know.
