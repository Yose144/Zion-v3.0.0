## The Story

The child opened the first gate himself. The second opened by itself — and that is worse.

The mountains above Babylon were not beautiful. Beauty is a thing for postcards; the mountains above Babylon were high, stony and indifferent. The boy walked for three days before he understood why the road was empty: **no one walks here, because nothing here makes sense.** Babylon had a price for answers. The mountains had a price for questions — and no one wanted to pay with a question.

The first night his fingers froze. The second day his water ran out and he drank from the grooves in the stones. On the third day he turned around — Babylon breathed in the distance like a mother asleep behind a door, and the boy had within him a silence into which two truths settled at once:

**No one will come to help.** It hurts — and at the same time it is the best news in the world, because it means: *this is my path. It is not borrowed, not bought, there is no button for it.* Help from others comes when things are going right — never as a substitute for the steps you must take.

So he walked on the fourth day and the fifth. He wore down his boots, learned to read the stones by where they turn their sharp shadow, and learned the most important foreign language in the world: **the silence of his own steps.**

Though he had not yet heard a word about it — he was walking through the quantum revolution. That is: he had lit within himself the truth that the world does not divide into things that can be checked and things that can be bypassed. **It divides into what can be verified and what we merely tell each other.** Step by step, stone by stone, he verified. And when one night the lights of Babylon vanished completely — he did not cry. He breathed contentedly.

That was the second gate. With the first *gate* he left the city. With the second *gate*, what he had believed in the city to be himself fell away from him.

## A treasure for Surta

It will sound harsh, son, and yet it is the truth that will never disappoint you in life:

1. **You truly learn only what you learn yourself.** Books help you find the map — but the mountains are walked barefoot.
2. **"No one will help me" is not a curse — it is freedom.** Help comes when you stand on your own two feet, not instead of them.
3. **Verify.** When someone tells you the world is this way or that, first look beneath their feet — on what stones that world walks. Babylonian sentences fall apart on the edges where advertising ends.
