# 2 · Wood and Pitch

## Story

The carpenter was demanding about wood. He walked through the yards and the piles and chose planks slowly — one moved his hand, another was not allowed. The apprentice at his side could not bear it one day: "Master, what are you looking at? It is only wood."

"Only wood," repeated the carpenter, and laid his palms on the plank as on a shoulder. "Look closer. Every piece of wood grew differently — one in the wind, one in the shade, one bent by a rock. A boat must not destroy them — it must take them as they are. You do not cover a crack with a worthless plank; you go around the crack and let it tell how it grew."

And he added quietly, more to himself: "People are like wood. Each is bent to a different side. A good boat does not remake them — it carries them."

When the ribs were fitted, he brought hot pitch — dark, thick, fragrant with resin. He smeared every seam with it, inside and out, until the hull began to gleam like washed honey.

"Why inside as well?" asked the apprentice. "The water does not get in there."

"That is exactly where it does — from sweat, from tears, from the rain that falls on the people in the boat. The pitch inside does what you cannot see from outside: it keeps the water out, even the water that comes from above."

In the old books that nobody in the workshop read and yet everyone knew, it stood that Noah had once built in this way: *wood of gofer, and with pitch you shall smear the boat inside and out.* And the oldest interpreters said that the Hebrew word for pitch — **kofer** — is the same root as the word for reconciliation: *that which covers and keeps the water out.*

"Remember," said the carpenter, "what covers people is what carries them over the water."

## From Scripture and Tradition

- **Genesis 6:14** — "Make yourself an ark of **gofer wood**… and smear it with **pitch** inside and out." The only boat in Scripture whose building is described — and the only wooden boat of the old world that survived the sea.
- **kofer (כֹּפֶר)** — the Hebrew word for pitch has the same root as *kapóret* / *kipur* — **covering, reconciliation** (Yom kippur = the Day of Atonement). Both rabbinic and Christian tradition read the pitch of the ark as an image of the covering of sin.
- **Noah as a prefiguration** — Church tradition (from 1 Pet 3:20–21) reads the ark as a prefiguration of salvation through water.
- The carpenter as builder — an image the story carries on into every plank of the boat.
