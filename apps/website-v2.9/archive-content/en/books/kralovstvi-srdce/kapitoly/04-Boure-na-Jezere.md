# 4 · The Storm on the Lake

## Story

The Kinneret can make a storm within an hour. Winds fall from the mountains into the lake basin as water into a funnel — and the surface that carried the sky in the morning can carry fear by evening.

That evening they were sailing to the other shore. The carpenter lay down in the stern, his head on a wooden cushion he had planed himself — and slept. The storm rose so that the waves broke over the side and the boat filled with water; the disciples, men of the water, held the oars and shouted over the wind.

And he slept. He slept the way one sleeps who built his own boat — he knows every rib of it and knows what it will bear.

"Master!" they shouted over the storm. "We are sinking, and you do not care?!"

He rose. He looked at the wind and at the sea — not angrily, but as a craftsman looks at work not yet finished — and said to the lake what is said to a boat that is finished: **"Be silent, be still."**

And the wind ceased, and a great silence came — such a silence that in it they heard drops running down from the steering oar. The disciples looked at one another and in fear — greater than before the storm — said: *who is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?*

And he turned to them, to their fear, and said what he has said at every storm since: "Why are you afraid? If you have faith like a grain of mustard seed…"

Later, when they told of it, someone remembered the old psalm about those who *go down to the sea in ships and do their work on the great waters* — how they cry out in trouble, and he stills the storm so that the waves calm, and he brings them into the **harbour they long for**.

## From Scripture and Tradition

- **Mark 4:35–41** — the storm on the lake, Jesus asleep on the cushion in the stern, "Be silent, be still!" (*siōpa, pephimōso*), the great silence, the disciples' question "who is this?".
- **Psalm 107:23–30** — "Those who go down to the sea in ships… he raised a storm… he stills the storm and brings them to the harbour they desire." — the Old Testament text the Church read from the beginning as a prefiguration of the calming of the sea.
- **Jonah 1** — Jonah **sleeps in the storm** in the bottom of the boat; the sea calms when he is thrown into the water — a prefiguration Jesus himself recalls as his sign (see ch. 6).
- The image of a boat that does not sink because its builder is on board — the Church's reading of this pericope from early Christianity.
