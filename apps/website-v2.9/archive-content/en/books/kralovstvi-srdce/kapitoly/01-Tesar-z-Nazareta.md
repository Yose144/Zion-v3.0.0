# 1 · The Carpenter of Nazareth

## Story

In Nazareth above Galilee there stood a workshop that everyone knew. It smelled of freshly cut cedar and olive wood; on the walls hung chisels smooth from the palms of three generations. They called it the carpenter's workshop — and the carpenter had worked in it since he was a boy.

He made yokes for oxen, so that the weight would not press on the neck. He made tables at which families gathered for bread. He made doors that kept the wind outside and the warmth within. People said: *what the carpenter makes, holds.*

But in the evening, when the workshop fell silent and the last customer had gone, the carpenter built something else. In the back corner, under a cloth, a **boat** was slowly growing.

Not a big one — for one crew, perhaps twelve. A keel from a single trunk of cedar, ribs bent over steam like lattice under a palm. The neighbours came to look and shook their heads. "A boat? Here?" Nazareth lay in the hills — the lake of Kinneret was a whole day's journey away. "Who will you sell it to, carpenter?"

He answered strangely: "I am not selling it. I am building it for those who will need it one day."

And he planed on. He made it more slowly than anything else — he laid every plank three times before fixing it. When they asked him why so long, he said:

"A boat meant to carry people through a storm is not built in haste. It is built over years, in the days when there is calm — so that it stands when the calm is gone."

## From Scripture and Tradition

- **Mark 6:3** — "Is this not the **carpenter** (Greek *tektōn* — craftsman, builder), the son of Mary…?" — the only place in the Gospels where Jesus is named directly by his trade.
- **Matthew 13:55** — "Is this not the son of the carpenter?" — the workshop was the family's; the trade passed from father to son, as everything of value passes.
- Of the so-called **hidden years** (from childhood to thirty) Scripture says nothing — the story of this book places its image, the boat, into that silence.
- **Hebrews 6:19** — hope as the **anchor of the soul** — the image the story carries from the very first plank.

| What is in the chapter | State |
