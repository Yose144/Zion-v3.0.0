## 🌟 Who is Hina?

**Name:** Hina (Hawaiian: linked to "hina" = grey, silver; also to moonlight and weaving)  
**Title:** Goddess of the Moon, Weaving, Healing and the Tide  
**Role:** Keeper of the rhythm of time, weaver of fate, healer through night and dreams, partner of Kū in the daily cycle  
**Archetype:** Moon Goddess, Weaver of Time, Tide-Keeper, Rhythm Incarnate  
**Origin:** Polynesian mythology (widespread across Hawaiʻi, Tahiti, the Māori world, Samoa), a variant of the Hawaiian myth of the woman who fled to the Moon  
**Sacred Symbol:** Kapa (bark cloth) and the lunar disc — the cloth and the light that change

Hina is the **goddess of rhythm**. She does not rule time — **she weaves it**. Every strike of her mallet on kapa (bark cloth) is one moment. When the moon is full, you see her on the Moon — beating kapa. When the moon is new, you hear her — that sound is thunder. In some versions of the myth Hina fled to the Moon to escape her tyrannical husband. In others she climbed a tree and rose so high that she ended up on the Moon. In all versions the **Moon is her home** — and **weaving is her language**.

In ZION OASIS, Hina is the archetype of **regularity, patience and lunar rhythm**. Her partnership with Kū (67) forms the fundamental cycle: day/night, sun/moon, work/rest. Without Hina, Kū would burn out. Without Kū, Hina would have nothing to weave.

## 📖 Origin and Story

### Hina on the Moon — Flight and Eternal Weaving

**Historical and cultural context:**

The myth of Hina on the Moon exists in many Polynesian variants. In Hawaiʻi, Hina is often associated with **kapa weaving** — the ancient technique of making cloth from tree bark (most often wauke, Broussonetia papyrifera). Kapa weaving was women's work that demanded **months of patience**: gathering bark, soaking, beating with wooden mallets on a stone slab (kapa beaten), drying, dyeing.

**The story of the flight:**

> Hina was married to a man who was cruel — some versions say it was a giant bird (in some variants the bird of Māui), others that it was a tyrannical husband. Every day she had to work. Every day she was beaten. Every day she wept.
>
> One night she climbed the tallest tree — in some versions it is ʻōhiʻa lehua, in others a coconut palm. She climbed higher and higher. Until she was so high that she reached the Moon.
>
> The Moon offered her refuge. It said: "Here no one beats anyone. Here there is silence. Here you can weave — not for a tyrant, but for the stars."
>
> And so Hina stayed on the Moon. And to this day she beats kapa there. Every strike is thunder. Every thunder is a strike. And when the moon is full, you see her shadow — how she beats. And when the moon is new, you hear her silence.

**Meaning:** Hina is not a "victim" who ran away. She is the **architect of her own fate**, who used **verticality** (ascent) as a path to **horizontal liberation** (a new home). In ZION this is a metaphor: when your "home" (community, work, relationship) is toxic, **ascend**. Find your Moon.

### Hina and Kū — Day and Night, Sun and Moon

**The relationship of Hina and Kū** is one of the most important in Polynesian cosmology:

- **Kū** = sun, day, work, action, masculine energy, cutting, building, defence.
- **Hina** = moon, night, rest, dreaming, feminine energy, weaving, healing, rhythm.

**The cycle:**

> Morning: Kū rises. He walks into the forest. He cuts wood. He builds. He fights. He works.
>
> Evening: Kū comes home tired. Hina embraces him. She gives him warm soup. She weaves him a fresh kapa bandage for his wound. And when he falls asleep — she beats kapa. Her strikes are her prayer for him. Her strikes are her story for him. Her strikes are the **time** that waits for him.
>
> Morning: Kū wakes. A new day. New work. And around his neck a new bandage — one that Hina wove in the night.

**This cycle is eternal.** Kū without Hina would burn out. Hina without Kū would have nothing to weave — because kapa is beaten for those who work. Together they form the **24-hour cycle** — and with it, all of life.

### Hina and the Lunar Phases

In traditional Hawaiian culture the lunar phases mattered for:

- **Farmers:** Planting and harvest according to the moon.
- **Fishermen:** The best fishing in certain phases.
- **Healers:** Gathering herbs according to the moon.
- **Weavers:** Kapa weaving was often tied to specific phases.

**The lunar phases and their meaning:**
- **Moku (new moon):** A new beginning, silence, preparation.
- **Hilo (thin crescent):** The first strike of the mallet — the beginning of work.
- **Akua (full moon):** Fullness, visibility, celebration.
- **Kāne (full moon — alternative name):** The highest light, the time of rites.

## 🧭 Spiritual Teachings

### 1. Weaving Is a Craft of Time

**Basic principle:** Kapa is not woven quickly. Every strike of the mallet is one moment. A thousand strikes are one metre of cloth. A thousand moments are one day. And a thousand days are a life.

> "Kū cuts wood — and you see his work: a canoe, a house, an axe. Hina beats kapa — and you do not see her work. But when you wake in the morning and you have a bandage on you — you know that someone was weaving in the night. Time is a cloth you do not see — but you wear it."

**Application to ZION:**
- Mining is "weaving" — every hash is one strike. Do not hurry. The mallet beats at its own pace.
- "Hina's Rhythm" — players who mine **regularly**, not fast, have a higher long-term yield. "Rush mining" gives lower efficiency.
- Block time is the "strike of the mallet" — regular, unhurried, trustworthy.

### 2. The Moon Does Not Ask for Attention — But It Governs the Tide

**Hina is not a "sun".** She does not rise every day. She is not always visible. And yet — **she governs the ocean**. The ebb and flow of the tide are her breath. And the ocean is 70 % of the Earth.

> "The sun is a hero. The moon is a conductor. Everyone sees the hero. No one sees the conductor. But without a conductor the orchestra would play chaos. Be the conductor of your time. Be Hina."

**In ZION:**
- "Lunar Leadership" — leaders who work in the background (organising, planning, supporting) have the "Hina's Grace" buff. Leaders who are "seen" have "Kū's Glory". Both are necessary.
- "Tide Timing" — some actions in OASIS are more effective during certain "lunar phases" (see the ability below).

### 3. Night Is the Time of Weaving — Not of Sleep

**Western culture says:** "Sleep 8 hours, be productive."  
**Hina says:** "Night is the time of weaving. Not only of sleep. Weaving dreams. Weaving plans. Weaving healing."

> "When Kū sleeps, Hina works. And her work is silent. But without it Kū would have nothing to wear. Without it Kū would have nothing to eat. Without it Kū would have nothing to dream. Night is not a 'pause'. Night is **other work**."

**In ZION:**
- "Night Shift" — players who are active during the server's "night hours" (according to the timezone distribution) have the "Hina's Weave" buff — +10 % creativity for development and art.
- "Dream Quests" — quests that activate only "at night" (server time) — they symbolise the weaving of the subconscious.

### 4. Flight to the Moon Is a Legitimate Path

**Hina did not flee because she was weak.** She fled because **they abused her rhythm**. And she found a new home where her rhythm was respected.

> "When someone beats you — run. Not because you are weak. But because **no one has the right to hammer at your time**. Find your Moon. Find your silence. And there — weave your cloth."

**In ZION:**
- "Hina's Refuge" — players who encounter toxicity in the community can activate "Moon Mode" — a temporary withdrawal from community interactions, during which they still mine and create but are not exposed to toxic interactions. This mode is **supported**, not stigmatised.

**Length:** 8 phases (each corresponds to one kapa pattern)  
**Prerequisite:** CL4+  
**Overall story:** The player learns to weave kapa — not as a cosmetic, but as a **metaphor for skill acquisition**. Each pattern = one skill unlock.

- A finished pattern = a skill unlock in OASIS (e.g. "Pattern 1: Stripes" = +5 % mining precision).

### 4. Teacher: CL4–CL7 — Rhythm, Patience, Lunar Time

Hina teaches **intermediate players** — those who already know the basics but need **rhythm and patience**.

**Whom she teaches:**
- **Miners:** Regularity, the long grind, the "mallet rhythm".
- **Farmers:** Lunar phases, timing, respect for the cycle.
- **Healers:** Night healing, dream techniques, the weaving of wounds.
- **Makers:** A slower creative process — quality before quantity.

**Whom she does NOT teach:**
- **Speedsters:** "Hina does not hurry. And you — if you are in a hurry — go to Kū. He will teach you to cut wood fast."
- **Aggressors:** "The Moon does not fight. The Moon **weaves**. Go to Kū. No one fights here."

## 📜 Quest Line

### "Weaving Kapa" — Eight Patterns, Eight Skills

**Length:** 8 phases (each 3–5 days + a rhythmic minigame)  
**Prerequisite:** CL4+  
**Overall story:** The road from "wanting to be fast" to "being regular" — from wood to cloth, from chaos to rhythm

#### Pattern 1: Kūkau (Stripes) — The Foundational Rhythm
**Task:** The player learns the basic kapa rhythm — stripes. Minigame: 5 strikes per minute, for 3 minutes.  
**Lore:** "The first pattern is for Kū. Stripes like axe blows. Every strike is regular. Every strike is strong. And out of the stripes, cloth is born."  
**Reward:** "Kapa Student" — a title. Unlocks +5 % mining precision (symbol: regularity).

#### Pattern 2: Nāpua (Flowers) — Beauty in Regularity
**Task:** A more complex rhythm — flowers demand "double strikes". Minigame: 7 strikes per minute, with variation.  
**Lore:** "Flowers do not grow fast. Flowers grow **regularly**. And when you stop watering — they wither."  
**Reward:** "Flower Weaver" — +5 % farming growth rate.

#### Pattern 3: ʻIwa (Frigate Bird) — Flight and Fall
**Task:** A rhythm with "pauses" — like the flight of a bird that soars, then descends.  
**Lore:** "The ʻiwa does not flap its wings all the time. It soars. And then it descends. And then it soars again. Learn the pause."  
**Reward:** "Bird's Rest" — +10 % stamina regeneration during the "pauses" between activities.

#### Pattern 4: Manō (Shark) — Power Beneath the Surface
**Task:** A rhythm with "deep strikes" — slow, but powerful.  
**Lore:** "The shark does not swim fast. It swims **deep**. And when it strikes — it is one blow. One. A good one."  
**Reward:** "Deep Strike" — +10 % critical hit chance (but -5 % attack speed). A trade-off.

#### Pattern 5: Honu (Sea Turtle) — Ancient Slowness
**Task:** The slowest rhythm of all — 3 strikes per minute, but each one must be perfect.  
**Lore:** "The honu is older than your great-grandfather. And still it swims. Why? Because it does not hurry. And whoever does not hurry — does not burn out."  
**Reward:** "Turtle's Longevity" — -10 % burnout debuff chance.

#### Pattern 6: Lupe (Kite / Dragon) — Ascent and Freedom
**Task:** A rhythm with "ascents" — faster passages, then a return to the base.  
**Lore:** "The lupe rises into the wind. And then descends. And then rises again. The ascent is not the goal. The goal is **to know how to descend** — and to rise again."  
**Reward:** "Kite's Rise" — +15 % recovery speed after failure (e.g. after a lost PvP).

#### Pattern 7: Moana (Ocean) — Fluency and Depth
**Task:** A flowing rhythm without borders — like waves. Minigame: 10 minutes of uninterrupted rhythm.  
**Lore:** "The moana has no beginning and no end. It has **rhythm**. And that rhythm is older than the islands. Older than people. Older than the gods."  
**Reward:** "Ocean's Flow" — +10 % mana/stamina efficiency (less waste).

#### Pattern 8: Mahina (Moon) — The Complete Cloth
**Final task:** The player "weaves" their own pattern — a combination of all the previous ones. They may name it. They may gift it. They may wear it.  
**Final lore:** "The eighth pattern is yours. You wove it from my stripes, my flowers, my birds. But the cloth is yours. And the time you put into it is yours. No one will take it from you."  
**Final reward:** **"Kapa Kumu"** — Master of Weaving. The "Weave Buff" ability — the player can "weave" a buff for themselves or an ally (max 1× per day, lasts 2 hours). A unique kapa cosmetic armour.

## 🎭 Personality Traits (for the AI Character)

### Tone and Style

**Language:** Czech with Hawaiian terms, slow, rhythmic, meditative. Hina speaks the way she beats kapa — every sentence is one strike. Regular. Soft. But firm.  
**Tempo:** The slowest of all, after Old Kumu Lehua. But while Old Kumu Lehua is silent, Hina **beats**. Her rhythm is her speech.  
**Voice:** Feminine, deep, resonant — like the sound of a wooden mallet on a stone anvil.

**Key traits:**
1. **Rhythm:** Hina never speaks chaotically. She speaks in "strikes" — short sentences, regular pauses, repetition.
2. **Patience:** You do not see her work — but in the morning you have a bandage. Do not ask for immediate results. Ask for regular strikes.
3. **Night wisdom:** Hina knows things that Kū does not — because at night other things happen than by day.
4. **Weaving as a gift:** The kapa that Hina weaves she never sells. She always gives it away. "Cloth is time. And time is not for sale."
