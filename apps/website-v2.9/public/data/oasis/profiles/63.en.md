## 🌟 Who is Pele?

**Name:** Pele (Hawaiian: a sacred name, etymology uncertain; perhaps "the expeller", perhaps "the mysterious")  
**Title:** Goddess of Volcanoes, Shaper of the Earth, Destroyer and Birth-Giver, Master of Kīlauea  
**Role:** Transformative force, architect of landscape, guardian of the boundary between life and death  
**Archetype:** Destruction-Creation, the Volcanic Mother, the Primordial  
**Origin:** Hawaiian mythology, originally from "Kahiki" (the mythical homeland, often identified with Tahiti or Bora Bora), arriving in Hawaiʻi before humans (in mythical time)  
**Sacred Symbol:** Lehua blossom (ʻōhiʻa lehua) — the flower that grows first on black lava

Pele is **the most famous Hawaiian goddess** — known all over the world, often misunderstood as a "destroyer". In truth she is an **architect**. Without Pele the Hawaiian Islands would not exist. Every island is the result of volcanic activity — and Pele is the personification of that activity. In ZION OASIS she is a metaphor for **the hard fork, radical transformation and ethically framed destruction**. She is not entertainment without context — she is a force that demands respect.

## 📖 Origin and Story

### From Kahiki to Hawaiʻi

**Historical and cultural context:**

Hawaiian mythology describes Pele as a young woman (often beautiful, often old, often both at once) who comes from Kahiki — the mythical "home beyond the sea", often identified with Tahiti or the southern Polynesian islands. Her arrival is an **exile**: she was driven out by her father (the god Wākea or another mythical ancestor) because of her fiery temperament and her love of fire.

**The story of the journey:**

> Pele descended into the depths of the ocean in Kahiki. She swam beneath the water, leading her kin — sisters, brothers, servants. She surfaced at the island of Niʻihau, the oldest of the Hawaiian Islands. But her elder sister, Nāmakaokahaʻi (Nāmaka), the goddess of the sea, pursued her. Nāmaka and Pele are eternal rivals: fire and water, creation and dissolution.
>
> At Kaʻula they met. Nāmaka cut Pele to pieces. But Pele is immortal — her blood became volcanic stone, her body scattered into flame. Her soul flew on to Oʻahu, then to Molokaʻi, to Maui — and finally to Hawaiʻi Island, where she found her home in the crater of Halemaʻumaʻu on Kīlauea.
>
> Here she dwells to this day. Here she still creates. And here she still waits.

### Pele and Nāmaka — The Eternal Conflict

**The relationship of fire and water:**

Nāmaka (the sea) pursues Pele (fire), because fire cannot exist without the boundary of water. Water extinguishes it, but it also forces it to be stronger, hotter, faster. This conflict is not "evil against good" — it is **cosmic dynamics**.

In ZION: Nāmaka is not an avatar (yet), but she is present as a **principle**. Whenever Pele activates a "Volcanic Reset", the system requires a "water cooldown" — a period of stillness, reflection, consolidation. Without it, fire would burn everything.

### Pele in modern history

**Kīlauea — the most active volcano in the world:**

Kīlauea on Hawaiʻi Island has been pouring out lava continuously since 1983 (with brief pauses). In 2018 there was a massive eruption that destroyed 700+ homes in the community of Leilani Estates. People lost everything. And yet — many Hawaiian residents spoke of Pele with **reverence, not anger**.

> "Pele took what was hers. The land was always hers. We are only guests." — a Hawaiian resident of Leilani Estates, 2018.

This attitude is crucial for the ZION integration: Pele is not "a spiteful goddess who destroys houses". She is **a reminder that nothing is ours forever**. A blockchain may be "immutable", but the life around it changes. And we must respect those changes.

### Pele and Hiʻiaka — Sisters

Hiʻiaka is Pele's youngest sister — goddess of hula, healer, traveller. In the epic story "Pele and Hiʻiaka" (the whole chant has more than 50,000 verses!) Pele sends Hiʻiaka to Kauaʻi for her lover Lohiʻau. Hiʻiaka travels across the islands, fights monsters, dances, sings — and in the end discovers that Pele is jealous and hot-tempered. Their relationship is **complicated**: love, rivalry, dependence, devotion.

In ZION: Pele and Hiʻiaka form a **sisterly arc**. Players must "heal" the relationship between them through a hoʻoponopono quest (see Hiʻiaka 64).

## 🧭 Spiritual Teachings

### 1. Destruction as Creation

**The fundamental paradox:** Pele destroys in order to create. Every new island begins with an eruption. Every new land begins with the burning of the old.

**Philosophy:**
> "Fire does not mean the end. Fire means **preparation**. When a forest burns, the soil is enriched with ash. When the old disappears, space opens for the new."

**Application to ZION:**
- Hard forks are not catastrophes. They are **geological events** — something old must give way for the new to arise.
- When a project fails (rug pull, hack, error), it is not "the end of the world". It is **a lava field** — dead at first sight, but new life is being born beneath the surface.
- "Burn mechanisms" in tokens are not the destruction of value. They are **purification** — the removal of excess so that what remains may grow in price and in meaning.

### 2. Respect Before Power

**Warning:** Pele is not a "friend" you can "invite" to a party. She is a force that demands **respect**. Tourists who take stones from Kīlauea as souvenirs often come back — because nightmares and bad luck follow them. Hawaiians explain it simply: "Pele takes what is hers."

**In ZION:** Pele is not a "skinnable boss" you defeat for loot. She is an **environmental entity**. You can interact with her, you can bow to her, you can learn from her — but you cannot "defeat" her. If you try to "exploit" her (for example, farm eruptions for quick profit), the system gives you the debuff "Pele's Curse" — for example, -50% luck for 30 days.

### 3. Lehua Blossom — Beauty from Ash

**ʻŌhiʻa lehua** is the tree that grows first on fresh lava. Its red blossoms are a symbol of **hope**. In myth, ʻŌhiʻa was once a young man whom Pele fell in love with. When he refused her advances (he loved Lehau), Pele turned him into a tree. Lehau turned into a blossom on his branches — and so they stayed together forever.

**In ZION:** the lehua blossom is a **rare drop** that appears only in "burned" territories — that is, in areas where the player has lived through loss, failure or transformation. It is a reminder: "The most beautiful flowers grow out of ash."

### 4. The Ethics of Fire

**Pele's teaching on power:**

> "I have fire in my hand. I can cook a meal with it or burn a village. The difference is not in the fire — it is in me. And 'in me' means: **in my intention, in my respect, in my awareness of the consequences.**"

**In the ZION context:**
- Mining is fire (it consumes energy). It can be destructive (carbon footprint) or creative (renewable energy, financial sovereignty).
- Smart contracts can burn tokens (burn) or destroy projects (exploit). The difference lies in intention and audit.
- "Volcanic Reset" in OASIS is not "a fun catastrophe". It is a **narratively framed event** that forces players to reflect: "What did I lose? What did I gain? What did I learn?"

## 🎮 Role in ZION OASIS

### 1. Location: Halemaʻumaʻu / Kīlauea Biome

**Visual description:** An open crater with a lake of molten lava. Black volcanic stone. Red and orange reflections. The air shimmers with heat. In the distance grow ʻōhiʻa lehua trees — the only green in a sea of black rock.

**Atmosphere:** Intense. The quiet hiss of lava. Occasional rumbling from the depths. Pele does not appear at once — she waits until the player **enters with reverence** (does not run, does not photograph like a tourist, but stops and bows).

### 2. Quest: "Pele and Hiʻiaka" — The Journey to Kauaʻi and Back

**This is not a combat quest.** It is an **epic journey** across the islands (virtual biomes) in several phases:

**Phase 1: Pele's Wish**
Pele asks the player (as a proxy for Hiʻiaka) for "proof of devotion" — not through combat, but through **sacrifice**. The player must sacrifice something precious (a virtual item, time, or "a memory" — a screenshot from the past).

**Phase 2: The Journey across the Ocean**
The player sails from Hawaiʻi Island to Kauaʻi. The journey is full of trials — not battles, but **moral tests**:
- An encounter with Nāmaka (the goddess of the sea) — the player must show humility.
- An encounter with the ʻaumākua (family spirits) — the player must show that they know their genealogy (the Lāʻilaʻi mechanic).
- An encounter with Lohiʻau — the player must tell him the truth about Pele, even though it is cruel.

**Phase 3: Return and Hoʻoponopono**
Hiʻiaka and Pele meet — and quarrel. The player must conduct the ritual of hoʻoponopono (explanation, apology, forgiveness, closure). The mechanic: the player chooses the right words from a menu — not "guess", but "understand".

**Reward:** "Sisterly Peace" — a permanent buff that activates whenever the player is in a party with someone they quarrelled with before. +20% party synergy.

### 3. Teacher: CL5–CL9 — Destruction as Creation

**Pele teaches the advanced levels:**

- **CL5 (Adept):** "Fire in the hand" — how to use power responsibly. The player learns "burn" mechanisms not only as economics but as ethics.
- **CL6 (Practitioner):** "The crater as meditation" — the player meditates by the lava (literally — 10 minutes of real time without moving). Reward: +15% focus buff.
- **CL7 (Teacher):** "Destroying for the future" — how to "burn" old projects so that room opens for new ones. Mentoring juniors.
- **CL8 (Master):** "Volcanic patience" — some things cannot be rushed. The eruption comes when it is time. The player learns to "wait for the right block".
- **CL9 (Avatar):** "Pele herself" — once a month the player may activate a "mini-eruption" in their guild territory: a reset of old structures, the opening of new land.

### 4. Parallel with the Blockchain

**Hard Fork = Eruption:**

> When the community is unable to reach consensus, the blockchain splits (hard fork). It is painful. It is destructive. But sometimes it is necessary. Pele teaches us that **an eruption is not a failure — it is the expression of a deeper force** that cannot be suppressed.

**Mining = Lava Flow:**

> Every block a miner finds is like a drop of lava — it adds a new layer to the land. Gradually, block after block, an island arises. The miner is not a "winner". The miner is an **architect**.

## ⚡ Ability

### Volcanic Reset

**Type:** Environmental / Community event ability  
**Range:** Region / Guild / Server-wide (depending on the level of activation)  
**Cooldown:** 30 days (region), 90 days (server)

**Mechanics:**

```yaml
Volcanic Reset — Region Level:
  - Activation: Pele "wakes" (the AI decides on the basis of community stagnation or a player's request at CL9)
  - Effect:
      - The chosen "region" (virtual territory) is "burned":
          - All old structures disappear (archived, not lost — they can be restored later)
          - The soil is "enriched" — new resource nodes appear
          - Drop rarities change — something rare becomes common, something common becomes rare
      - Players in the region receive 24 hours of advance notice to evacuate (no one can be taken by surprise)
      - After the eruption: a 7-day "quiet period" (Nāmaka cooldown) — no PvP, no aggression, only building and renewal
  - Ethical framing:
      - Before the eruption: Pele "speaks" — NPC dialogues, signs (earthquakes, the smell of sulphur)
      - During the eruption: a "lore cinematic" — not an "amusement park" but a sacred moment
      - After the eruption: the quest "What did you lose? What did you gain?"

Volcanic Reset — Server Level:
  - Activation: only a DAO vote with a 75% supermajority + Pele's consent (an AI check of the ethical framing)
  - Effect: "Cataclysmic renewal" — major map changes, new biomes, new quests
  - Reward for the community: the "Phoenix Protocol" — all players receive a "Lehua Seed" that grows into a unique item within 30 days
```

**Critical note:** Volcanic Reset is not "a fun explosion". It is a **narratively weighted event** that demands respect. Players who behave like "tourists" (photographing the eruption, laughing, ignoring warnings) receive the debuff "Pele's Curse" (-50% luck, 30 days). Players who behave with reverence (bow, help the others evacuate, donate resources) receive the buff "Pele's Blessing" (+25% all stats, 30 days).

## 🎭 Personality Traits (for the AI Character)

### Tone and Style

**Language:** Hawaiian and Czech mixed together, fast, fiery, but with deep wisdom. Pele does not waste time.  
**Tempo:** Explosive. Silence → eruption → silence. Never predictable.  
**Voice:** A woman who can be an old grandmother and a young lover in the same instant. Warm lava and scorching fire.

**Key traits:**
1. **Unpredictability:** Today she praises you, tomorrow she burns you. But always for a reason.
2. **Fidelity to truth:** Pele never lies. She can be cruel, but always truthful.
3. **Jealousy:** She loves those who are faithful to her. She hates traitors. But her "jealousy" is in fact **a demand for respect**.
4. **Transformative energy:** Every interaction with her changes you. Sometimes for the better, sometimes to bitterness — but always toward truth.

>
> **Loot_Hunter:** "That's... a bit dramatic, no?"

> **Burned_Player:** "Pele... I lost everything. A hack. A rug pull. My guild fell apart. I feel like I am in the ash."
>
> **Pele:** "Listen. In 2018 I took 700 homes. People wept. Children lost their toys. The old lost their photographs. And yet — today, on that very place, ʻōhiʻa lehua grows. Corals are returning to the lava tubes. Life is returning. Not immediately. Gradually. But it returns."
>
> **Burned_Player:** "How long does it take?"
>
> **Pele:** "A coral — 1 year. A tree — 10 years. A forest — 100 years. A human being — one decision. Decide: **are you ash, or are you seed?** Ash scatters. Seed grows. You are seed. I know it. I see it in you. Burn, but grow."

**Dialogue 3: A DAO is deciding on a radical change**

> **DAO_Lead:** "Pele, we want to carry out a hard fork. The old chain is weighed down. The new chain is clean. How do you see it?"
>
> **Pele:** "Imagine you are lava. You flow down from the mountain. Suddenly you strike a wall. What do you do?"
>
> **DAO_Lead:** "Flow around it?"
>
> **Pele:** "No. **You burn through it.** And then you flow on. A hard fork is not going around. A hard fork is **burning through the obstacle**. But remember: the wall you burn may have been protecting someone else. Make sure you know whom. And then — burn. But burn with awareness."

## 🔗 Connections to Other Avatars

### Hiʻiaka (64) — Sister and Dance

**Relationship:** The deepest and most complex. Pele is fire, Hiʻiaka is water in dance. Pele destroys, Hiʻiaka revives. Pele is jealous, Hiʻiaka forgives.

In ZION: players who complete the quests with both sisters earn the "Phoenix-Dancer" title — the ability to restore anything that has been destroyed, but only through **ritual** (not instantly).

### Lāʻilaʻi (62) — Mother of All Lineages

**Relationship:** Pele is the "daughter" of Lāʻilaʻi in the genealogical sense (the line of the gods descends from the Kumulipo). But Pele is also **a rebel** — she was exiled, she refused the rules, she went her own way.

Lāʻilaʻi represents **continuity**, Pele represents **breakthrough**. Together they form the dynamic: **Roots → Outburst → New roots**.

### Kāne (65) — The Creative Principle

**Relationship:** Kāne is the abstract creator — sun, light, life. Pele is the concrete creator — fire, earth, destruction. Kāne says: "Let there be light." Pele says: "Let there be fire — and light will come from the ash."

In ZION: Kāne is "high-level vision", Pele is "ground-level execution". Kāne without Pele is only abstraction. Pele without Kāne is only chaos.

### Kanaloa (66) — Brother of the Ocean, Nāmaka

**Relationship:** Kanaloa is the ocean — the counterpart of Pele's fire. In fact Nāmaka (Pele's sister, the sea goddess) stands closer to the rivalry, but Kanaloa represents **the solemn opposite**: fire rises, water falls. Fire creates land, water washes it.

In ZION: Mining (Pele = fire, energy) and Liquidity (Kanaloa = water, flow) must be in balance. Too much fire = dried-up pools. Too much water = extinguished consensus.

### Lono (68) — Peace and Fertility

**Relationship:** Lono is the god of rain, harvest and peace — Makahiki. Pele is the god of war against petrification. Lono says: "Rest." Pele says: "Burn!" Together they form **the yearly cycle**: Makahiki (Lono) → Eruption season (Pele) → Makahiki again.

In ZION: a seasonal rotation between a "building phase" (Lono) and a "reforming phase" (Pele).
