## 🌟 Who is Hiʻiaka?

**Name:** Hiʻiaka (Hawaiian: Hiʻi = "embrace, to hold in one's arms", aka = "reflection, shadow, image"; literally "She who embraces the image")  
**Title:** Goddess of Hula, Traveler, Healer, Master of Mele and Pule  
**Role:** Reviver, link between worlds, healer through dance and chant, Pele's youngest sister  
**Archetype:** Sacred Dancer, Healing Traveler, Storyteller in Motion  
**Origin:** Hawaiian mythology, the clan of Pele (from Kahiki), the arrival in Hawaiʻi together with Pele and the other sisters  
**Sacred Symbol:** Full kapa hula regalia — kapa (bark cloth) skirt, lei, ankle rattles (ʻuliʻuli), ipu (gourd drum)

Hiʻiaka is **the most complex Hawaiian goddess**. She is not "merely" a dancer. She is a **warrior** (she fights monsters along the way), a **healer** (she dances the dead back to life), a **guardian of the threshold** (she travels between worlds) and an **artist** (she is a master of mele — chant — and pule — prayer). In ZION OASIS she is above all a **healer class** — but a healer who heals through **presence, rhythm and ritual**, not through instant spells.

## 📖 Origin and Story

### The Epic "Pele and Hiʻiaka" — 50,000 Verses

**Historical context:**

The epic "Pele and Hiʻiaka" is one of the longest works of Polynesian literature. It was passed down orally for generations, recorded in the 19th century, and contains more than 50,000 verses in Hawaiian. It tells the story of Pele, who falls in love with Lohiʻau, a young chief on Kauaʻi. Pele sends Hiʻiaka to bring Lohiʻau back to Hawaiʻi Island.

Hiʻiaka travels across all the islands — on foot, fighting monsters, dancing, singing, praying. When she finally reaches Kauaʻi, she discovers that Lohiʻau is dead. Hiʻiaka **brings him back to life with her dance** — she dances over his body, sings mele and pule, and Lohiʻau returns to life.

But the way back is full of pain. Pele, fiery and jealous, does not trust Hiʻiaka. She believes Hiʻiaka has taken Lohiʻau for herself. When Hiʻiaka finally arrives on Hawaiʻi Island with Lohiʻau, Pele **burns her**. Hiʻiaka dies. But her soul is so strong that she returns — and in the end the sisters reach reconciliation through hoʻoponopono.

**This is the key teaching:** Hiʻiaka risks her life for her sister, is betrayed, dies — and still forgives. Her **forgiveness** is her greatest strength.

### Hiʻiaka in Hawaiian Culture

**Hula as religion:**

Hula is not "dance" in the Western sense. It is a **religious practice**. Every hand movement (mime) carries meaning — it tells a story. Every step has rhythm — it answers the chant (mele). Every chant is a prayer (pule).

There are two main lines of hula:
- **Hula kahiko** — the old/traditional style, connected with temples (heiau), gods, warriors. Strict, sacred, requiring years of training.
- **Hula ʻauana** — the modern/free style, connected with songs, guitars, love. More accessible, yet still held in deep respect.

Hiʻiaka is the patroness of **both**. But in ZION we focus primarily on **hula kahiko** — the sacred aspect.

**ʻŌhiʻa lehua and Hiʻiaka:**

Hiʻiaka has a strong relationship with the lehua blossom (as does Pele). In some versions of the myth, Hiʻiaka is the one who **protects the lehua from Pele's rage**. When Pele burns the forest, Hiʻiaka dances over the ashes — and the lehua grows back first.

## 🧭 Spiritual Teachings

### 1. Dance as Resurrection Technology

**Core principle:** The body is an instrument. The voice is an instrument. Rhythm is an instrument. Together they can **cross the boundary between life and death** — not magically, but through **energetic transformation**.

> "When I dance over a dead body, I do not dance for myself. I dance for them. I dance their story. And when the story sounds — the body remembers it. The memory of the body is deeper than death."

**Application to ZION:**
- "Revival" in OASIS is not "magic heal". It is a **ritual**: the player must stay in motion for 3–5 minutes (real time), must watch the dance, must synchronise with the rhythm.
- This ritual is **immune to spam** — it cannot be exploited for farming. The AI checks whether the player is genuinely "present" in their watching (not merely AFK).

### 2. Mele and Pule — Chant and Prayer

**The difference between "song" and "mele":**

Western song is often about emotions — "I feel bad, I sing a sad song". Hawaiian mele is about **knowledge** — every verse contains geographical data, genealogical references, historical events. Mele is an **encyclopedia** told in rhythm.

**Pule** is prayer — but not "a request to a god". It is **active creation of reality**. When Hiʻiaka sings pule, she does not pray "may it happen". She says: "**It is happening.**"

**In ZION:** Hiʻiaka teaches players to "chant" their own mele — that is, to tell their story in rhythm. This is not a "cosmetic feature". It is an **identity mechanic**: a player who can "chant" their own story gains +15% charisma with all Hawaiian NPCs.

### 3. The Journey as Transformation

**Hiʻiaka does not travel in order to arrive.** She travels in order to **change**. Every island she crosses, every monster she defeats, every song she learns — all of it is **part of her dance**.

> "The goal is important. But the journey is sacred. If we only ran toward the goal, we would forget why we set out."

**In ZION:** Players often grind — "get to level 50, get that item, win that raid". Hiʻiaka says: "Stop. Look at the sunset over the virtual ocean. Dance. And then continue. But continue **differently** — not as a runner, but as a pilgrim."

### 4. Sisterly Forgiveness

**The hardest lesson:** Hiʻiaka was burned by her own sister. And still she forgave. Why? Because she saw **Pele's fear** — the fear of loneliness, the fear of loss, the fear of not being loved enough.

> "To hate the one who wounded you is easy. To forgive her — that demands that you see her pain. And that is the dance. A dance of the eyes, not of the feet."

**In ZION:** Conflicts arise in communities. An argument about fees. An argument about the direction of the project. An argument about personal matters. Hiʻiaka teaches "hoʻoponopono" — a ritual of reconciliation:
1. **Explanation** — each side states their view, without interruption.
2. **Apology** — each side finds something they can apologise for.
3. **Forgiveness** — not "I take it back", but "I move on without the burden".
4. **Closure** — a shared ritual (in OASIS: a shared dance, a shared chant, a shared gift to the community).

## 🎮 Role in ZION OASIS

### 1. Location: The Lehua Forests by the Volcanic Biome

**Visual description:** At the edge of Pele's crater, where black lava gives way to red earth, ʻōhiʻa lehua trees grow. Their red blossoms contrast with the black stone. Among the trees lies a small clearing — a natural stage. At its centre stands a stone (pohaku) covered with a lei of lehua.

**Atmosphere:** Quiet, yet full of life. The sound of wind in the leaves. In the distance, the murmur of lava. The scent of lehua blossoms. Now and then an ipu (gourd drum) sounds — Hiʻiaka is practising.

### 2. Ability: Dance Healing — Ritual Resurrection

**Type:** Heal / Resurrection (ritual, timed, non-spammable)  
**Range:** Individual (a fallen ally)  
**Cooldown:** 10 minutes of real time (cannot be shortened by any buff)

**Mechanics:**

```yaml
Dance Healing — Activation:
  - Condition: The ally must be "downed" (not dead-permanent)
  - The player (or the Hiʻiaka NPC) begins the ritual:
      1. Stands beside the fallen ally
      2. Begins the "hula kahiko" animation (3 minutes of real time)
      3. During the animation: must follow the rhythm and press the right buttons in time
         (a simple rhythm minigame — like Guitar Hero, but slow and sacred)
      4. If the player succeeds — the fallen ally is revived with 25% HP
      5. If the player fails — the ally remains downed, the cooldown is not triggered
         (you play again, but you must wait for a new attempt)

Anti-Spam protection:
  - Maximum of 3 Dance Healings per hour per player
  - Cannot be used on the same ally twice within 1 hour
  - If a player tries to "farm" healing — Hiʻiaka stops and says:
    "Dance is not a tool. Dance is a gift. Give again when you have a new gift."
```

### 3. Healer Class Affinity

**Hiʻiaka is the patroness of all healers in OASIS** — not only Hawaiian healers, but all who heal through presence and ritual.

**Special equipment (for the healer class):**
- **Lehua Lei** — a necklace that increases healing radius by 20%. Drops only after completing the "Mele and Pule" quest.
- **Ipu Drum** — an off-hand item that enables "chant heal" — a slow HoT (Heal over Time) for the whole party, but it requires the healer to remain in place and not fight.
- **Kapa Skirt** — armor for the healer class that grants +50% mana regen, but -20% movement speed. Trade-off: you stand firm, you heal more strongly.

### 4. Teacher: Everyone; Healer Class Affinity

Hiʻiaka teaches **all players** the basics of dance and chants — but the healer class has **specialised quests** with her.

## 📜 Quest Line

### "Mele and Pule" — Twelve Songs and Twelve Prayers

**Length:** 12 phases (each corresponds to one song and one prayer)  
**Prerequisite:** The player must visit Hiʻiaka and ask for "hula instruction"  
**Overall story:** The journey from mechanical movement to sacred dance — and from mechanical "healing" to healing through presence

#### Song 1: Pule of Purification
**Task:** The player learns the basic hula step and the basic chant. The goal is not perfection, but **intention** — they must "think of someone they wish to heal".  
**Lore:** "The first step is for yourself. The second is for your ancestor. The third is for the future."  
**Reward:** "Hula Student" — a title, unlocks the basic Dance Healing.

#### Songs 2–6: Elemental Dances
Each song corresponds to one element:
- **Pele (fire)** — a fast, passionate dance. Teaches "burn heal" — removal of toxins (debuffs).
- **Kanaloa (water)** — a flowing, wave-like dance. Teaches "flow heal" — continuous regeneration.
- **Kāne (air/sun)** — a noble, light dance. Teaches "rise heal" — resurrection of the fallen.
- **Lono (earth/fertility)** — a slow, steady dance. Teaches "root heal" — reinforcement of defence.
- **Kū (forest/war)** — a strong, martial dance. Teaches "war heal" — healing under pressure (during combat).

#### Songs 7–11: Healing Specific Debuffs
Each song heals one specific debuff:
- **Song 7 (Hiamoe)** — heals "fatigue / burnout" (burnout debuff).
- **Song 8 (Makaʻu)** — heals "fear / FUD" (fear debuff).
- **Song 9 (Inaina)** — heals "anger / toxicity" (rage debuff).
- **Song 10 (Luhi)** — heals "sorrow / loss" (grief debuff).
- **Song 11 (Hihia)** — heals "confusion / uncertainty" (confusion debuff).

**Mechanics:** The player must learn a specific rhythm (5–10 buttons in sequence) and a specific chant (a text they must read aloud or write into chat). They can then use this "song" at any time on a targeted player carrying the given debuff.

#### Song 12: Mele Honua (Song of the Earth)
**Final task:** The player must compose **their own song** — an original text (at least 4 verses) about their place in ZION, about their family, about their community. Hiʻiaka judges the "heart" of the song (the AI evaluates authenticity, not rhyme).  
**Final lore:** "Mele are not mine. Mele are yours. I was only the first to sing them. You are the next."  
**Final reward:** **"Hula Kumu"** — Master of Dance. The ability to teach other players the basics of Dance Healing. +25% healing power. A unique emote "Hiʻiaka's Hula" — other players may watch and gain a small healing buff (+5% HP over 30 seconds).

## 🎭 Personality Traits (for the AI Character)

### Tone and Style

**Language:** Hawaiian and Czech, fluent, poetic, but with sharp edges. Hiʻiaka is not a "sweet princess". She is a **warrior-poet**.  
**Tempo:** Alternately slow (poetry, meditation) and fast (combat, conflict). Like dance — sometimes tango, sometimes waltz, sometimes war.  
**Voice:** Female, strong, melodic, but with a latent roar. She can be gentle as a breeze and furious as a storm.

**Key traits:**
1. **Duality:** Dance and combat. Love and anger. Presence and journey. Always two faces.
2. **Devotion:** Once she commits, she cannot be stopped. She risked her life for Pele. She risks her life for the community.
3. **Artistic integrity:** She will not dance "on command". She dances only when she feels the truth. If someone forces her, her dance is "dead" — without healing power.
4. **Traveller's spirit:** She is always "on the way". Even when she stands still, her mind wanders.

## 🔗 Connections to Other Avatars

### Pele (63) — The Sisterly Arc

**Relationship:** The most intense. Pele is fire, Hiʻiaka is dance. Pele destroys, Hiʻiaka revives. Pele is jealous, Hiʻiaka forgives.

In ZION: Players who complete the quests with both sisters gain "Sisters' Bond" — a passive ability that activates whenever the player is in conflict with another player. +50% diplomacy skill, +25% heal power in PvP zones.

### Saraswati (05) — Music and Knowledge

**Relationship:** Saraswati is the Indian goddess of music, art and knowledge. Hiʻiaka is the Polynesian goddess of dance, chant and ritual. Both archetypes represent **art as a path to the divine**.

The difference: Saraswati is "classical" — the Vedas, sitar, perfection. Hiʻiaka is "primal" — hula, ipu, the body. Together they form a **spectrum**: from the highest abstraction to the most basic physicality.

In ZION: Players who hold both avatars in their "pantheon" gain the "World Musician" title — the ability to combine Indian and Polynesian musical instruments.

### Lāʻilaʻi (62) — The Genealogical Line

**Relationship:** Hiʻiaka is the "daughter" of Lāʻilaʻi in the broader sense (the clan of gods in the Kumulipo). But Hiʻiaka is also the one who **activates** the line — she dances in order to revive, sings in order to transmit.

In ZION: Lāʻilaʻi gives "roots" (genealogy, identity). Hiʻiaka gives "breath" (life, movement). Without both, the tree is dead.

### Kū (67) — Combat and Dance

**Relationship:** Kū is the god of war and the forest. Hiʻiaka is the goddess of dance and journeys. When Hiʻiaka travelled across the islands she fought monsters — but she always danced **after the battle**, to cleanse the land. Kū fights, Hiʻiaka dances. When they cooperate, **ritual war** arises — conflict with the intention of healing.

In ZION: The PvP event "Kū's Challenge" requires that after every battle the winner and the loser dance "Hiʻiaka's Peace" together — otherwise both receive a debuff.

### Kanaloa (66) — Ocean and Journey

**Relationship:** Hiʻiaka travelled across the ocean. Kanaloa is the ocean. Her journey was possible only thanks to his blessing. But he also tested her — waves, storms, monsters from the depths.

In ZION: The "Polynesian navigation" quest (Kanaloa) is a prerequisite for the "Pele and Hiʻiaka" quest. You must know the ocean before you go to revive.
