# Part II — The historical line of Amma and Bhagavan

*Ekam Deeksha — Chapters 3, 4 and 5*

## Chapter 3 — Jeevashram, Satyaloka and Oneness University

*The laboratory arc of the line of oneness*

---

Every living spiritual line has two layers. The first is the layer of statements, symbols and public language. The second is the layer of the laboratory, in which a certain type of consciousness is actually tested, formed and verified in the lives of concrete people. If we want to understand Ekam only as today's temple, brand or global programme, this second layer escapes us.

What later showed itself to the world as Oneness or Ekam did not begin as a finished ideology. It was born gradually as an experiment with human pain, relationships, education, perception and the possibility of real transformation.

---

### Jeevashram (1984–1994) — A field of formation

In **1984**, in Varadaiahpalem in the Chittoor district of Andhra Pradesh, Vijaykumar (later Bhagavan Sri Kalki) together with Padmavathi (later Amma) opened a school called **Jeevashram**. The name joins the words *jīv* (life, soul) and *āśram* (refuge, place of concentration). Literally: a refuge of the living.

Jeevashram was not a school in the ordinary sense of the word. It was a pedagogical experiment. Bhagavan formulated then the basic question that drove the whole line: *"What would happen if children grew up in an environment where the separating structure of self and other is not fed?"*

The school worked with children in a way that departed from the standard curriculum. Emphasis was placed on natural intelligence, relationality, inner calm, individual rhythm and the least possible traumatisation. Many of these practices today bear the name of alternative pedagogy or mindfulness in education — but Jeevashram applied them in the 1980s, long before these notions became mainstream.

From this first field came the first generation of people who carried a living experience of another way of being. And precisely this generation became the germ of the later Oneness community.

---

### Satyaloka (1994–2003) — A field of truth and process

Satyaloka — *satya* (truth) + *loka* (world, sphere) — is the name for an expanding field of practice that took shape in the 1990s. Its physical centre became the Varadaiahpalem area, but Satyaloka as a notion denotes rather **a state and a process** than a specific place.

In this period a fundamental shift occurs: **from formation to transmission**. It turned out that a pedagogical environment — however good — is not enough to change the basic state of consciousness. What was needed was a direct transmission that bypasses intellectual filtering.

Bhagavan described this transition as follows: *"We found that when we taught, people learned. But when we transmitted, people changed. That is the difference between a lesson and Deeksha."*

In this period the specific practice of **Sparsha Deeksha** also crystallised — transmission by touch on the head. Thousands of people went through this process at Satyaloka and reported transformative experiences: deep calm, a feeling of connectedness, emotional breakthroughs, a new seeing of relationships.

Scientifically speaking, these reports remained in the domain of anecdotal evidence. But their consistency — similar descriptions from people of different cultures, education and religious background — suggested that this was a recurring phenomenon, not a random placebo effect.

---

### Oneness University (2003–2012) — A field of language and global spread

In **2003** the **Oneness University** campus was opened in Andhra Pradesh — a large complex intended for intensive processes of transformation, which as a rule lasted 21 days. This moment marks the transition of the line from a regional experiment to a global movement.

The name *university* is symptomatic. On the one hand it accepts the modern longing for method, structure and comprehensibility. On the other it stretches its own boundaries, because what was to be shared here was not only teaching. What was to be shared here was the possibility of another state of consciousness. And it is precisely in this tension that Oneness University must be read.

#### Processes and structure

The basic format was a **21-day process** — a deep immersion combining:
- meditation in silence
- group Deeksha (transmission from facilitators and from Amma and Bhagavan themselves)
- contemplation in specially designed spaces
- process group work focused on relationships, family and trauma
- direct meeting with Amma and Bhagavan

Participants came from all continents. In 2007, some 60,000 people from more than 100 countries went through the processes of Oneness University. Many returned several times. A global network of **Oneness Blessing Givers** (Deeksha facilitators) arose, whose number exceeded 150,000 in 2010.

#### Strength and tension

Every such movement carries both an immense transformative power and the inherent tension of institutionalisation. As soon as a living field of transformation begins to spread into many countries, it must create programmes, processes, roles, terminology and organisation. That is inevitable. At the same time it holds that every institutionalisation carries the risk that what was originally living grace will appear as a reproducible technique.

This critique of Oneness University was heard especially around 2011–2013, when part of the community distanced itself from the organisation's commercial pressure. **Bhagavan himself acknowledged this tension.** In internal interviews he said that every spiritual organisation goes through a phase in which the form threatens what it carried. And that the remedy is not to preserve the organisation at any cost, but to be willing to transform it.

## Chapter 4 — Amma and Bhagavan as a twofold principle

*Love and insight — compassion and liberation*

---

### Biography and context

**Vijaykumar Naidu** — later **Sri Kalki Bhagavan** — was born on **7 March 1949** in Nathur, Andhra Pradesh. From childhood people around him described him as essentially different: deeply quiet, naturally compassionate, capable of a presence that awakened calm in others.

**Padmavathi** — later **Sri Amma** — was born on **15 August 1954**. They met at a young age and soon began together to seek a new way of education and formation, which culminated in Jeevashram.

Their marriage in 1969 was the beginning of a partnership that goes beyond the personal level. In the Oneness and Ekam tradition, Amma and Bhagavan are understood not as individual beside individual, but as a **twofold principle** — a polarity that makes a whole.

---

### Symbolic polarity

In Indian philosophy there is an ancient pattern for describing creative forces: **Shiva and Shakti**, **Brahma and Sarasvati**, **Vishnu and Lakshmi**. Always these are pairs in which the principle of consciousness and the principle of energy, the principle of insight and the principle of love, together form a dynamic that carries the world.

In the Ekam line, Amma and Bhagavan are read as the living fulfilment of this pattern:

| Principle | Amma | Bhagavan |
|---------|------|---------|
| Element | Love, compassion, acceptance | Insight, liberating clarity |
| Function | To warm, to accept, to care | To illuminate, to dissolve illusion |
| Energy | Shakti — movement, the presence of the body | Shiva — silence, consciousness |
| Image | Mother, heart | Father, light |

Amma is described as the **seat of compassion** — the inseparable acceptance of every person exactly where they are. Bhagavan as the **seat of knowledge** — a penetrating clarity that sees through illusions and brings a liberating confrontation with truth.

---

### What this means for practice

In the Oneness and Ekam community this principle manifests concretely: **to balance love and truth**. Awakening without love leads to cold detachment. Love without truth leads to sweet self-deception. Only together do they form the condition for real transformation.

Bhagavan formulated it: *"Amma melts, I illuminate. Amma accepts, I show. Amma heals, I liberate. Without her my teaching would be cruelty. Without me her love would be without meaning."*

---

### The transmission of leadership — Krishnaji and Preethaji

Around 2016 the transition of leadership from the founders to their children began:

**Sri Preethaji** (whose own name is Preethaji Krishnaji, *b. approximately 1981*) — the wife of Sri Krishnaji, co-author of the books *The Way of the Awakened* (2019) and *Four Sacred Secrets* (2019). She is considered a profound teacher of meditation and relationships.

**Sri Krishnaji** (*b. approximately 1986*) — the son of Amma and Bhagavan. Together with Preethaji he led the global O&O Academy (Oneness and Oneness) and shifted the emphasis of the line from process immersion to more accessible formats — lectures, online programmes, community practice.

This generational transition was difficult for part of the older community, but both successors clearly preserve the core: **the form does not matter, the transformation of consciousness does**. At the same time they bring their own contribution: a deeper connection with psychological models (Preethaji) and with philosophical traditions and the global context (Krishnaji).

## Chapter 5 — From Oneness University to Ekam

*A transformation of form, a preservation of the core*

---

### The transition of 2012–2016

Around **2012** Oneness University begins to pass through a silent reformulation. The name gradually transforms. The processes change in structure and length. A new identity begins to form: **Ekam**.

Ekam — in Sanskrit **"one"** — is a designation that identifies neither an organisation nor a tradition. It identifies **a state**. This choice is programmatic: the line does not root itself in the history of the founders nor in a method — it anchors itself in an ontological fact. One being. One reality. One germ from which everything proceeds.

---

### The Ekam temple

In **2018** the **Ekam** temple was opened in Varadaiahpalem — a monumental building of white marble, designed as a physical map of consciousness.

The architecture of the temple is not accidental. Every element carries a symbolism:

- **The outer shell** — white purity, marble from Rajasthan — a symbol of transparency, without the colouring of ego identity
- **The centre — the Sanctum** with the **Golden Orb** — a golden sphere as the physical symbol of Hiranyagarbha, the golden germ
- **The access axes** — orientation according to the cardinal points and the solar movement
- **Sri Chakra geometry** — a mandala consisting of nine interlocking triangles, the most complex yantra of the Vedic tradition — is set into the ground geometry of the complex
- **Meditation halls** — designed for group work with silence and the field of consciousness

---

### O&O Academy and global presence

Under the leadership of Krishnaji and Preethaji, the **O&O Academy** arises — a platform that carries the core of Ekam into a more accessible format:

- Meditation programmes (online and live)
- Academic cooperation with researchers of consciousness
- Conferences connecting spiritual practice with psychology, neuroscience and philosophy
- Meditation guides: *The Way of the Awakened*, *A Handful of Quietness*, *Four Sacred Secrets*

This openness is a conscious choice. Krishnaji and Preethaji repeatedly emphasise that Ekam does not want to be an exclusive tradition for the initiated, but an **open field** for everyone who seeks.

---

### What remained unchanged

Through all the transformations of form — from school to ashram, from ashram to organisation, from organisation to temple, from temple to global network — one core remained unchanged:

**The conviction that a change of consciousness is possible. That awakening is not a myth. That Deeksha — transmission — can catalyse what the mind alone cannot.**

And precisely this continuity is what gives the Ekam line a legitimacy transcending organisational form. The line survived its forms because its core was never stored only in an institution, but in a living transmission from person to person.
