## Chapter 9 — The architecture of oneness

*White marble, sacred geometry and the consciousness of space*

---

### The temple as a map of consciousness

The word **temple** (Latin *templum*, Sanskrit *mandir* or *devalaya*) originally denoted a bounded sacred space — a place of meeting of the heavenly and the earthly. In the Indian tradition a mandir is not merely a building for ritual. It is a **physical cosmology** — a microcosm in which architecture is a map of consciousness itself.

The **Ekam** temple in Varadaiahpalem (*Andhra Pradesh, India*), opened in **2018**, was designed as the most ambitious physical realisation of the line of oneness. White marble brought from Rajasthan, the complex set into a green hill, proportions derived from the canon of Vastu Shastra — Vedic architecture.

*Vastu Shastra* (वास्तु शास्त्र) is the traditional Indian architectural science that understands space as a living system of energetic fields. The orientation of buildings towards the cardinal points, the ratios of lengths and heights, the placement of windows and passages — all of this is codified in Vastu Shastra according to how space is to resonate with the principles of cosmic order: sun, moon, planetary forces and natural elements.

---

### Sri Chakra — the basis of the geometry of Ekam

The central geometric principle of the whole complex is the **Sri Chakra** (श्रीचक्र), also called Sri Yantra. It is the most complex mandala of the Vedic tradition: nine interpenetrating triangles arranged around a central point — the **bindu**.

Four triangles with the apex downward (Shakti — the feminine principle, movement, energy)
Five triangles with the apex upward (Shiva — the masculine principle, consciousness, silence)

Their intersection creates **43 smaller triangles**, bounded by two rings of lotus petals and an outer square with the thresholds of the four cardinal points.

In the language of tantric philosophy the Sri Chakra is not merely an image — it is a **living map of the consciousness of the universe and of the consciousness of a human being at once**. Meditation within the Sri Chakra or in a space built on its basis leads, as the tradition holds, to a feeling of the oneness of microcosm and macrocosm.

This principle was integrated into the ground geometry of the Ekam temple: in the plan, in the elevations of the tower, in the placement of the meditation halls and access paths, the proportion derived from the Sri Chakra repeats.

---

### Sacred space as a catalyst

In modern environmental psychology there is a well documented phenomenon: **architecture affects the psyche**. The height of ceilings correlates with a feeling of freedom and a soaring of thought. The presence of natural elements (water, stone, trees) lowers cortisol. The proportions of rooms affect the feeling of safety or threat.

In the traditional context this principle reaches much further: **sacred space is designed as a catalyst for the transformation of consciousness**. Not by manipulating consciousness, but by creating conditions in which the natural movement towards quieting and openness is made easier.

The Ekam temple was designed with this claim as its basic design axiom. Every detail — light entering at a specific angle at a specific time of day, the sound of water elements, the material texture of the stone — is an attempt to create an environment that by the silence of its own composition invites human consciousness to an analogous quieting.

## Chapter 10 — The Golden Orb

*The golden centre — the embryo of a new identity*

---

### Physical description

At the centre of the Sanctum of the Ekam temple — at the place corresponding to the *bindu* of the Sri Chakra — there is the **Golden Orb**: a golden sphere approximately 1.5 metres in diameter, set on a pedestal in a room designed as the culmination of the energetic composition of the whole temple.

Material: gold sheet on a steel structure.
Surface finish: a matt golden sheen, without ornament.
Lighting: natural light coming from a window at the apex.

By the simplicity of its appearance the Golden Orb is visually the opposite of the baroque maximalism of most Indian temple decoration. This plainness is intentional: **a symbol must not shout down what it points to**.

---

### The symbolism of the sphere in spiritual traditions

The sphere as a symbol is one of the most universal. Across cultures it shares a specific set of connotations:

- **Wholeness without beginning and end** — no side, no hierarchy, no centre exceptional above the others
- **Potentiality** — the sphere as an egg, a germ from which anything can arise
- **Solar energy** — the sphere as the disc of the sun, the source of light and life
- **Transcendence of duality** — where there is no edge, there is no duality

In the Ekam line the Golden Orb is explicitly connected with **Hiranyagarbha** — the golden germ of Vedic cosmology (see Part V). It is the physical materialisation of the oldest cosmological image: the egg from which the universe is born.

---

### The Golden Orb as a meditative object

In the space of the Sanctum the golden sphere is not merely decoration. It is designed as a **yantra** — a meditative object, a mediator. A fidget for the mind: concentrating the gaze on a form without edge and without end naturally leads the mind to let go of analysis and open to quieting.

In the Ekam meditation instructions, as Krishnaji describes them, meditation with the Golden Orb includes:

1. Physical presence — the feeling of the body, of the breath
2. Visual contact with the sphere without striving "for something"
3. A phase of contemplation: *"What is this?"* — not an answer, but an open question in presence
4. A transition from seeing the object to experiencing the space in which the object is
5. A possible breakthrough: the vanishing of the boundary between observer, space and object

---

### The embryo of a new identity

The most strongly resonating metaphor around the Golden Orb is the **embryo** — a germ for which the old identity has become a shell.

In the traditional biological understanding: an egg holds the substance from which a new life is born. A seed carries the code of a tree. An embryo carries the whole potentiality of an organism — long before it manifests.

In the context of Ekam: **every human being carries within themselves the germ of a consciousness that needs no defence**. Not as a possibility of a distant future, but as a present fact — covered by layers of charges, traumas, conditioning and cultural patterns.

The Golden Orb is a physical symbolic instruction: what you are lies within. Beneath all the noise. Beneath all the defence. Gold needs no justification, confirmation or improvement. **It is.**

---

### The Golden Orb in ZION TerraNova

In the Cosmic Harmony v3 algorithm — the proof-of-work algorithm of the ZION blockchain — the first stage of the six-phase pipeline carries the name **Hiranyagarbha**. It is the initialisation phase: the seed from the block header unfolds into a primordial state — the golden germ of the computation.

Every mined block begins with this germ and passes through a process whose goal is the attainment of **Samadhi** — unification, a valid proof-of-work. The technique thus carries the philosophy literally — in the code, in the data structure, in every cycle of computation.

**A block of the blockchain is a digital golden egg.** It begins in chaos, passes through structure, attains unification. And a valid block — as an act of naturally attained oneness — is accepted by the network without the need for a central authority.

## Chapter 11 — Ekam as a planetary field

*Group meditation, a critical mass of consciousness, a local temple and a global network*

---

### The hypothesis of a planetary field

One of the most ambitious — and most difficult to grasp — theses of the Ekam line is the hypothesis of a **planetary field of consciousness**:

> *"Consciousness is not only individual. It is a shared field. As electromagnetism pervades space, as the field of gravity acts at a distance — so the field of consciousness can be collectively cultivated or polluted. A critical mass of awakened consciousness changes the quality of the whole field."*

This hypothesis is not a novelty of the Ekam line. Analogous ideas were formulated by the physicist David Bohm (the implicate order), the biologist Rupert Sheldrake (morphogenetic fields), the psychologist Carl Jung (the collective unconscious) and the sociologist Émile Durkheim (collective consciousness). In the line of Sri Aurobindo one speaks of **"supermental consciousness"** as a collective field that spreads as an evolutionary leap.

Bhagavan made this hypothesis concrete: *"There are now seven or eight billion people in the world. Not even one per cent of them have tasted real liberation. And yet — if one million consciousnesses tasted a real breakthrough out of separateness, the pressure on the whole field would change in a quantum way."*

---

### The Ekam World Peace Festival

The concrete manifestation of this hypothesis is the **Ekam World Peace Festivals** — global meditation events, held as a rule in June or July, when hundreds of thousands of people are connected in simultaneous meditation:

- A central stream from the Ekam temple — 10,000–50,000 participants present on the site
- An online stream — hundreds of thousands of meditators from home at synchronised times
- Local satellite groups in dozens of countries

The festival is not merely a meditation event. It is an **experiment with the hypothesis of a collective field**. The question to which Ekam seeks an empirical answer: *Do measurable indicators in the world change in the week preceding, during and following the festival?*

This question runs into extremely difficult methodological problems (causality vs. correlation, the choice of indicators, control groups). But the very effort to pose it moves the Ekam project from purely spiritual practice into a domain that at least in part attempts empirical verification.

---

### Comparison: Maharishi's TM and the Maharishi Effect

The closest analogous project is the **Transcendental Meditation of Maharishi Mahesh Yogi** and the so-called **Maharishi Effect** — the hypothesis that group TM in 1 % of a population reduces crime and social tension. Research on this in TM is controversial: some studies demonstrate a correlation, critics point to methodological shortcomings.

Ekam did not take over the TM model, but learns from its attempts. The difference is in openness: Ekam actively invites researchers to cooperate and does not push its hypothesis as dogma, but as a hypothesis that deserves rigorous examination.

---

### Local temples and the global network

The physical Ekam temple in Andhra Pradesh is a node of the global field — but not its monopolistic centre. In the Ekam line there is a model of **satellite groups** — local communities around the world that meditate regularly (weekly or monthly), connected online with live transmissions from the temple or with recordings.

This decentralisation is a conscious choice: the field of consciousness must not depend on one place, one guru or one organisation. It must be alive in the world — in schools, families, businesses, communities.

**A local group of meditators becomes a node of the network of consciousness** — analogously to how every node of the internet is part of a global system without being dependent on a centre for its function.

This visionary infrastructure is today at an early stage. But if the hypothesis of the field of consciousness proves valid, this decentralised architecture could be one of the most important design decisions in the history of the global spiritual movement.
