# Part III — Deeksha as a technology of consciousness

*Ekam Deeksha — Chapters 6, 7 and 8*

## Chapter 6 — What Deeksha is

*Transmission, initiation, the quieting of the mind*

---

### Etymology and basis

**Deeksha** (Devanagari: दीक्षा, also written *dīkṣā*) is a Sanskrit expression whose root lies in the verb *dīkṣ* — to consecrate, to prepare, to establish. In traditional Indian philosophy it denotes:

1. Formal initiation into a spiritual tradition (shiṣya-Deeksha)
2. The transmission of mantric energy from guru to disciple (mantra-Deeksha)
3. Initiation into yoga, meditation or a special practice (kriya-Deeksha)
4. In the broader sense: every form of handing over that transforms the receiver

In the Oneness and Ekam line the term Deeksha is used in an extended sense that includes all these layers but adds a specific accent: **transmission is not primarily a transmission of information, method or tradition. It is a transmission of a quality of consciousness that transcends the individual psyche.**

---

### Forms of Deeksha

#### 1. Sparsha Deeksha — transmission by touch

The best-known form. A facilitator (earlier called a Oneness Blessing Giver, today a Deeksha Giver or Ekam Meditator) gently places both palms on the crown area of the receiver — approximately above the crown chakra — and holds them for 1–2 minutes in a state of deep presence and intention.

Physical contact functions here as a **conductor** — the conductive medium of transmission, not its source. The source is in the field of consciousness to which the facilitator tries to be transparent.

Receivers describe various responses:
- deep calm and the quieting of mental noise
- thermal or electrical sensations in the area of the head and spine
- emotional release (weeping, laughter, a feeling of liberation)
- flashes of clarity or presence
- in some cases visual or auditory experiences

#### 2. Smarana Deeksha — transmission by intention

The transmission takes place without physical touch — through the power of presence and the intention of the facilitator or the group. Bhagavan described Smarana as *"the transmission of pure consciousness across space"*.

This form became the basis for group and online Ekam meditations: thousands of people meditating synchronously from different continents form whatever can be described as a **distributed field of Deeksha**. From the point of view of physics this is immeasurable. From the point of view of experience it is a repeatedly reported phenomenon.

#### 3. Oneness Meditation / Ekam Meditation

A structured group meditation led by a facilitator or by a recorded process (video, audio of the voice of Amma or Bhagavan). It contains:
- a phase of quieting and bodily presence
- a passage through specific emotions or themes (gratitude, pain, connection)
- a phase of receiving Deeksha from the group field
- a phase of silence and integration

At the Ekam World Peace Festivals — when 10,000–100,000 people meditate at the same time — this form is the most extensive.

#### 4. Jnana Deeksha — transmission through word and presence

In a traditional guru meeting, Deeksha comes through the word itself — not as information, but as a carrier of living energy. Bhagavan's or Amma's talks and satsangs are perceived in the Ekam line as a form of Jnana Deeksha: *"It does not lie in the content. It lies in the field from which the words come."*

#### 5. Paduka Deeksha

Paduka — sandals — are in the Indian tradition a sacred object of the guru. Touch, meditation or presence with the Paduka of Amma or Bhagavan is understood as a form of transmission even at a distance, through a physical symbol.

---

### What Deeksha claims to do

From the perspective of the Ekam line, Deeksha has these functions:

1. **Quieting the restlessness of the mind** — reducing the intensity of constant inner commentary
2. **Releasing "charges"** — emotional residues stored in the body/nervous system
3. **Opening perception** — a transition from an analytical to a global mode of processing sensory input
4. **Catalysing a breakthrough** — in the optimal case, a break in the identification with separation; the experience of oneness
5. **Stabilising the "Beautiful State"** — a state of calm presence as baseline, not as exception

Bhagavan emphasised repeatedly: *"Deeksha does not add. It takes away. It takes away what obstructs the natural state of oneness. The natural state is always present — it is only covered."*

---

### Neuroscience and Deeksha

A research team cooperating with Oneness University (a study of 2004–2008) recorded during Sparsha Deeksha and group Oneness Meditation, using EEG and fMRI:

- **A decrease in beta waves** (associated with analytical thinking and inner dialogue) during the transmission
- **An increase in alpha and theta** (associated with a relaxed state, creativity, meditative presence)
- **Activation of the parietal area** — typically associated with a feeling of connectedness, a reduction of the self/world boundary
- **A transient decrease in the activity of the Default Mode Network** — the network of brain activity largely responsible for the inner narration of the "I"

These findings do not explain the mechanism — but **they confirm a biological correlate of the experience** that Deeksha describes phenomenologically. The brain is doing something different. The experience changes.

## Chapter 7 — The anatomy of transmission

*A change of perception, emotional release, grace and readiness*

---

### The "charges" model — energetic imprints of the past

Bhagavan introduced a key operative term: **charge**. It is a metaphor for experiences of the past that were not fully lived through and integrated at the moment, and therefore are stored as latent tension patterns in the nervous system and the body.

Each emotional charge:
- activates the ego into a defensive mode
- filters subsequent perception (we see a threat where there need not be one)
- is triggered by similar situations in the present
- keeps the structure of the separate self in chronic vigilance

In traditional psychological language a similar phenomenon is called **traumatic conditioning**, **attachment wounds** or an **emotional block**. The term "charge", however, emphasises its energetic — not merely cognitive — character.

According to Bhagavan, Deeksha does not act primarily on the level of movement — through verbal processing or cognitive reframing. It acts through an **energetic intervention into another layer**: it returns the system to the state before conditioning, allows the charge to pass through and leave without the necessity of reliving it in its full traumatic sense.

---

### Grace and readiness

One of the most discussed questions in the Oneness community has always been: *"Why does Deeksha work for some people immediately and for others less, or not at all?"*

Bhagavan's model distinguished two components:

1. **Grace** (*kripa*, *anugraha*) — the natural power of the line and of the transmission, which is not conditioned by the receiver. It is a gift — in theistic language a divine gift, in secular language the gift of a living field of consciousness.

2. **Readiness** (*yogyatā*) — the capacity of the receiver to accept the transmission. It depends on:
   - the amount of accumulated charges and blocks
   - the willingness to let go of control and trust the process
   - the presence of a basic *bhakti* — surrender, not blind, but as a conscious choice to open

This combination creates a spectrum: from an immediate breakthrough, through a gradual, repeated accumulation of experience, to an apparent "nothing is happening" — which, however, may be a phase of quiet preparation.

---

### Transmission and the relationship with the facilitator

Deeksha is not exclusively a transmission from a guru. In the Oneness line a democratisation was encoded: anyone who completes a preparatory course and receives authorisation may give Sparsha Deeksha.

This is a conscious decision that Bhagavan defended: *"If Deeksha is grace, then grace must not be withheld within a small circle of the elect. It must flow freely. The facilitator is a pipe — not the source."*

Nevertheless it holds that the depth of the transmission depends on the state of the facilitator. The more the facilitator is themselves anchored in silence and in the absence of an ego agenda, the more purely the transmission takes place. That is why the practice for facilitators includes their own regular meditation, contemplation, group processes and the ongoing receiving of Deeksha.

---

### Transmission as a shared culture

Deeksha transcends the individual therapeutic act. It is a **cultural pattern**. Societies in which the transmission of conscious presence and grace is a shared practice — in the family, the school, the community — form a different type of social environment than those in which the dominant mode of relationship is transaction and performance.

In her lectures Sri Preethaji describes the Japanese phenomenon of *amae* — a specific form of dependent trust and acceptance that is socially normalised in Japanese culture — as an example of how a cultural pattern of transmitting care can shape a collective psychology. **Deeksha is an attempt to create an analogous pattern, transferable across cultural boundaries**, and to anchor it not in the randomness of culture but in a conscious choice of community.

## Chapter 8 — Deeksha, karma and relationships

*The family as a field of awakening — wound, forgiveness, the ontological role of love*

---

### The family as the most intensive laboratory

Bhagavan said: *"If you want to know how far you are in your awakening, do not sit in a temple. Come home. Sit with your parents, your partner, your children. The truth lies there."*

In the Oneness/Ekam line **the family is understood as the primary field of awakening** — not as an obstacle to spiritual seeking. This approach runs against a strong current of Indian spiritual tradition, in which the monastic path (*sannyāsa*) is often considered higher than the path of the householder.

The reason is pragmatic: our deepest charges were formed in the family field. That is precisely where they took root. And therefore they must also be integrated there — not in the quiet space of a monastery, where the space is relatively safe, but in the living friction field of everyday relationship.

---

### Karma in the language of Oneness

The word **karma** (karmā) in the traditional Indian language is the law of cause and effect — a cosmic lawfulness according to which every act bears fruit in future lives or in the present one. In the language of Oneness/Ekam, karma receives a more psychological reading:

> *"Karma is not a punishment of fate. It is a pattern that repeats because it has not been integrated. Every recurring emotional pattern in a relationship — jealousy, abandonment, control, resistance — is karmic conditioning. Deeksha does not release it by erasing the past. It releases it by detaching consciousness from the compulsion to repeat."*
> — Bhagavan, Oneness University 2008

---

### Processes of relational Deeksha

In the 21-day processes of Oneness University there was a specific module devoted to parental relationships — called the **Parental Blessing Process**:

1. The receiver in meditation re-enters the key moments of the relationship with mother and father
2. Without suppression or dramatisation, pain, rejection, disappointment and guilt are present
3. In a state of deep presence (a field held by the facilitator) the charge is allowed to run through without a reactive pattern
4. A movement is cultivated from the feeling of victimhood towards an understanding of the parents' context
5. Space opens for **gratitude without denial** — to accept pain and gift at the same time

Hundreds of reports from these processes describe deep breakthrough encounters with parents — a marked change in the relationship to them. Not as psychological forgiveness, but as a change of perception: I stop being the child who was wounded and see a person who was themselves wounded and did what they could.

---

### The ontological role of love

At the heart of the Ekam line lies a conviction that goes further than an ethical call to kindness: **love is an ontological reality, not an emotional state**.

The separate self experiences love as a transient state dependent on another person: it comes and goes according to whether the other fulfils our needs. But the consciousness of oneness experiences love as a **natural texture of being** — not as a feeling that comes and goes, but as the ground beneath all experience.

Sri Preethaji formulates it in *Four Sacred Secrets* (2019): *"Love is not what you feel. It is what you are. The problem is not a lack of love — the problem is that we suppose we must seek love, earn it or obtain it from others. But love is what we are woven from."*

This thesis — love as an ontological foundation, not primarily an emotional state — is one of the deepest points of encounter between the Ekam line and philosophical traditions from Plato (*erōs as longing for the origin*) through Spinoza (*amor intellectualis Dei*) to Levinas's phenomenological notion (*the face of the other as the source of ethical obligation*).
