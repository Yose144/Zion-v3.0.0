# Království srdce · Nirvana — the Ark (13th node · sails with humpback whales)

> **L5 Free World · the thirteenth Terra Nova node** · Status: 🟣 Vision — concept and four working studies
> *“Build yourself a ship. Not because a flood is coming — but because one day someone will be in the water.”*

---

## Purpose

Twelve L5 nodes stand on land: gardens, temples, governance laboratories, relationship nodes on sacred ground — and María del Camino, the sailing line that connects them. **The thirteenth node is different.** It is not a place, and it is not a pilgrims' ship either. It is **a harbour that moves**.

**Království srdce · Nirvana** is an ark: a sailing colony of at most one hundred people, a seed vault, a library of civilisation and a full node of the chain — and above all a ship that **knows how to come back**. Where the three Marys carry the way *outward*, the ark carries the way **home**: for a person, for a seed, for a book, for a skill.

## The fourth vessel

When the three Marys sailed onto three oceans, one question stayed on the shore that no sail can answer:

> *“The pilgrims' ships carry those who set out. But who sets out for those who cannot?”*

So the ark is not a new ship. It is **the same ship built again** — larger, in steel, with seeds instead of animals and one hundred people instead of eight — and on its keel it carries the same name every hull carries that brings people home.

| | Tres Marías | The ark |
|---|---|---|
| Who | pilgrims | a colony — families, children, all generations |
| How many | ~14–15 per hull | ≤ 100 |
| Purpose | the way outward — initiation, learning | the way back — rescue, archive, renewal |
| Deck | sails as light | production decks, archive, workshop |
| Anchor | a node of the network | none — a moving harbour |

## What the ark carries

1. **Citizen-colony** — one hundred people as a self-sufficient village: energy from wind and light, water from the sea, food from the deck, governance by a sociocratic constitution. Around forty critical roles are covered **twice** — two of everything, engines included. *If it works at sea forever, it works anywhere for a while.*
2. **An archive that sails** — a seed vault at −18 °C, thirty to sixty thousand accessions of priority crops, duplicated on land.
3. **The memory of civilisation** — a library without the internet: books, medicines, manuals, charts, technical documentation and a full chain node in a shielded rack.
4. **Rescue capacity, honestly declared** — space for those taken aboard, medivac and isolation as a module, not as drama.
5. **A teaching ship** — what the ark knows is passed on: every node learns to be a small ark.

## Deck arrangement — study B3

A concept study (not a production plan): roughly 85 m working length, a community of up to 100, a steel hull with wooden interiors. Stability, capacity and construction have to be verified by naval architects.

| Deck | What is on it |
|---|---|
| **Upper** | Navigation bridge and communications, a shared terrace with seating, solar panels, a glasshouse for growing food and herbs, safe walk-around decks |
| **Main** | Common dining room with several tables, kitchen and back-of-house, library and school, quiet room / prayer, infirmary, workshops and creative space |
| **Accommodation** | Family cabins, double-bunk cabins, shared sanitary facilities, laundry and cleaning, storage, two separate stairways, a quiet zone forward |
| **Technical spaces** | Food and material stores, water tanks, battery room, auxiliary generators, electric propulsion (×2), service spaces, separate safe subdivision |

**Construction intent:** steel frame and plating, watertight subdivision, light superstructures, service access.
**Propulsion and energy:** sails, auxiliary electric drive, batteries, solar, emergency generators.

## Whale navigation

The humpback does exactly what the ark needs: **it feeds in cold productive waters in summer and calves in the tropics in winter** — and keeps to continental shelves and seamounts all the while. Its corridors also alternate in precisely the rhythm in which the two hemispheres' cyclone seasons alternate. So the ark does not plan against the weather, but **with it**.

| Season | Hemisphere | Humpbacks | The ark | Node |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| December – April | northern winter | Caribbean and Baja — calving grounds | Caribbean → Costa Rica | **LUMI** |
| April – June | northern transition | northern migration along the shelf | Azores and Canaries → Iberia | **Genesis Garden** |
| June – October | northern summer | feeding off Iceland, Norway, Greenland | Iberia → Azores → temperate Atlantic | Tomar · Galicia |
| October – November | southward transition | southern migration toward the equator | Cape Verde → equatorial passage | — |
| June – October | southern winter | Tonga, Fiji, Raiatea, Mozambique | Tahiti and Raiatea → Tonga → Fiji | **Te Pīko Ora** |
| November – March | southern summer | feeding off Antarctica | NZ → southern Africa (the ark only to the edge, ~40–50° S) | **Boa Esperança** |
| year-round | Indian Ocean | the only non-migrating population | a circuit of the Indian Ocean | **Bodhi Lanka** |

The result is a **two- to three-year circuit of the world (~30,000 nm)** that visits one node of the network on every leg — the ark becomes a moving link of the network, not an isolated bunker. The route avoids both hemispheres' cyclone windows, passes risky straits in convoy, and only visits the ice edge as a reserve: *whales may go higher, the ark does not.*

## Food, water, energy — how it holds

- **Food** — greenhouse hydroponics, aquaponics, sprouting, poultry, rabbits, micro-goats, spirulina, a mushroom room and fishing on the shelves. Kitchen waste returns to the farm; the seed vault is both a bank and a larder.
- **Water** — desalination in the window when the wind blows or the sun shines; the superstructure roof doubles as rain catchment; grey water for technical use.
- **Energy** — a photovoltaic skin on the roof, wind turbines on frames, hydro-generation under sail; batteries cover the days without sun or wind. **Diesel is a medicine cabinet, not a fridge.**

## Surviving the storm — “ark mode”

A closed profile for a mega-storm: minimum openings below deck, three-compartment standard, a double bottom along the full length, passive stabilisation, a sea anchor and a citadel core where people gather. The goal is not to outrun the storm — the goal is to **survive it**. Every critical system is aboard twice: desalination, generators, steering, communications.

## Memory and the chain

The third living cargo besides people and seeds is **memory**: an offline library, technical documentation, the printed core of the corpus — and **a full chain node with a ledger archive**. The ark carries the network in such a way that the network survives a restart: even as its only node, it remains the memory. Communication rests on HF radio, which needs no infrastructure; satellite is a bonus, not a dependency.

## Patron and the name on the keel

The **Little Prince** sails on all three hulls of the Tres Marías — in red, white and gold robes. Aboard the ark he receives **a fourth robe: the colour of the sea**. It is not aesthetics, it is craft — the pitch that seals the hull inside and out is born of blue water and returns to it. The child stands on the prow where María del Camino set down her lantern, holds the world in his palm and looks into the dark the lantern illuminates.

**The Kingdom of the Heart** is not the name of a new ship — it is the name carried by every hull that brings people home. It began in a carpenter's workshop in Nazareth, continued with a boat on the Kinneret and a fleet that gathers, and returns aboard the ark in its largest form. Which is why the ark is not a continuation of the flood story. It is the story of **water never getting the last word**.

**Horizon for a working ark: ~3.5–6 years from the study.** Until then the programme is a vision with documents you can read — and that is its entire status.

## What the ark is not

- **It is not a finished ship.** No keel has been laid, no hull exists, the registry entry is a proposal.
- **It is not a rescue agenda.** The ark has rescue *capacity* and a legal duty to assist; it is not built as a political rescue fleet.
- **It is not a creationist ark.** It carries a name from the corpus — the ship that brings people home — not a literal reading of the flood.
- **It is not a token or an investment.** The programme has no token, no yield and no “tokenised deck”.

## Open questions

- A purpose-built schooner (~€70–100M, 4–6 years) vs. a research-hull refit (~€30–55M, ~3.5 years)?
- Enrol the programme as a vision node now, or only after the feasibility study?
- Ice-margin resilience — necessary, or is the northern edge of the whale band enough?
- A colony means families and children: school, birth suite and a safety standard aboard?
- Relation to the fleet — the Marys' mother ship, or a sister line?
- Security doctrine of a neutral sanctuary — where does help end and defence begin?
