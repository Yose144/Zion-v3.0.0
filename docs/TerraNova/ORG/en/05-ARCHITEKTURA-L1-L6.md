# Part V — Architecture L1 to L6

Every great vision eventually meets the same question: what exactly is this world made of? Terra Nova cannot answer with metaphor alone — it must have architecture. This chapter is not a detour away from community and care; it is their stress test. Only here does it become clear whether what earlier chapters articulated as ethics and a way of life can also survive the pressure of reality, operations, economics and a long technical horizon.

## L1 — the foundation stone

L1 is where the vision first becomes unsparing. As of April 2026, the canonical reality layer is: active runtime concentrated on the Prague primary host; the historical Prague / USA / Singapore rehearsal remaining as audit evidence; the on-chain block-subsidy split confirmed as **89 / 5 / 5 / 1**; bridge running via Prague relay; and public launch not yet occurred, still gated by final conditions. The chapter is explicit about this because Terra Nova must not confuse readers about what is already live and what is still under construction. The split 89/5/5/1 is then read not merely as tokenomics but as philosophy encoded in the rules of the network: 89% freedom for those who carry the network computationally; 5% care for the humanitarian stream; 5% horizon for Issobella; 1% operational discipline for the infrastructure that must keep running.

## L2 through L6 — layers of a hierarchy of care

L2 (bridge, liquidity, governance, treasury) is described not as a completed triumph but as a layer with real technical foundations still facing the test of public maturity. L3 — Neural Conscious Layer, AI Native, WARP — is the most sensitive area: it must be written honestly as an architectural direction and migration horizon, not as a fully realised production core. L4 / OASIS is a question of whether the culture of the future can create a space where entertainment is not an addiction factory but a form of maturation, imagination and cooperation — a design direction for 2026, not a finished product. L5 is where blockchain, DAO and AI must prove they mean something for soil, water, energy, school, health, supply and humanitarian work — the hardest layer, where philosophy must become institution without rigidifying. L6 / Issobella must not be written as a triumphalist prize at the end of the story but as an ethical test: reaching the stars will be meaningful only if at least basic civilisational maturity on Earth has been achieved first.

The best way to read these layers, the chapter concludes, is not as game levels or a corporate roadmap, but as a hierarchy of care — each layer serving the layer below it, and all of them ultimately serving life.
