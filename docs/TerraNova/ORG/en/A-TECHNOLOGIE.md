# Appendix A — Technology, Compute and the Production Horizon

This appendix exists for one reason: the technological material is important to Terra Nova, but must not disrupt the rhythm of the main narrative. It gathers the compute pyramid from edge devices to regional and orbital infrastructure, Nvidia and associated hardware horizons, DGX Spark, DGX Station, Vera Rubin and other key reference points, the technical implications for AI Native architecture, and the boundary between available hardware, community capacity and speculative horizon.

The appendix establishes a foundational principle: technological fascination must not overwrite the book's core axis. Hardware matters — but without the right civilisational intention it is still only a faster machine. The text therefore works with three explicit levels of claim: **available now** (hardware that can actually be acquired, operated or realistically planned); **architecturally important** (elements that determine the direction of design even when a specific product line eventually disappears); and **horizon** (layers that today have the form of development, simulation or strategic intent). This protects the text from two failures simultaneously — technofetishism and vague vision without grounding.

## Why compute is essential to Terra Nova

Terra Nova cannot speak of AI, coordination, medical assistance, education or community autonomy without honestly asking the question of computational infrastructure. The fundamental problem of today is not merely that AI is powerful — it is that its power tends to be concentrated in very few centres and tied to a business model that turns the human being into a data source. Compute in Terra Nova is therefore important not as a status symbol but as a condition of sovereignty. Without local and regional compute, a community remains dependent on external APIs, external commercial priorities and external limits; and where dependence is total, genuine autonomy of care, education or coordination is impossible.

The compute pyramid is described in three tiers. The **edge layer** — small, energy-efficient, locally operable systems — keeps basic intelligence close to the body, sensors and daily operations: local inference without cloud, biofeedback data for the Medical Table, energy and security automation with low power consumption. The **community layer** — a community server or hub — brings together multiple local flows: mid-size language models, documentation, community knowledge, scheduling, translation, coordination of supplies and energy planning, supporting a first real degree of AI sovereignty. The **regional layer** — systems such as DGX Spark — enables more systematic fine-tuning, heavier inference, model portfolio management and support for multiple communities simultaneously, creating a bridge between local use and research capacity without requiring immediate entry into hyperscaler infrastructure.
