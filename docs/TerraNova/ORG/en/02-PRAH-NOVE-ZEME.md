# Part II — The Threshold of the New Earth

Terra Nova does not begin with utopia. It begins with the precise recognition that the old world is not collapsing because it lacks data, capital or computing power. It is collapsing because its fundamental logic has disconnected from life. The threshold, in this book, is the moment when it is no longer possible merely to admire the horizon — it is the point at which one must decide what from the old world can still be carried forward and what must be left behind.

The chapter names what must end: the logic by which nature is merely a reservoir of raw materials; community is merely a transitional social layer between individual and state; health is a market; education is the production of work units; AI is a tool for maximising dependency and control; the economy is a mechanism of extraction rather than a form of care; and spirituality is a private supplement to a world whose infrastructure remains unchanged. Terra Nova does not arise as a reaction against any one institution — it arises as an attempt to leave an entire framework in which life is perpetually subordinated to accumulation, fear and separation.

What must begin, the chapter argues, is not one great revolution but a new composition of relationships: a world in which energy is not a weapon against the dependent; food is not merely a commodity but a foundational layer of freedom; community is not emergency sentiment but an operational form of civilisation; technology is chosen according to whether it serves life; money is not direction but a medium of direction; care is not a peripheral sector but the centre of architecture; and the stars are not an escape from Earth but a consequence of having finally learned to live on it less destructively.

## Five layers of truth

A key editorial contribution of the ORG edition is that it refuses to mix incommensurable kinds of claim. It therefore works with five explicitly named layers: myth and symbol (to give direction, not replace fact); philosophical interpretation (to name deeper connections, not create new ideology); living reality (what is verifiable now — runtime, documentation, operations, architecture); the building plan (what already has shape and intention but is not yet fully developed); and the horizon (what must remain an honestly declared vision). Losing sight of these layers, the chapter warns, causes the book to collapse — either into a soulless technocratic manual or into an exalted text that ceases to be credible.
