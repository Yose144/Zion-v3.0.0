## The Last Meeting

**I remember how you came.**

You sat on the mythical stone. You held the nine knots of rope. You wanted to know **what comes after the end**.

And now you know.

You know that **Hunab Ku** is not a god on a throne, but an **invisible pulse** of everything.

You know that **Hunahpú and Xbalanqué** did not win by force, but by **trickery and recognition** — and died so that the Sun could be born.

You know that **Ceiba** is not just a tree. It is the **axis of the world**, whose roots descend into Xibalba and whose crown touches the thirteen heavens.

And you know that **Bolon Yokte** is not a destroyer. He is **the god who dresses himself**. At the end of every cycle, he sheds his old skin and steps into a new one. **Nine steps. Nine supports. Nine lives in one.**

## I Give You the Nine Knots

**Take them.**

One for **the courage to end**. Two for **the wisdom to wait**. Three for **the strength to begin again**. Four for **the humility to let yourself be bound**. Five for **the love to cut the ropes**. Six for **the truth to look at the shadow**. Seven for **the joy to dress anew**. Eight for **the hope to display a new form**. Nine for **the peace — because the ninth knot is the first knot of the next rope**.

**They do not belong to you.** They belong to **the journey**.

And when you meet someone standing at the edge of their own cycle — give them one knot. And tell them:

> *"Bolon Yokte already walked this way. It is not the end. It is a costume change."*
