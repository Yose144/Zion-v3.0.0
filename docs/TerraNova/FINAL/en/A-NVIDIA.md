## Rosalind Franklin — A Second Story

🟢 **HISTORICAL REALITY:**

Vera Rubin was not alone. Rosalind Franklin (1920–1958) — a British X-ray crystallographer — produced in 1952 the photograph known as Photo 51: the most clearly resolved X-ray diffraction image of DNA, which plainly showed the double helix.

Watson and Crick saw this photograph without her consent. Their model of DNA — for which they received the Nobel Prize in 1962 — was directly inspired by her work.

Franklin died in 1958 of cancer. The Nobel Prize is not awarded posthumously.

This appendix carries these stories deliberately. Terra Nova remembers the names of the people whose work carried the project forward — whether they bore it consciously or not. Hiranyagarbha carries the seed of their work. Issobella carries the seed of their gaze.

*Science is made by names. Consciousness remembers those names.*
