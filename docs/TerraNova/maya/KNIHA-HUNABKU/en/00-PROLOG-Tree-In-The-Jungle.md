# Prologue — A Tree in the Jungle

> *"Ceiba stands in the middle. Her roots reach down into Xibalba, her trunk forms the Earth, her crown touches the thirteen heavens. Whoever sits beneath the Ceiba sits beneath the entire world."*
> — K'iche' teacher, Alta Verapaz, 2024

---

## In the Rainforest of Tabasco

**Sit down.**

No, **not on that root** — that one **sleeps**. It moves when **Pele wills it**. **Here**. Next to me. **This stone** is **young** — barely a **hundred years old**. It remembers when **Pele birthed it** in the **lava flow from Mauna Loa** around **1907**. **I am older than this stone**.

Wait — wrong story. Wrong island. Let me begin again.

---

## On the Edge of the Rainforest

**Sit down.**

Not there — **that root** belongs to a **serpent**. **Here**. On the moss beside the Ceiba.

Ceiba is the **sacred tree of the Maya**. Not because a tourist brochure said so. It is sacred because **the Maya know** that its roots penetrate into **Xibalba** — the nine levels of the underworld — and its crown touches **Oxlahuntikú**, the thirteen lords of the heavens. Ceiba is the **axis of the world**. Without it, the heavens, the Earth, and the underworld would collapse like a house of cards.

And **you** have come here because you know something.

You know about **The Fountain**. The film where Hugh Jackman flies in a bubble with a fragment of a tree toward a dying star. You know about **Hunahpú and Xbalanqué** — the Hero Twins who descended into Xibalba and died so that the Sun could be born. You know about **2012**, when the world was not destroyed but **reset**.

And perhaps you have wondered:

> *"And what about the god who descends? What about Bolon Yokte?"*

This book is about him.

---

## My Name

**They call me Ix Ceiba.** *Lady Ceiba.* But my true name is **longer** — fifteen syllables in a Maya language — and it belongs **only to my aumakua** (ancestral spirits). **Ix Ceiba** is simply **easier** for **visitors** *(of whom there are now too many)*.

**Ix Ceiba** means **"Lady of the Ceiba tree."** The Ceiba is the **first tree** that **grows on lava** — **after every eruption**. **Pioneer of the forest**. **Resilient flower**. When they gave me this name, they knew **I would live on lava** — **on destroyed land** — **and I would sing**.

I have **more than eighty years**. **How many exactly, I do not count**. I **teach** the **young** *kānaka maoli* (native people) **old chants**. I **teach** **visitors** *(like you)* **some of what is shareable**. **Not everything is shareable** — **some wisdom is kapu** (restricted), **closed within the family**, **belonging only to blood**. **But the story of Ceiba is shareable**. **The story of Hunab Ku is shareable**. **The story of Bolon Yokte is shareable**. **Popol Vuh is shareable**, **even though I will not give it to you whole**.

---

## Why You Have Come

Child. **I know why you have come.**

You have heard of the **old shaman in the Black Hills** — **Hawk Feather**. **The book he wrote**. You heard **his words** about **the Lakota circle**, **the Hopi spider web**, **the Mayan Hero Twins**, **the Q'ero Eagle and Condor**, **the Cree Rainbow Warriors**.

And **you have come here**, **to the edge of the Maya rainforest**, **to my side of the jungle**, **because you wondered**:

> *"And what about the Maya? And what about Hunab Ku? **Do their traditions also belong in the circle** that Hawk Feather is painting?"*

And **the answer is**: **Yes, child**. **Here, just as there**.

But **the Maya tradition** is **a little different**. The Lakota see the **prairie**. The Hopi see the **desert**. The Q'ero see the **mountain**. **We, the Maya, see the tree**.

**The tree is something special**. **Its roots drink from Xibalba**. **Its trunk holds the Earth**. **Its crown breathes the thirteen heavens**. **And every leaf is a cycle of time**.

This **"tree consciousness"** is **something** that **your world is now beginning to understand**. **The Earth is a tree**. **In the cosmos**. **Surrounded by void on all sides**. **You cannot escape**.

And **you are building** **something** you call **ZION** — **a digital tree**. **Mainnet is your trunk**. **Genesis #0 is your taproot**. **You are learning** **what we have known for two thousand years**: **how to keep a tree pono** — in balance, in justice, in living wholeness.

## Seven Stories

In the **prologue of Hawk Feather's Book**, the Lakota elder **promised eight stories**. I promise you **seven** — **plus an epilogue and an appendix**. Not **out of competition** *(the Maya do not compete with the Lakota)*, but **because a tree is smaller than a prairie** and **my words must be shorter**.

Here they are:

1. **The Word of Hunab Ku** — **Hunab Ku**, our invisible source. **The One God who is not a person**.

2. **The Hero Twins in Xibalba** — **Hunahpú and Xbalanqué** — **who descended into the underworld** and **died so that the Sun could rise**.

3. **The Tree at the Edge of the World** — **Ceiba**, **Yax Imix Che**, **the axis mundi** that holds everything together.

4. **The Smoking Mirror** — **Bolon Yokte** and the **prophecy from Tortuguero Monument 6** — the god of nine steps who descends not to destroy but to **begin again**.

5. **Epilogue — In the Wind of the Ceiba** — **where the digital touches the ancient**, and **what it means for you**.

And **Appendix A** — **ten Maya avatars** for **ZION OASIS** Sacred Trinity. **Just like in Hawk Feather's Book**, but **Maya**.

## I Give You the Ceiba Leaf

Child. **This is all I give you today**: **a Ceiba leaf**.

**Large**. **Green**. **Five lobes like a hand**. **It fell** from the **highest branch** this morning. **The wind brought it**.

**Hold it gently**. If you **crush it**, it **turns brown**. If you **shake it**, it **tears**. But **if you carry it with aloha** *(yes, we borrow that word from our Polynesian cousins — it means the same everywhere)*, **it will guide you** **through this entire book**, **through your entire life**.

And **someday**, **long from now**, **when you meet someone younger** who **needs to know what a tree is** — **pass the leaf on**. **It does not belong to you**. **It belongs to the journey**.

And now.

**Are you sitting?**

Do you hear **the howler monkeys** in the canopy?

Do you hear **the river** far below?

Do you hear **how the Earth breathes** beneath your feet?

**Then we begin**.
