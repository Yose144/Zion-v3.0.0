## Why we are so lonely — even though we are so connected

Consider one statistic.

In 2023, the U.S. government issued a report designating loneliness a public health epidemic. Surgeon General Vivek Murthy wrote in it that loneliness is medically comparable to smoking **fifteen cigarettes a day**. It raises the risk of heart disease by 29%, stroke by 32%, dementia by 50%.

Meanwhile, thanks to the internet and social networks, we are "connected" more than at any other point in history. Everyone has hundreds of "friends" on Facebook. Everyone can instantly message anyone in the world.

And yet. An epidemic of loneliness.

How is this possible?

The answer is simple, and it hurts: **digital connection is not the same as real community.**

Community is physical. It is a place where you share air with people who matter to you. Where someone knows when you are sick. Where children grow up and adults grow old and everyone has a role that makes sense.

Modern civilization has methodically dismantled this community over the past hundred years. The Industrial Revolution moved people from villages to cities for work. The individualistic ideology of the 20th century elevated personal success above collective well-being. The hypermobility of the labor market means people change their place of residence every few years — and never have time to take root.

Terra Nova says: enough.

Not as a political statement. As a practical project.

## The science of community — Dunbar's number

In 1992, British anthropologist Robin Dunbar made an observation that has since been called Dunbar's number.

Dunbar studied the neocortex of various primates and correlated its size with the average social group size of each species. For humans he estimated the maximum number of relationships that we can maintain with genuine social investment: **approximately 150 people.**

Dunbar's number of 150 has proven surprisingly consistent:
- The average village in pre-industrial Europe: 100–200 people
- The basic military unit (a company) in most armies of the world: 150–200 soldiers
- The average Hutterite farm (an Anabaptist community) — when it exceeds 150, it **automatically splits**
- The average number of genuine social contacts on networks: close to 150

Why? Because above 150 people, the brain can no longer maintain an individual social map. You begin to need formal institutions — rules, hierarchies, bureaucracy — because personal trust is no longer sufficient.

**A Terra Nova community is designed in accordance with Dunbar's number.** The goal is not a mega-community of thousands of people. The goal is a network of smaller communities — each in the range of 50–500 people — connected by ZION blockchain infrastructure.

## How a community is built — three waves

A community does not arise overnight. It is a living organism — it grows gradually, adapts, and learns from its mistakes.

### 🟢 REALITY 2026 — First wave: founders (12–30 people, years 1–2)

This is the hardest phase. And the most important.

Twelve to thirty people decide to begin. They buy or lease land — collectively, through a DAO structure. They build the basic infrastructure: water, energy, internet, shelter. And they begin to learn to live together.

Why is it the hardest? Because these people come from a world of individualism. Suddenly they must make joint decisions about things that in a Prague apartment each person handled alone.

What will we cook for dinner? Who will clean the common areas? What happens when someone stops fulfilling their commitments?

These questions sound trivial. But they are precisely these "trivial" conflicts that break apart communities that had not prepared a way to resolve them.

That is why Terra Nova works with **sociocracy** from the very first wave.

### 📋 ROADMAP — Second wave: growth (30–100 people, years 2–3)

New members accepted by DAO vote. Specialization: some are farmers, others builders, others healers, others technologists. The community stops being a group of friends and becomes a functioning miniature economy.

In this phase, the Medical Table and a local ZION node are launched. Mining supports the financing of infrastructure.

### 🌟 HORIZON — Third wave: maturity (100–500 people, years 3–5)

Full energy self-sufficiency. 80% food self-sufficiency. A school of its own. A health space. A cultural center.

And connection to other Terra Nova communities — exchange of seeds, sharing of experience, mutual aid in crises. The rhizome begins to function.

## Sociocracy — democracy that works

The word "democracy" comes from the Greek *demos* (people) and *kratos* (to rule). The people rule.

But how, exactly? Who are the people? How do they rule?

**Sociocracy** is a method of community governance designed for small groups, where direct participation of each member is possible and desirable.

**Core principles:**

**Circles instead of hierarchy.** The community is not composed of a boss and subordinates, but of overlapping circles — groups of people who jointly govern a specific area (garden, energy, health, finances). Each circle has autonomy in its area.

**Consent instead of consensus.** This is the key difference. Consensus = everyone actively agrees. Consent = no one has a *fundamental objection*. "I can live with this" is enough. This dramatically accelerates decision-making.

**Double linking.** Each circle elects a representative to the parent circle — and the parent circle sends its representative down to the circle. Information flows both ways.

**ZION DAO and sociocracy:**

ZION DAO smart contracts translate sociocracy into digital form. Every vote is recorded on the blockchain — transparently, immutably, publicly. Every expenditure from the treasury is auditable.

*A practical example:* The community wants to expand the solar system. A member proposes a project with a budget of 50,000 ZION. The proposal goes to the DAO. 72 hours of open discussion. Then a vote: who has a fundamental objection? If no one — the proposal has passed. The funds are automatically released. Everything is recorded. No secretary, no notary, no official.

## Permaculture — nature as teacher

In 1978, Australian botanist Bill Mollison and his student David Holmgren published the book *Permaculture: A Designers' Manual*. The result of years of observing natural systems — how a forest functions without human intervention — was translated into the design of human settlements and gardens.

**The core insight:** Nature is the most efficient farmer that has ever existed. A forest needs no fertilizer, no pesticides, no irrigation. It functions sustainably and productively for thousands of years, with enormous diversity.

Mollison called it: *"The problem is the solution."* A problem becomes a solution if you place it correctly.

**Three ethics of permaculture:**
1. **Care for the Earth** — treat the soil, water, and living beings as valuable in themselves
2. **Care for people** — design systems that support human flourishing
3. **Fair share** — share surpluses, do not exceed a fair share of resources

If these three ethics seem familiar — it is because they are precisely the three values encoded into the ZION protocol.

## Rhizome — how communities form a network

Rhizome is a biological term. It describes the way bamboo, grass, and most fungi grow — not from one center, but as an underground network with no center. Every point is connected to neighboring ones, but none is "primary."

**Terra Nova Rhizome** = a global network of self-sufficient communities connected by:

- **ZION blockchain** — the economic backbone. Shared treasury, DAO governance, humanitarian fund
- **Mesh internet (LoRa, Meshtastic)** — communication without dependence on centralized ISPs. LoRa transmits data for tens of kilometers with minimal energy consumption
- **Seed library exchange** — seed swapping. Biological diversity as living heritage
- **Medical Table protocols** — anonymized data on the effectiveness of therapeutic protocols shared across communities
- **Knowledge commons** — a shared database: how to build a passive solar house, how to ferment, how to repair a wind turbine

Each community is autonomous. It needs no permission from any other. But it is stronger as part of the network.

*144,000 Guardians — not as an army. As mycelium.*
