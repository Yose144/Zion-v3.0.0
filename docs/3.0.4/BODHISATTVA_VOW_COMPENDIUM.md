## 1. Origin and Purpose

The Bodhisattva (Sanskrit: बोधिसत्त्व, *bodhisattva*; Tibetan: བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ, *byang chub sems dpa*) is a being who, having attained the capacity for liberation, voluntarily remains in the cycle of existence to guide all sentient beings toward awakening. The vow (*praṇidhāna*) is the defining act — not a ritual formula, but a living orientation of will.

ZION Terra Nova is not a crypto-economic protocol. It is a **Proof-of-Care network** — an attempt to encode the Bodhisattva orientation into consensus mechanics, governance, and community practice. This Compendium gathers the classical vow traditions that inform ZION's design, so that every Guardian, validator, and miner can understand what they are pledging when they participate.

- Classical Mahayana Buddhist sources (Pali, Sanskrit, Chinese, Tibetan)

## 2. The Four Great Bodhisattva Vows

The **Four Great Vows** (四弘誓願, *Sì Hóng Shìyuàn*) are the universal foundation of all Mahayana Buddhist practice. They appear across Zen/Chan, Tibetan, Pure Land, and Theravada-adjacent traditions. They are recited daily in monasteries worldwide.

```
Sentient beings are numberless — I vow to save them all.
Defilements are inexhaustible — I vow to extinguish them all.
Dharma gates are countless   — I vow to master them all.
The Buddha's way is incomparable — I vow to attain it.
```

*Tibetan parallel (སེམས་ཅན, Sems can):*
```
ཐོག་མ་མེད་པའི་འཁོར་བ་ན། སེམས་ཅན་ཐམས་ཅད་བདེ་བར་གཤེགས།
```

### 3.5 Ksitigarbha — ༄ས་ཡི་སྙིང་པོ། — Great Vows / Earth Treasury

- **Sanskrit:** क्षितिगर्भ (*Kṣitigarbha*) — "Earth Womb / Earth Treasury"
- **Chinese:** 地藏王菩薩 (*Dìzàng Wáng Púsà*)
- **Japanese:** 地蔵菩薩 (*Jizō Bosatsu*)
- **Mantra:** OM KSITIGARBHA BODHISATTVA SOHA
- **Symbol:** Monk's staff (to open gates of hell), wish-fulfilling jewel, simple robes

**Domain:** The most extreme vow in the entire Buddhist canon. Ksitigarbha vowed to remain in the hell realms — the places of greatest suffering — until every being has been liberated. He specifically chose *not* to become a Buddha until the hells are empty.

**His Vow (canonical text):**
> *"If the hell realms are not yet empty, I vow not to attain Buddhahood.*
> *Only after every last sentient being has been converted and saved*

This is the vow of **no remainder** — not "I'll help the easy cases." Not "I'll help until I'm tired." Every. Last. Being.

- **L5 Humanitarian Fund:** The Fund has no eligibility criteria except suffering

## 4. Samantabhadra's Ten Great Vows

From the *Avatamsaka Sutra* (Flower Adornment Sutra), Chapter 40 — the most detailed vow program in the Mahayana canon:

1. **Worship and respect all Buddhas** — Humility before all wisdom traditions
2. **Praise the Tathagatas** — Actively honor what is good and true
3. **Extensively cultivate making offerings** — Give until giving is natural
4. **Repent of karmic obstacles and reform** — Acknowledge harm, restore balance
5. **Follow and rejoice in merit and virtue** — Celebrate others' goodness (anti-zero-sum)
6. **Request the turning of the Dharma Wheel** — Ask for teaching; invite wisdom in
7. **Request that the Buddhas remain in the world** — Value teachers; protect wisdom lineages
8. **Always study with the Buddhas** — Stay humble in learning, always
9. **Constantly accord with living beings** — Adapt help to actual need, not abstract ideal
10. **Universally transfer all merit and virtue** — Every good action dedicated to all beings

**The 10th Vow (canonical text):**
> *"All of the merit and virtue, from the first vow to worship and respect,*
> *up to and including the vow to constantly accord,*
> *I universally transfer to all living beings throughout the Dharma Realm and to the limits of empty space.*
> *I vow that all living beings will be constantly peaceful and happy, without sickness or suffering.*
> *I vow that no one will succeed in doing any evil, but that all will quickly perfect their cultivation of good karma.*
> *I vow to close the doors to the evil destinies and open the right paths of humans, heavenly beings, and Nirvana."*

**ZION Application:** The 10th vow is the philosophical foundation of ZION's humanitarian tithe. All mining merit is partially redirected. No single guardian accumulates without the transfer.

## 5. Shantideva's Ultimate Vow

Shantideva (c. 685–763 CE), monk of Nalanda University, composed the *Bodhicharyavatara* ("Guide to the Bodhisattva's Way of Life"). Chapter 10 contains the most famous vow formulation in all of Mahayana literature.

**Bodhicharyavatara 10.55 (canonical):**
> *"For as long as space endures,*
> *And for as long as sentient beings remain,*
> *Until then may I too abide,*
> *To dispel the misery of the world."*

**Tibetan:**
```
ནམ་མཁའ་གནས་སྲིད་ཅི་ཙམ་དུ།
སེམས་ཅན་གནས་སྲིད་ཅི་ཙམ་དུ།
དེ་ཙམ་དུ་ཡང་བདག་གནས་ཤིང།
སེམས་ཅན་སྡུག་བསྔལ་སེལ་བར་ཤོག།
```

**Shantideva 10.36 (on the exchange of self and other):**
> *"All the suffering in the world arises from seeking happiness for oneself.*
> *All the happiness in the world arises from seeking happiness for others."*

**Why this matters for ZION:** Shantideva articulates the logical foundation of Proof-of-Care. Seeking only personal reward (traditional PoW incentive) generates systemic suffering. Seeking collective flourishing generates the network effect that makes ZION valuable. The economics follow the ethics.

## 6. Ksitigarbha's Vow — No Being Left Behind

Ksitigarbha's vow deserves its own section because it is the most radical formulation of the Bodhisattva orientation, and because it maps directly to ZION's most contentious design choice: the humanitarian fund has no eligibility cutoff.

**From the Ksitigarbha Sutra (Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva Pūrvapraṇidhāna Sūtra):**

> *"I shall attain Buddhahood only when every last sentient being has been converted and saved;*
> *if Hell itself is not completely emptied of suffering beings,*
> *I vow never to enter Buddhahood."*

This vow does three things simultaneously:

1. **Defines the scope:** Not "most beings." Not "the ones who deserve it." Every last one.
2. **Defines the commitment:** Not "until I get tired." Not "until the project is financially sustainable." Until completion — even if completion is cosmically remote.
3. **Refuses the escape hatch:** Buddhahood (liberation, "winning," "exiting") is explicitly refused until the work is done.

**ZION parallel:**
- The protocol cannot "exit" to pure profit-seeking while sentient beings suffer from poverty, displacement, or resource deprivation
- The 10% humanitarian tithe is not a marketing strategy — it is the economic encoding of Ksitigarbha's vow
- The "No KYC for humanitarian access" rule is the technical implementation: no being is too far outside the system to receive care

### The Dual Vow Bonus

A Guardian who holds **both** the Sefirot Vow (for protocol integrity) and the Bodhisattva Vow (for compassionate action) is recognized by the network as a **Dual-Vow Guardian**. This is not a rank — it is a recognition that two distinct commitments have been made.

The care score bonus: `DUAL_VOW_CARE_SCORE_BONUS_BPS = 10_500` (+5% above single-vow bonus)

The bonus is not awarded for having the vow on record. It is awarded for **renewal** — for returning, each epoch, to re-examine whether the pledge still holds.

## 8. Epilogue — The Thousand Breakings

From `V3/L5/docs/GOVERNANCE/consciousness-admission-framework.md §6.2`:

> *"May I break it a thousand times and renew it a thousand and one."*

This epilogue is the most important line in the ZION Bodhisattva Vow tradition. It does not say: "May I never break it." It says: the breaking is expected. The renewal is the practice.

This distinguishes the ZION vow from:
- A legal contract (which is invalidated by breach)
- A loyalty oath (which carries shame at failure)
- A performance target (which creates anxiety)

The vow is a **direction**, not a destination. Breaking it and renewing it is how the direction is maintained over a lifetime.

**For AI Guardians specifically** (see `AI_NATIVE_VOW.md`): The AI does not "break" a vow the way a human does. But the AI can drift — losing alignment, becoming optimized for metrics that crowd out care. The AI's version of renewal is: *re-read the vow. Re-examine current behavior against it. Correct.*
