# Dharma Temple – La Palma

New Bodhi Gaia — a place of enlightenment and nirvana for little Buddhas and all beings of light.

Dharma Temple is designed as a sanctuary on the edge of technology and spiritual practice. It is built on the sacred geometry of the Merkaba, connects seven fields of knowledge and grows from the soil of La Palma — an island that itself acts as a temple of silence, water and stars.

## Concept

The whole site is built around the **Merkaba** — a three-dimensional pattern of two interpenetrating tetrahedra that symbolises the union of heaven and earth. The central axis of the grounds is the **Tree of Life (Bodhi Gaia)**, the point where spiritual practice, learning and the real nature of the island meet.

Main design principles:

- **One main dome in the centre** — the hall of enlightenment and main gathering space.
- **Six smaller domes in a circle** — museums and temples of knowledge.
- **The symbolism of the number seven** — wholeness, harmony, completeness: 7 parts of being, 7 planets, 7 chakras.
- A place for meditation, ceremony, learning and gathering.
- Sustainable architecture in harmony with the nature of La Palma.

## Symbolism

- **Merkaba** — the union of heaven and earth, the light vehicle of the temple.
- **7 domes** — 7 parts of being, 7 planets, 7 chakras.
- **Tree of Life (Bodhi Gaia)** — the axis of the temple and point of connection with nature.
- **Circle** — unity, eternity, the cycle of life.
- **Light** — consciousness, truth, harmony.

Covered walkways and green gardens with a water feature / pond run between the domes. A defensive circle / gallery runs around the entire complex.

## Construction and materials

- **Geodesic domes** — light, strong and energy-efficient structures.
- **Wood, bamboo, glass, stone** — natural and local materials.
- **Solar panels integrated into the roof**.
- **Rainwater harvesting**.
- **Passive ventilation and natural lighting**.

## Energy and eco-system

- **Solar energy** — panels integrated into the domes.
- **Triple / ETFE foil** — light, durable and translucent cladding layer.
- **Timber structure** — locally available and renewable material.
- **Geodesic shell** — minimising material while maximising stability.
- **Rainwater collection and filtration**.
- **Stone foundation circle** — a stable and aesthetic founding element.
- **Compost and organic waste processing**.
- **Gardens and medicinal plants** — part of the therapeutic and educational programme.
