## The Story

When the builders reached the Grand Unification, they stood on the shore of the New Earth and watched the six layers shine as a single field. And someone — a pilgrim who had walked the whole way since the first ray — asked the question the glowing map did not answer:

*"And what of the water? The springs you opened flow into the earth — but who carries them across the sea?"*

For the gardens that Free World had planted did not stand side by side. The Garden of Genesis lay in one land, Dharma Temple in another, Te Pīko Ora in the middle of the planet's greatest ocean, Golden Republic in the heart of a continent and Bodhi Lanka on an island beyond the horizon. Seven fixed points — and between them only wind-blown water.

The builders looked at the map and saw what they had always seen, only differently: **points form a network only when something lives between them.** The seventh point was a bridge. The eighth was not a point at all — it was a **line**.

And so the fleet **Tres Marías** was born — three ships for three oceans, each christened after one of the three apparitions the pilgrim had received on the way.

**The first hull — *Santa María la Mayor* — was given the Atlantic.** A classic sailing vessel in the line of the Camino seafaring tradition, home port Pontevedra in Galicia, where the basilica of Mary was built by the people of the sea. She wears the robes of the red queen — and her word: *"Your journey is only beginning."*

**The second hull — *Nossa Senhora de Fátima* — was given the Pacific.** A solar catamaran in the line of the *wa'a kaulua*, the Polynesian double canoe that reads the ocean by waves and stars. Her home is Te Pīko Ora on Raiatea. She wears white wedding garments — the wedding of heaven and earth — and on her prow a vieira, the pilgrim's shell.

**The third hull — *María de las Nieves* — awaits the Indian Ocean.** The ship of unfinished miracles, of the patroness of La Palma; the bearer of the child who **stands on his own** — the Little Prince, patron of the whole fleet, who sails on all three hulls in the robes of his Mary. And that ship is not yet — the third hull is a horizon, and a horizon deserves honesty.

Where the oceans cannot meet by water, the ships will span them overland — across the isthmus of Nueva América, where the Atlantic and Pacific crews reach out to one another at the lantern. Where the world is sewn together by water, they meet at Boa Esperança and in the waters of Ekam — at the place that gave the chain its name.

And the sails? **The sails are light.** Solar cells laminated into them turn wind and sun into current; beneath the hull free-spinning turbines charge the batteries, sprouting trays and a greenhouse grow on deck, water is born from the sea through solar pumps — and in the middle of the ocean a full node runs, validating the chain over satellite. Fifty people, their own energy, their own water, their own food — a proof of self-sufficiency: *if it works at sea, it works anywhere.*

At Finisterre pilgrims once burned their clothes, because the road ended at the sea and there was nowhere further to go. Tres Marías are precisely that extra step: a path that does not end at the sea — it continues on it.

## What it means

**Tres Marías del Camino is the eighth point of the Terra Nova network — a point that is not a point but a line: a moving node that physically connects all the others.**

The seven fixed nodes (Garden of Genesis, Dharma Temple / Nová Bodhi Gaia, Te Pīko Ora, Golden Republic Bohemia, Bodhi Lanka, LUMI, Uluru) become a network only through circulation — and the fleet is its artery:

1. **L5 circulation:** the seed bank, the cultural archive, people and grants flow between the nodes across the sea — the L5 protocols (seed exchange, knowledge commons, resonant calendar) gain maritime transport. The ship becomes the trade artery of the network, not a tourist attraction.
2. **A floating embassy:** the ship sails into harbours as the embassy of the whole network — open days, a Medical Table on deck, an exhibition of all seven nodes along a single visitor route.
3. **The solar sail as protocol:** sail = solar cell. The intention is the same as with the 5% stream from each block — infrastructure that feeds itself on light, not on extraction.
4. **An initiation route for Guardians:** the form of the route is a circumnavigation of the world — whoever is to hold the network's witness first passes through the water that binds the witness together.

The status according to the corpus, honestly: hulls I and II are **UNDER CONSTRUCTION** — concept, route, five phases and the reserve are designed, no keel is laid. Hull III is **HORIZON** — a ship that is not yet. The apparitions on which the names stand are **MYTH** — an authorial layer on living traditions (Fátima 1917, the Bajada on La Palma, the basilica of the seafaring people in Pontevedra).
